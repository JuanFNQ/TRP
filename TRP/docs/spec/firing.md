# Firing and reloading

How the weapon decides to fire, how fast, and how a reload runs. Written from a reading of the game (0.16.9.5, SPT 4.1.6 assembly) and from the weapon's own animation data. Class and field names are the game's.

## How the game structures it

The firearm controller runs one operation at a time: idling, a single shot, automatic fire, a reload, and others. An operation starts states on the weapon's animator and waits for marks that are placed in those states. Recoil is separate: the operation calls `Shot`, which ends in the weapon animation's `Shoot(strength)` (see the weapon-handling spec).

## Animator states and marks

The weapon's animator controller (`WeaponPrefab._originalAnimatorController`) has seven layers. The `Hands` layer holds the weapon's states (58 for the AKM). Each state names a clip, or a blend tree of clips with one per variant, and a speed. A state lasts `clip length / speed`.

Marks are not Unity animation events. Each state carries an `AnimationEventsStateBehaviour` with the state's full name and an index into a table (`AnimatorControllerStaticData`) that ships beside the controller. An entry of the table is a list of marks: a function name, a time as a fraction of the state, an optional parameter, and optional conditions on animator parameters.

Marks fire when the state's normalised time passes them. When a state is entered part-way, every mark before the entry point fires at once.

States and marks the logic below uses, with the AKM's values:

| State | Clip, speed, length | Marks (fraction of the state) |
|---|---|---|
| `Hands.FIRE` | `akm_fire`, 10, 0.097 s | `FiringBullet` 0, `ShellEject` 0.310, `DelAmmoChamber` 0.665, `AddAmmoInChamber` 0.776, `FireEnd` 0.873 |
| `Hands.DRY FIRE` | `akm_fire_dry`, 0.5, 0.27 s | `Sound Misfire` 0.049 |
| `Hands.RELOAD START` | `akm_hands_out_half_1`, 0.75, 0.44 s | |
| `Hands.RELOAD OUT` | one of 6, 1 | `MagOut` 0.462, `MagHide` 0.850 |
| `Hands.RELOAD IN` | one of 6, 1 | `MagShow` 0.092, `MagIn` 0.707 |
| `Hands.RELOAD END` | `akm_hands_in_half_2`, 0.73, 0.46 s | |
| `Hands.RELOAD CHARGE END` | one of 3, 1 | `Arm` 0.476, `AddAmmoInChamber` 0.584 |
| `Hands.FIREMODE` | one of 2, 1 | `Sound Selector` 0.431 |
| `Hands.IDLE` | | `IdleStart` 0 |

`Sound` marks carry the name of the sound to play. Which clip of a blend tree plays is chosen by an animator parameter; for the magazine states it is the magazine's `magAnimationIndex`, which the animator's own blend tree goes by.

`probe states <weapon template>` prints every state with its clips and marks.

## Pressing the trigger

From idle, a press with a round in the chamber starts an operation by the selected fire mode: single, doublet and semi-automatic start a single shot; anything else starts automatic fire. A press on an empty chamber plays the dry-fire state and nothing else. The trigger is reported on change only; holding it through another operation does not fire when that operation ends.

**Not in a sprint or a crawl** (read on 2026-10-09). The settings object has a list of movement states in which the hands take no input (`UnsuitableStates`): sprinting (5), crawling (3), breaching a door (9), unlocking (14), working a door (16) and walking up to one (17). The idling operation drops a press of the trigger made in one of them: nothing fires, nothing clicks, and since the trigger is only reported on change, holding the button until the state ends does not fire either. The list is asked of the movement state, not of the sprint's flag, so in the fraction of a second between the sprint key and the sprinting state a fresh press still fires (the trigger held at the key is let go by the sprint's flag: "Aiming, and what takes it away"). Only the idling operation asks: automatic fire begun lying still carries on into a crawl for as long as the trigger is held, and nothing but a release ends it. The second shot a single shot remembers is a press like any other when the first is over, and is dropped in such a state. **Recorded** (eighth recording, 52). The trigger is pressed in a sprint, then pressed in the sprint and held 0.7 s past its end, then pressed standing still; and the same three in a crawl and lying still. Two shots are fired in all, by the two presses made at rest (at 4.50 s and 10.50 s); neither press in a state on the list fires, and neither does holding one past the state's end. The simulation's rounds are the game's on every one of the 1,612 frames. Still from the code only: automatic fire carried on into a crawl, and the remembered second shot.

## A single shot

1. The shot's rounds are worked out at once: the chambered round will fire, and the next one comes from the magazine if it has any.
2. A cooldown of `60 / SingleFireRate` seconds starts (AKM 450 a minute, 0.133 s).
3. The fire state is started at 0.2 of its length. `FiringBullet` sits before that, so it reaches the operation on the animator's first update of the state, which is the next frame's: then the shot itself is made (the bullet, the sound and the recoil's kick) and the trigger is released. So a single shot is two frames' work: the round count drops on the frame of the press, and the shot comes a frame after. (Corrected 2026-10-09; it stood here as "fires at once", which is right for the rounds and was wrong for the recoil. See "The recoil's kick, and which physics step takes it" below.)
4. `ShellEject`, `AddAmmoInChamber` and `FireEnd` follow from the animation.
5. When the weapon is back in its idle state and the cooldown has run out, the operation ends.

**The operations run on the player's clock** (found 2026-10-16 in a recording of the Five-seveN, built 0.36.2). The arms are updated with the player's whole-millisecond frame time (`Player.UpdateTick` hands its `DeltaTime` to `ArmsUpdate`, which updates the hands' controller and so its operation), while the weapon's animator runs on the engine's. So the cooldown counts down 8 or 9 ms a frame where the frames are 8.33: the Five-seveN's 0.133 s ran out after 16 frames or 17 as their milliseconds came to 133 or 134, and a shot queued in it came on that frame, recorded. The automatic fire's cycle and a look's wait go by it too: with it, the AKM's automatic fire in the scripted recordings (`18-fire-automatic` of the fifth, eleventh and twelfth, `19-fire-automatic-aimed` of the thirteenth) has the game's round count on every frame, where one shot of each had come a frame off (regression `after73`: the recoil's camera gap 0.49 to 0.32 degrees, the weapon's place 14.6 to 9.7 mm, in the fifth). `Firearm::step_on_clock` takes both times; the replay, the app and `byhand` give it the player's.

A press during the operation is remembered if the weapon allows it (`CanQueueSecondShot`) and is fired as a press and release the moment the operation ends. So single shots cannot be fired faster than `SingleFireRate`, however fast the trigger is pulled.

## Automatic fire

`T = 60 / bFirerate` seconds per shot (AKM 600 a minute, 0.1 s).

1. On start the weapon is marked as in automatic fire (recoil reads this), the fire state is started at 0.2, and the first shot is made at once.
2. A clock then runs in the operation itself, not in the animation. Each cycle of `T`: a shot at 0, the shell at 0.25, the next round into the chamber at 0.75, and the end-of-cycle check at 0.99. The clock starts just above zero so that the first cycle does not shoot twice. A frame longer than `T` runs every cycle it spans.
3. At the end-of-cycle check:
   - Burst mode: if fewer than `BurstShotsCount` shots were fired and a round was chambered, carry on; otherwise the trigger counts as released.
   - Trigger held and a round chambered: carry on. The next shot comes at the top of the next cycle.
   - Trigger held and no round: back to idle. Without a bolt catch the trigger is then pressed again, which dry-fires.
   - Trigger released: back to idle.

The trigger is only looked at there. A tap fires one round; letting go mid-cycle still finishes the cycle.

**The sound's two moments** (2026-10-16, 0.40.0; `sound.md`, "The shots"). The weapon's sound player hears of each shot as it is made (`MakeShot`, once for each chamber fired: an automatic shot at its cycle's top, a single shot at `FiringBullet` with the trigger still held, a cylinder's and the barrels' at theirs), and of every time the controller's trigger is set to let go (the setter of `IsTriggerPressed`, which raises `BreakLoop`): by the player mid-cycle, by the cycle's end on an empty chamber, by a single shot after its sound, by a planned reload or put-away. The simulation says both (`Event::FireSound { trigger, doublet }`, `Event::TriggerReleased`), the trigger's every change going through one setter; nothing else of the simulation changes (the regression `after78`, every report the same as `after77`'s byte for byte).

## Reloading with a magazine

`ReloadExternalMagOperation` sets animator parameters and the animator walks `RELOAD START`, `RELOAD OUT`, `RELOAD IN`, then one of two endings. The marks drive the logic:

- `MagOut`: the old magazine leaves the weapon. `MagHide`: it is put away or dropped.
- `MagShow`: the new magazine appears in the hand. `MagIn`: it is in the weapon and its rounds count.
- At `MagIn`, if a round is already chambered the operation hands over to idle through `RELOAD END`. If the chamber is empty the animator goes on to `RELOAD CHARGE END`, where `AddAmmoInChamber` chambers the first round of the new magazine.

**The reload is over for the logic before its animation is** (read on 2026-10-08, `ReloadExternalMagOperation`). The reload operation hands over to the idling one at `MagIn` when no round has to be chambered (one is in the chamber already, or the magazine going in is empty), and otherwise at `AddAmmoInChamber`. The animator then plays out the rest (`RELOAD IN` and the ending, 0.8 s for the AKM with a round chambered) with the weapon's logic idle. In that tail:

- the aim key works again, as at any other time, and the calls that lower the sights from outside are obeyed ("Aiming, and what takes it away");
- a press of the trigger is still refused. The reload raises a flag on the controller as it starts, and the idling operation drops any press while it is up. What clears it is `IdleStart`, a mark at the very beginning of `IDLE`, which therefore falls on the frame the animator starts blending into `IDLE`. A release is taken at any time;
- the "tactical reload" is switched off (the idling operation does that as it starts).

Two parameters of the **body's** animator follow the reload:

- `ProneReload`, a flag. It is raised when the reload operation begins and lowered when it ends, so it is up from the frame of the key to the mark above. Despite its name it is set in every movement state, standing too; the base class of the movement states does it and none overrides it. (Opening the inventory raises it as well; not built.)
- `ReloadFloat`, set to 1 when the reload begins and back to 0 by `IdleStart`. Other operations set it too (folding the stock, and a fire-mode switch on weapons that have a sprinting variant of it).

What the body does with them is in `body-animator.md`, "Reloading"; what the first does to the prone key, for the online raid's player only, is in `movement.md`, "Prone".

Built: `Firearm::reloading` is the first flag (hosts pass it on as `movement::Input::reloading`), and `set_aim` and `lower_aim` go by it. `Firearm::hands_busy` is the second (`movement::Input::hands_busy`): the body's animator answers it in a sprint.

Compared with the game (sixth recording, which writes both parameters every frame): the simulation's flag is the game's `ProneReload` on every frame of all five reload scenarios, standing and lying. It comes up on the frame of the key and drops on the frame of `MagIn` with a round chambered (frames 358, 539, 419 and 659 of scenarios 23, 29, 30 and 39) and on the frame of `AddAmmoInChamber` from an empty chamber (1182 of 24). The game's `ReloadFloat` drops on the frame its animator starts blending into `IDLE` (451 of 23, 754 of 39).

Seventh recording, whose recorder also writes the name of the operation in hand: both parameters, and the operation (a reload in hand, a look at the weapon, or neither), are the game's on every frame of all 49 scenarios, ten of which reload or look. The tail was pressed in (48: the aim key held from 0.29 s after the magazine went in, the trigger pressed 0.1 s later and again after the weapon was at rest): the sights come up on the frame of the key, with the weapon's animator still in `RELOAD IN`; the first press of the trigger fires nothing; the second fires. The simulation does the same on every frame. With the sights up in the tail the eye follows the sight on the weapon as the reload's clip still has it: fed the recorded place of the weapon's own bone, the replay's eye is within 1.6 mm (root mean square) and 8 mm at worst, where the sight at rest left it 45 mm off for half a second.

The animator does not play these states to their ends. Each of its transitions has an exit time (the fraction of the state being left at which it may start), a blend length (seconds, or a fraction of the state being left) and an offset into the next state. The next state's own clock, and so its marks, start when the blend starts. For the AKM:

| From | To | Leaves at | Blends for |
|---|---|---|---|
| `RELOAD START` (0.44 s) | `RELOAD OUT` | 0.30 of it, 0.13 s | 0.75 of it, 0.33 s |
| `RELOAD OUT` (1.43 s) | `RELOAD IN` | 0.78, 1.12 s | 0.22, 0.32 s |
| `RELOAD IN` (1.77 s) | `RELOAD END` | 0.90, 1.59 s | 0.10, 0.18 s |
| `RELOAD IN` | `RELOAD CHARGE END` | 0.95 | 0.05 |
| `RELOAD END` (0.46 s) | `IDLE` | 0.98, 0.45 s | 0.03, 0.01 s |
| `RELOAD CHARGE END` | `IDLE` | 0.92 | 0.25 s |

Firing, the fire-mode switch and the magazine check are entered from `IDLE` with no blend and return to it at their end with none.

**Which clip a state plays.** Two of these states choose between clips by a parameter, and the choice was got wrong until the seventh recording showed the hands' markers elsewhere:

- `IDLE` blends two clips by `LauncherID`, one at -1 and one at 0. The game sets that parameter only on a weapon with an underbarrel launcher (to -1 while the launcher is not the weapon in use). On any other weapon it stays at the 0 the controller starts it with, and the animator rests in the second clip, the one whose name speaks of a launcher (`akm_launcher_0_idle_off`). The two differ in the left hand: its marker sits 2.6 cm and 55 degrees apart, the left elbow's goal 6 mm, the left upper arm a degree. Recorded: both hand markers are that clip's within 0.1 mm and 0.1 degrees on every frame the weapon is at rest. `trp_sim::rest_clip`; before, the clip named `..._idle_armed` was taken.
- `FIREMODE` blends two clips by `FireMode`, which the game sets to its own number for the mode switched to: 0 for automatic, 1 for single shots (3 for bursts). The clip is therefore picked by the mode, not by the mode's place in the weapon's list, where single shots come first. Recorded: with the two the right way round the right hand's marker is within 8 mm through the switch (it was 72 mm off: the hand reaching for the selector the wrong way).

Measured for the AKM with its 30-round magazine: 3.3 s with a round chambered, the magazine out at 0.8 s and the new one in at 2.5 s. Played end to end, as an earlier version did, the same states take 4.1 s.

**When within a frame.** The game runs this logic and the weapon's animator once per drawn frame, after that frame's physics steps. Three things follow, each read off the fourth recording:

- A command acts in the frame it is given: the trigger pulled in a frame fires in that frame, and the round count drops then.
- A state a command starts has run none of its time when that frame ends (`FIRE` stands at its starting point of 0.2, `RELOAD START` at 0). Its clock starts with the next frame.
- A state left at an exit time hands the next state what is left of the frame after that point, as in the body's animator (`body-animator.md`, "Where in the frame a transition starts").

**The recoil's kick, and which physics step takes it** (read and recorded on 2026-10-09, when the recoil was checked over). Making a shot (`InitiateShot`) does not kick the weapon: it raises a flag on the firearm controller, and the controller's own late update, finding the flag, calls the weapon animation's `Shoot(strength)`. That late update is called at the end of the player's (`Player.VisualPass`), after the frame's physics steps have been run through the weapon's springs. So the springs feel a shot on the first physics step of a later frame, never in the frame the shot is made in. For automatic fire the round is taken and the shot made in one call (the fire mark's), so counted from the frame the rounds drop the kick is taken by the first physics step in a frame after it. A single shot takes its round a frame before it makes the shot (above), so its kick is taken by the first physics step in a frame from the second on: one physics step later than an automatic shot's wherever the frame after the press has a physics step, and the same step where it has none.

Recorded (the first shot of each firing scenario in the fourth to the tenth recording, 28 in all, with the springs at rest before it; the frames counted from the one the rounds drop on to the one the position spring first moves on). Automatic: 1 or 2 frames, the simulation's the same in 10 of 12 and a frame either way in the other two. Single: 2 or 3 frames in the game, never 1; with the shot's kick made with the round the simulation had 1 or 2, the game's own in 8 of 16, a frame early in 4 and two early in 4. Built 2026-10-09 (`Firearm`: a single shot's `Shot` event comes with the step after the one that takes the round).

## The quick reload, and the magazines as things (read 2026-10-14, when the user asked for the quick reload mechanic)

Read from `FirearmHandsInputTranslator` (`QuickReload`, `Reload`, `ReloadExternalMagazine`, `InIdleStateForInvokeOperation`), `Player.FirearmController` (`QuickReloadMag` in `FirearmOperation`, `FireOperationBase` and the idling operation; `ReloadExternalMagOperation`; `ReloadExternalMagResult.Run`; `ShouldForceQuickReload`), `FirearmsAnimator.Reload`, `Weapon.CanReloadFast`, `Inventory.FastAccessSlots`, `InventoryController.GetReachableItemsOfTypeNonAlloc` and `InventoryEquipmentExtension.GetPrioritizedGridsForUnloadedObject`, and from the install's AKM controller.

**The key.** `QuickReloadWeapon`, which the install binds to the reload key's double click (`R`, `DoubleClick`): that binding is why a plain press of `R` waits out the double click's time ("Three more keys" and `settings.md`). The command is heard only while the weapon's animator is at rest (`InIdleStateForInvokeOperation`, which asks the animator whether its hands' layer is in `IDLE` or one of its other resting states); the plain key has no such test. A weapon fired once and thrown away (`IsOneoff`) takes neither.

**What it does, by the weapon's reload mode** (`QuickReload`):

- `ExternalMagazine`: a magazine's reload, the quick way where it can be (below).
- `ExternalMagazineWithInternalReloadSupport`: the same, and with no magazine found, rounds a time into the fitted one (as the plain key's `ReloadWithAmmo`).
- `InternalMagazine`: a revolver's cylinder emptied whole and filled (`ReloadRevolverDrum` with its quick flag: every chamber counted free, the animator's `ReloadFast`); any other weapon of the mode, the plain key's rounds a time.
- `OnlyBarrel` with more barrels than one: the barrels loaded, as the plain key does.

The plain key goes the same way with the quick flag down, unless the head is stunned (`ShouldForceQuickReload`: an active stun on the head makes every reload quick).

**The magazine taken** (`ReloadExternalMagazine`). The magazines within reach are those lying directly in the grids of the pockets and of the vest worn, in that order (`FastAccessSlots`; the search does not go into things inside them, and never into the backpack), each holding at least one round. They are sorted fullest first, and the first that the weapon's magazine slot would take is used. With none, the character only says that it needs ammunition (`ReloadMagNotFound`), and the plain key then tries to put one loose round into an empty chamber by hand (`LoadAmmoToChamber`, the state `LOAD ONE`).

**Quick or plain.** The quick way is taken when the key asked for it, a magazine is fitted, and the weapon's template allows it (`isFastReload`, `Weapon.CanReloadFast`): `QuickReloadMag`. Otherwise the plain one, `ReloadMag`, which is given a place for the old magazine: the first free place in the vest's grids and the pockets', searched grid by grid with the smallest grid first (a tie keeps the vest before the pockets; the backpack is not among them). In the install 134 of the 176 weapons that say allow it. Of those built here the AKM, the Five-seveN, the SV-98 and the SCAR-L (so the StG 44) are reloaded the quick way by the key; the Model 700 allows it too, but its magazine stays in it (`InternalMagazine`), so its key fills it as the plain key does; the SKS, the Rhino, the MP-43e and the M3 do not allow it.

**The operations.** The idling operation takes the command at once: the sights come down below mastering 2, the trigger is let go, and the magazine's reload begins. A shot in hand takes it as it takes the plain one, planned for when the shot is over. Every other operation refuses it.

**The exchange is made as the reload begins** (`ReloadExternalMagResult.Run`). The old magazine is moved to the place found for it, or, with none (always, the quick way), taken out of the weapon with nowhere to go; the new one is moved into the weapon's magazine slot; and with the chamber empty, the new magazine's top round is moved into it. That is why a recording's count jumps on the reload's first frame ("Compared with the running game").

**The animator.** `FirearmsAnimator.Reload(old, new, quick)` sets the two magazine indices as for any reload (`RelTypeOld`, `RelTypeNew`) and then raises `ReloadFast` in place of `Reload`. In the AKM's controller `IDLE` leaves for `RELOAD OUT ALL` on `ReloadFast` with `MagInWeapon` and `CanReload` (blend 0.098 of the state): a clip of 1.0 s that throws the magazine out (`MagOut` 0.486, `MagHide` 0.602) and leaves for `RELOAD IN` at 0.9 of it, after which the quick reload is the plain one, the same states and the same endings. `RELOAD OUT ALL` stands in for the plain reload's `RELOAD START` (0.44 s, left at 0.3) and `RELOAD OUT` (1.43 s, left at 0.78).

**Where the old magazine goes** (`OnMagPuttedToRig`, at `MagHide`). A magazine that was taken out with nowhere to go is thrown into the world from where the weapon's magazine is at that moment, falling with a hundredth of a metre a second downwards and a random spin (`DropMod`, `GameWorld.ThrowItem`): loot on the ground, which keeps its rounds. A magazine that had a place is already there.

**What the plain reload's key does in the game, magazine by magazine**, follows from the same code: the fullest magazine within reach goes in, and the old one, with what is left in it, goes to the vest or the pockets. "A magazine put away is kept" (above) is that.

### Built (2026-10-14)

- `trp_sim::firearm`: `Firearm::quick_reload_with(rounds)` is the command: refused with the animator not at rest; quick where a magazine is fitted and `FirearmSettings::fast_reload` (the template's `isFastReload`) allows, else the plain reload. The animator is told `ReloadFast`, and then everything is the plain reload's. Two events tell the host: `MagazineSwap { left, quick }` as the reload begins (the rounds the old magazine leaves with, none if there was none fitted) and `MagazineHidden` at `MagHide`.
- `trp_sim::inventory`: `next_magazine` (the fullest magazine within reach that the weapon's slot takes), `swap_magazine` (the exchange at the start: the old magazine with its rounds to the vest or the pockets, or kept out to fall; the new one into the weapon), `magazine_rounds`, `set_magazine_rounds`, `fill_magazine`.
- The app: the plain reload and the quick one both take the magazine from the pockets and the vest, by those rules; the quick one's old magazine, and a plain one's that has no room, lie on the ground at `MagHide`, where they can be taken up again. A weapon in the crate has its magazine full in the inventory as in the hands, and every weapon fed from magazines has spare ones beside it.

### Not built, and open

- A revolver's quick reload (the cylinder emptied whole), the plain key's round put into the chamber by hand with no magazine found (`LoadAmmoToChamber`, `LOAD ONE`), the stun that makes every reload quick, the voice line for a magazine not found, and the left hand's layer that `ReloadFast` resets.
- `PROVISIONAL`: magazines of the same count are taken in the order they lie (the game's sort is not a stable one, so its order between two magazines that hold the same is not known).
- `PROVISIONAL`: the magazine in the weapon is brought up to date in the inventory when it leaves the weapon (its rounds then are the simulation's), where the game takes each round out of it as it is fired; the carried weight is the heavier by the rounds fired since the last reload.
- `ESTIMATE`: where a magazine let fall lies. The game drops it from the weapon's own magazine and lets it fall; the app has no physics, and lays it on the ground under where the weapon is held (`MAGAZINE_DROP` in `trp-app/src/inventory_view.rs`).

## The weapon's animator as a state machine (read 2026-10-11, the session after the evening's)

What follows is what the game's operations do to the weapon's animator, call by call, and when in a frame each thing happens: what is needed to run the weapon's controller as the engine runs it, in place of the fixed sequence of states above. Read from `FirearmsAnimator`, `ObjectInHandsAnimator`, `Player.FirearmController` and its operations, `AnimationEventSystem` and `WeaponPrefab`. The AKM's controller is `probe states 59d6088586f774275f37482f`.

**Which animator.** The local player's weapon runs on the engine's own animator, updated by the engine after every script's update, like the body's (`BaseLocalGame`: the arms' update mode is `Auto` unless the config switches the game's own "fast" animator on for the hands, `UseHandsFastAnimator`, which this install does not). Its controller has seven layers: `Base Layer` (empty), `Hands` (the weapon's states, weight 1, all 119 bones), `Selector`, `LActions` (the left hand's gestures, weight 0 until used), `Hammer`, `Stock`, `Malfunction`.

**A frame, in order.**

1. The frame's commands reach the controller (trigger, reload, fire mode and so on) and the operation in hand answers: it sets animator parameters, asks the animator to play a state outright, or hands over to another operation.
2. The operation's own update (`Player.ArmsUpdate` calls the controller's `ManualUpdate`): the wait after a single shot, the clock of automatic fire, the idle timer.
3. The engine updates the animator. Transitions are taken by the parameters as they now stand. A state asked for outright (`Animator.Play(state, layer, time)`) is entered at that time and has run none of it when the frame ends; so has a state a condition alone started. The states' marks that this update passes are **queued**, not delivered (the weapon's emitter is set to emit on demand).
4. The late update: the visual pass, at whose end the controller's own late update gives the recoil a shot that was made (`ManualLateUpdate`), and after it `Player.ComplexLateUpdate` calls `ArmsLateUpdate`, which **delivers the queued marks**. Each mark's conditions are tested then, against the parameters as they are then, and its handler runs: the operation in hand sets parameters, makes the shot, hands over.

So a parameter set by a mark's handler is first seen by the animator in the next frame, and a shot made by a mark's handler (a single shot's) is given to the recoil a frame after one made by a command (automatic fire's first): which is the frame between the two that the recordings show ("The recoil's kick", below).

**Marks** (`AnimationEventsContainer.CheckEvents`). Each state with marks keeps the point it had reached when last looked at, and is looked at when it is entered, on each update while it plays, and when it is left. A mark fires when its time lies at or after that point and before the state's time now. More exactly:

- A state that does not loop (nearly all of the weapon's) fires only through its first cycle, with both ends held to 0 and 1; past 1 nothing more fires. When it is left its point goes back to 0.
- Entered part-way, every mark before the entry fires at that first look. Entered at exactly 0 with none of the frame left, nothing fires until the next update (the two ends are equal); `IdleStart`, at 0 of `IDLE`, so fires on the first update that moves `IDLE` on.
- **A state being left fires all its remaining marks at once**, on the first update in which a transition out of it is under way: its point is put to the end. `RELOAD OUT`'s `MagHide` (0.850) therefore comes when the blend into `RELOAD IN` starts (0.779), not at its own time.
- A mark may carry conditions on animator parameters: a whole number equal, not equal, greater, less, at least or at most a value; a float greater or less than one; a flag equal to one. In the AKM's `FIRE`: `FiringBullet` and `DelAmmoChamber` want `AmmoInChamber` above 0, `ShellEject` and `FireEnd` want `Malfunction` 0, `AddAmmoInChamber` wants `AmmoInMag` above 0 and `AmmoInChamber` below 1 with no malfunction.
- **Queued marks are handed out one at a time** (`AnimationEventsEmitter.EmitEvents`): each is tested and its handler run before the next is tested, so a parameter one mark's handler sets is what the next mark's conditions see, within the same frame. (Built so since 2026-10-12; until then all of a frame's marks were tested before any handler ran. Nothing of the AKM's turns on it, and every recording replays as before; the M3's bolt catch does: `shotgun.md`.)
- The marks' handlers: `WeapIn`, `WeapOut`, `IdleStart`, `FiringBullet`, `ShellEject`, `DelAmmoChamber`, `AddAmmoInChamber`, `FireEnd`, `MagOut`, `MagHide`, `MagShow`, `MagIn`, `StartUtilityOperation` each call the operation in hand; `Arm` and `Disarm` set the animator's `Armed` flag (and the `Hammer` layer's weight to 1 while not armed); `Sound` plays the named sound; `ThirdAction` tells the body's animator.

**The parameters and who sets them** (`FirearmsAnimator`; a trigger is a flag the transition that uses it clears):

| Parameter | Set |
|---|---|
| `Active` | true as the weapon is drawn (`SpawnOperation`), false as it is put away (`Remove`) |
| `Inventory` | the inventory open, by the idling operation at once; a shot or a burst sets it as it hands back to idling; a reload sets it to false as it starts and to the screen's state at `MagOut` and `MagShow` |
| `Fire` | the trigger as the controller has it (not the finger): see each operation |
| `AmmoInChamber`, `AmmoInMag` | the rounds, at the points below |
| `MagInWeapon` | false at `MagOut`, true at `MagIn` |
| `CanReload` | true when a reload starts; nothing here sets it back |
| `Reload` (trigger) | a reload starts |
| `RelTypeOld`, `RelTypeNew` | the animation index of the magazine in the weapon and of the one going in (`magAnimationIndex`); the first again at `MagIn` |
| `FireMode`, `FiremodeSwitch` (trigger) | the mode switched to, by the game's own number (single 1, automatic 0, burst 3), and the switch |
| `CheckAmmo` (trigger) | a look at the magazine |
| `Armed` | the `Arm` and `Disarm` marks |
| `WeaponLevel` | the character's mastering of the weapon |
| `Aim_angle` | the view's pitch, every frame, where the controller has the parameter (the AKM's has not) |
| `LauncherID`, `StockFolded`, `IncompatibleAmmo`, `BoltCatch`, `Malfunction` and the rest | as the weapon is; for the stock AKM they stay as the controller starts them |

**The operations.**

- *Being drawn* (`SpawnOperation`). `Active` true, the `LActions` layer's weight 0, the body's animator told the weapon's width and that the hands are busy (`ReloadFloat` 1), `AmmoInChamber` and `AmmoInMag` as the weapon has them. The animator leaves `SPAWN` for `OUT TO IDLE` (or, with a magazine of rounds and an empty chamber, `OUT TO IDLE ARM`, which racks the bolt). At `WeapIn` (the draw's last mark) the weapon has appeared: the idling operation takes over, the body is told the hands are free, the weapon's length is measured. The opening of the inventory does nothing to this operation; the animator's flag is whatever idling last set, and `OUT TO IDLE` leaves for `OPEN INVENTORY` at 0.78 of itself if it is up.
- *Idling.* As it starts it puts the animator's values in order (`SetAnimatorValues`: the fire mode without the switch, `AmmoInChamber`, `MagInWeapon`, `AmmoInMag`, `RelTypeOld`, the stock). *The trigger* (`SetTriggerPressed`): a press is dropped in a movement state that gives the hands no say, and while a reload's flag is up; otherwise `AmmoInChamber` and `AmmoInMag` are set and, pressed with a round chambered, a single shot or automatic fire starts by the mode; else `Fire` is set to the trigger, and a press dry-fires (the animator goes from `IDLE` to `DRY FIRE` on `Armed`, no round and `Fire`, or to `DRY FIRE DISARMED` when not armed; either leaves for `IDLE` at the end of a cycle once `Fire` is down). *The fire mode* (`ChangeFireMode`): the weapon's mode changes at once, `FireMode` is set and `FiremodeSwitch` raised; the operation stays idling. *A look at the magazine* (`CheckAmmo`): sights down, `CheckAmmo` raised, the body told the hands are busy, and a `UtilityOperation` takes over until the look's own first mark (`StartUtilityOperation`) and then `IdleStart` have come. *The inventory*: sights down, trigger let go, `Inventory` set. *Put away* (`HideWeapon`): sights down, trigger let go, `Remove` takes over. *`IdleStart`*: the body is told the hands are free and the reload's flag on the trigger is cleared.
- *A single shot* (`FireOperation`). At the start: the rounds are settled (`PrepareShot`), `Fire` set to the trigger, the wait set to `60 / SingleFireRate`, and `FIRE` asked for outright at 0.2. `FiringBullet` (delivered at the end of that same frame, since the state is entered part-way): the shot is made, the trigger let go, `Fire` false. `DelAmmoChamber`: `AmmoInChamber` 0. `AddAmmoInChamber`: `AmmoInChamber` and `AmmoInMag` as after the shot, `Fire` to the trigger. `FireEnd`: the trigger let go. `IdleStart` after the shot: ready to end. Each update the wait runs down, and once it is out and the operation is ready it hands back to idling, setting `AmmoInChamber`, `AmmoInMag`, `Inventory` and `Fire`; a second press that was remembered is then a press and a release.
- *Automatic fire* (`AutomaticFireOperation`). At the start: `Fire` set to the trigger, `FIRE` asked for outright at 0.2, and the first shot made at once. Its clock does not run until the first `FiringBullet` has been delivered. Then each update takes the clock through the cycle of `60 / FireRate`: a shot at 0 (with `Fire` set to the trigger), the shell at 0.25, at 0.75 `AmmoInChamber` put to 0 and then it and `AmmoInMag` to what they are after the shot and `Fire` to the trigger, and at 0.99 the end of the cycle ("Automatic fire" above), which sets the two counts again. The animator is not asked for `FIRE` again: `FIRE` runs out into `IDLE`, and `IDLE` goes back to `FIRE` by its own transition while `Fire` is up and a round is chambered. Handing back to idling: the trigger let go, `Inventory` and `Fire` set, and **`IDLE` asked for outright at 0.1**. After the last round, with no bolt catch, the trigger is pressed again, which dry-fires. `FIRE` itself leaves for `DRY FIRE` at 0.95 when the mode is automatic and chamber and magazine are both empty.
- *A reload with a magazine* (`BaseReloadOperation`, `ReloadExternalMagOperation`). At the start: `Inventory` false, the body told (`ReloadFloat` 1, `ProneReload`), the flag that refuses the trigger raised, `CanReload` true, `RelTypeOld` and `RelTypeNew`, `Reload` raised. `MagOut`: `Inventory` to the screen's state, `MagInWeapon` false. `MagShow`: `Inventory` again. `MagIn`: `MagInWeapon` true, `AmmoInMag` the new magazine's count (one more if a round is to be chambered from it), `RelTypeOld` the new magazine's; with nothing to chamber, the handover to idling. `AddAmmoInChamber`: `AmmoInMag`, `AmmoInChamber`, the handover. The inventory opening in a reload is only noted until the magazine is out or the new one has shown. The animator's own transitions do the rest: `IDLE` to `RELOAD START` on `CanReload`, `Reload` and `MagInWeapon`; `RELOAD OUT` to `RELOAD IN` on `CanReload`; `RELOAD IN` to `RELOAD CHARGE END` with rounds in the magazine and none chambered, else to `RELOAD END`; with `Inventory` up, to `RELOAD INVENTORY END` and `IN INVENTORY` instead.
- *Put away* (`Remove`). `Active` false (and `FastHide`), the body told the hands are busy. The animator goes `IDLE TO OUT` and at `WeapOut` the weapon is hidden and the next thing is made.

**An exit point is a crossing.** A way out that waits for a point in its state is taken only in a frame whose two ends have the point between them, and a point in the first cycle comes round every cycle whether the state loops or not (`body-animator.md`, "States and how one gives way to another"). `DRY FIRE` and `DRY FIRE DISARMED` are the weapon's case: 0.267 s long, not looping, left "at 1 once `Fire` is down". With the trigger held past the first cycle and let go in the second, the state is left at 2, not at once. Recorded (thirteenth recording, `24-reload-empty`: the trigger let go at 1.44 of the state, `IDLE` entered at 2.00).

**Built** (2026-10-11).

- *The engine* (`trp_sim::animator`) can be asked for a state outright (`Animator::play`: entered by the next update, which runs none of it), keeps what each update looked at (`Look`: each state entered, played on or left, with its time and whether a transition out of it was under way), and takes an exit point as a crossing.

- *A weapon's animator* (`trp_sim::weapon_animator`): the weapon's own controller read whole (the AKM's: 99 states over seven layers, 104 clips) and posed over every bone of the rig; parameters by name; `play`; `update`; `deliver`. Its clips' scale tracks are not posed: none of the stock AKM's, sampled through its reloads, shots and looks, is away from 1.
- *Beside the weapon's logic* at first, watched and not obeyed; since the night of the same day the logic goes by it ("The weapon goes by its animator", below).

The replay runs the animator beside the logic, started where the recording's stood, and sets the three beside the game's (`hands_anim`, `hands_anim_time`, `hands_blending`, and `machine_entering` for the state being entered; `bash local/hstates.sh <game trace> <sim trace>` lists every frame on which they part).

## The weapon goes by its animator (2026-10-11, night)

Until then the logic stepped a fixed sequence of states of its own, each cut down to a length, its marks and one way out, and the animator ran beside it, watched. Now the animator is what there is. `Firearm` holds the operation in hand and the weapon's own controller on the engine's state machine; the operation tells the animator what the game's tells its own; and everything the logic waits for is a mark the animator delivers at the frame's end. Which state follows which, how long each lasts and how two are blended is the controller's own data and nothing else. A host draws the animator's pose.

What set it off: the user, the same day, on the weapon going to its hold for an open inventory: "it blends with a empty reload better in tarkov". The fixed sequence finished a reload, brought the hands back onto the weapon, and only then took them off again. The game's animator has a way of its own from a reload into the hold (below).

**Read for it** (`Player.FirearmController`: `Idling`, `FireOperationBase`, `FireOperation`, `AutomaticFireOperation`, `BaseReloadOperation`, `ReloadExternalMagOperation`, `SpawnOperation`, `Remove`, `UtilityOperation`), beyond what "The operations" above has:

- **A command is answered by the operation in hand, and where that has no answer it is dropped** (the base class logs the call and does nothing). The trigger: idling, a single shot (which remembers a second) and automatic fire answer; a reload, a look at the weapon, a draw and a put-away do not. The fire mode and the look at the magazine: idling only, and the fire mode in a look too, since a look is an idling operation with some of its answers taken away.
- **Idling is the operation in hand through every tail:** while a reload's animation plays out, through a fire-mode switch, while the hands are down for the inventory. So the fire mode can be switched, the magazine looked at and another reload begun in a reload's tail (the animator takes the trigger parameter up when it is next at rest), and the trigger and the reload key work through a fire-mode switch (a shot asks the animator for `FIRE` outright, whatever it is playing). A press of the trigger is refused in a reload's tail only by a flag the reload raises and `IdleStart` clears. (All of it recorded on 2026-10-12: below.)
- **A shot plans.** A reload asked for during a single shot or a run of automatic fire is kept as the one thing the shot hands over to when it ends, in place of idling: a single shot when its wait is out and the weapon back at rest, automatic fire at the end of the cycle. The trigger is let go and the sights are put down. A put-away asked for in a shot is planned the same way and takes a planned reload's place. While something is planned no reload is taken.
- **A shot notes the inventory for itself.** Opened in a single shot or in automatic fire, the screen is noted in the operation and the animator told as it hands back to idling; a second shot that was remembered is then not fired. **Automatic fire ends at the end of the cycle in which the inventory is open, trigger held or not.** (The controller's own note of the inventory is not touched by a shot, so a reload planned in that shot tells the animator the older state at its marks. Kept as read.)
- **A reload** tells the animator of the inventory at once if its magazine is out or the new one has shown, and otherwise at those two marks. Each of its marks is answered once. `IdleStart` reaching a reload whose new magazine has shown ends it. A put-away asked for in a reload happens as it hands over to idling.
- **The animator's own ways for an open inventory** (the AKM's; `probe states <weapon> INVENTORY`). `RELOAD IN` leaves for `RELOAD INVENTORY END` (at 0.94 of itself, blended over 0.16) when the flag is up and a round is chambered or the magazine going in is empty, where it would leave for `RELOAD END`. `RELOAD CHARGE END` leaves for it at 0.98, where it would leave for `IDLE`. `RELOAD INVENTORY END` (`akm_hands_out_half_2`, 0.333 s) gives way to `IN INVENTORY` a quarter of the way through, blended over the rest of itself. So **a reload made or finished with the inventory open ends in the hold, and the hands do not come back to the weapon in between**, whether a round was chambered or the bolt had to be racked. Besides: `OUT TO IDLE` has a way into `OPEN INVENTORY` (at 0.78); `IN INVENTORY` has one into `IDLE TO OUT` (a weapon put away from the hold, blended over 0.35 s) and ways into `RELOAD IN`, `INV TO RELOAD`, `LOAD ONE`, `MOD SETUP` and `DISCHARGE` for what is done to the weapon in the screen (none of those is built).
- **Being drawn** does not hear of the inventory (`SpawnOperation.SetInventoryOpened` is empty). But **when the weapon has appeared the player is told how the screen stands afresh**: the draw's last act is the call that says the hands have changed, the player's owner answers it by setting the new controller, and that ends in `Player.SetInventoryOpened` with whether the inventory screen is the one showing (`Player.SpawnController`, `PlayerOwner.RefreshHandsController`, `GamePlayerOwner.SetHandsController`). The idling operation, in hand by then, tells the animator. So **a weapon drawn under an open screen comes up, and goes down for the screen as soon as it is up** (`IDLE`, `OPEN INVENTORY`, `IN INVENTORY`). The same is done for empty hands and for a grenade. (Read on 2026-10-12, after the recordings made by hand showed the hands' animator told of the screen on the very frame something had appeared; the night before's reading had missed the owner's part and had the weapon stay up. The knife's draw reads the player's flag besides.) A weapon with rounds in its magazine and none chambered takes one over as the draw starts; the animator, whose counts are still the old ones, plays `OUT TO IDLE ARM`, and that state's `AddAmmoInChamber` mark gives it the new counts. The draw ends at `WeapIn`, or at `IdleStart` if that comes first.
- **Being put away:** idling (and a look) put the sights down, let the trigger go and start `Remove`: `Active` off, the body told the hands are busy. At `WeapOut` the weapon is hidden and the next thing made.
- **A look at the weapon** gives up 2.5 s after it started if its state's first mark has not come (the time is not counted while the inventory is open).
- **The body's `ReloadFloat`** is raised by a reload's start, a look's start, a draw and a put-away, and lowered by `IdleStart` reaching the idling operation and by the weapon having appeared.
- **The aim key in a shot** sets the sights with nothing asked: not the movement state, not the obstacle.

**Built.** `trp-sim/src/firearm.rs`: the operations `Spawn`, `Idle`, `Single`, `Automatic`, `Reload`, `Utility`, `Remove` (and `Away`, not in the hands), each with the game's answers to the trigger, the aim key, the reload key, the inventory and a put-away, and to the marks; `Firearm::draw`, `hide`, `start_drawn` (in hand and at rest, for a replay and a run that begins armed); `Event::Appeared` and `Hidden`. Its settings carry no timing any more. `trp-sim/src/empty_hands.rs`: the empty hands' controller on their own animator (`inventory.md`, "Empty hands"). `rig::WeaponRig::pose` puts a rig as an animator has it. The fixed sequence, the per-state timings (`StateTiming`), and the clip players of the replay and of the app are gone. The logic's unit tests run on a controller written out by hand (`Controller::by_hand`, eighteen states with round numbers).

**Compared with the running game** (the regression kept as `after27`, every recording from the third to the fourteenth and the vault traces, set beside `after26` value by value: `local/metricdiff.sh`):

- **Nothing the logic gives out moved.** Rounds, chamber, sights, the operation in hand, the body's two flags, recoil, the springs, distances, the eye, stamina: the largest gap of every one of them is the same in every scenario.

## Aiming, and what takes it away

Whether the sights are up is the firearm controller's (`IsAiming`), and the aim key only asks: pressed it asks for them up, let go it asks for them down (`SetAim`, passed to the operation in hand). Read on 2026-10-08 and compared with the sixth recording the same day (below).

- **By movement state.** The settings object lists the movement states that do not allow aiming (`NO_AIM_STATES`): crawling, sprinting, jumping, falling, breaching a door, looting and picking up. Entering one puts the sights down, and a request to raise them in one is refused. Going down to lie, lying still, getting up, landing from a jump and walking are not on the list. A jump also puts them down by a call of its own. (The falling state on the list is one the game never enters: `movement.md`, "Falling". What a fall does to the sights is "By a long fall", below.)
- **By the sprint switching on.** The character's sprint is a flag of its own (`Physical.Sprinting`, kept by the stamina object as "the sprint is being paid for"), and it comes on before the sprinting movement state does: the sprint key in a walk forward sets it at once, while the animator only enters its sprint state when the speed has wound up (0.23 s later in the sixth recording). Whenever what the stamina pays for changes, the firearm controller is told the flag (`SprintStateChangedEvent`). Told that it is on, it puts the sights down and lets go of the trigger. So the sights go down on the frame of the key, not on the frame the sprint starts. In the window between the two the movement state is still the walk, which allows aiming, so a fresh press of the aim key raises them again until the sprint state puts them down.
- **Only an idle weapon obeys.** All three of these (the state entered, the sprint on, the jump or fall) are calls on the operation the weapon's logic has in hand, and only the idling operation acts on them (and a bolt action's shot). In a single shot, in automatic fire and in a reload the call is one of those the game only logs. The sights then stay up until the next such call finds the weapon idle. The trigger's release when the sprint comes on is the controller's own and happens whatever the operation. Read from the code; no recording fires or reloads into one of these.
- **They do not come back by themselves.** Nothing raises the sights again when the state ends with the key still held; the key has to be pressed again. The one exception is the obstacle, next.
- **By an obstacle** (read again and built on 2026-10-09; `weapon-handling.md`, "Turn-away", has how the depth is measured). At the end of every frame the controller knows how deep the weapon would stick into something. Deeper than `STOP_AIMING_AT` (0.1 m, settings object) with the sights up, it puts them down and remembers that an obstacle did it. Once the depth is under that again and the turn-away has run down to a value under 0.2, with the sights still down, it puts them up and forgets. Both go through the call the aim key makes, so the operation in hand has its say as with the key; and any use of the key, either way, clears the memory, so sights let go of while pushed back stay down. The weapon at rest refuses a raise while the depth is over the limit; so does a reload in hand from mastering 2 (below that it refuses everything). A shot in hand does not ask. Built: `Firearm::overlap`, told the depth by the host each frame. **Recorded** (ninth recording, 53 and 54: lying and crouched, the view turned down until the weapon is in the ground and back): the sights go down on the frame the depth passes 0.1 m and come back by themselves when it is under that and the value under 0.2, in the game and in the simulation alike (`weapon-handling.md`, "Turn-away", has the table). The aim key let go and pressed with the weapon pushed back by less than the limit raises them as anywhere. Sights let go of while pushed back past the limit staying down is still from the code: the script meant for it ended with the weapon 8 cm in.
- **By a long fall.** The controller puts the sights down, every frame, once the character has been falling for more than half a second. It does so through the aim key's own call (so a reload in hand below mastering 2, or a look at the weapon, ignores it), after the movement's update of the same frame, and nothing brings them back. Built on 2026-10-09: `Movement::long_fall`, on which a host calls `Firearm::set_aim(false, ..)` each frame. Seen in the app (a walk off a 2.5 m platform with the aim key held: up 0.35 s into the fall, down at 0.6 s and after the landing); not recorded.
- **Refused also:** while blind firing (not built).
- **What the sights sound** (2026-10-16, 0.41.0; `sound.md`, "The aiming's sounds"): a change made by `SetAim` (the key, the obstacle, the long fall, a vault, a climb) rattles the kit and, coming up, plays the weapon's aiming clip; the state entered and the sprint switched on go through `OnAimingDisabled` and are silent. `Firearm::set_aim` says so (`Event::Aimed`); `lower_aim` and `sprint_started` do not.
- **A reload.** Starting one puts the sights down if the character's mastering of the weapon is below level 2 (or the weapon is a belt-fed machine gun). While the reload operation is the one in hand the aim key then does nothing either way, up or down. That is until the new magazine is in (or the round chambered, from an empty chamber), not until the animation ends: in the tail after it the weapon's logic is idle and the key works ("Reloading with a magazine"). From level 2 the sights stay up through the reload and the key works.
- **A look at the weapon.** Checking the magazine, checking the chamber, checking the fire mode and examining the weapon are one kind of operation in the game (`UtilityOperation`). Starting one of the first three puts the sights down, and for as long as it is in hand the aim key does nothing either way; neither does the trigger (but in examining, which a press ends), a reload cannot start and no second check can. It ends as a reload's tail does, at `IdleStart`, on the frame the weapon's animator starts back into `IDLE`. Switching the fire mode is not one of these: the idling operation does it itself, so the sights stay as they are and the key works. Read from the code on 2026-10-08. Built for the magazine check, the only look the simulation has. Compared with the game (seventh recording, 42: a look at the magazine with the aim key held, let go 0.7 s into it and pressed again for half a second): the sights go down on the frame of the look, neither the release nor the press does anything, the look is in hand for 4.34 s, and the key pressed after it raises them; the simulation's sights and its operation in hand are the game's on every frame. In a sprint (43) likewise.
- **The reload with the sights up** ("tactical reload"). While a reload is under way and the sights are up, a blend runs to 1 at 4 per second; when either ends it runs back at 2 per second after 0.2 s. (The game's blend is `clamp01(start + (now - startTime) * speed * direction)` with the start time put 0.2 s ahead, so through the wait the value sits above where it started, at most at 1.) By that blend:
  - the `weapon` bone, which the reload's clip swings about, is held toward its rest: its turn about x and z is cut by `TacticalReloadStiffnes.x` and `.z` times the blend, its turn about y by `.y` times the blend and applied about a point on the moved bone at the recoil pivot's depth, and its position is drawn toward zero by `TacticalReloadFixation` times the blend (both per weapon, from the item template);
  - the camera's target is worked out from where the sight rests on the weapon, not from where the clip has swung it;
  - the weapon clip's turning of the camera is weighted by one less the blend.

What is built: everything but the last in `Firearm` (`set_aim`, `lower_aim`, `sprint_started`, `overlap`, `aiming`; `FirearmSettings::mastering`, 0 unless the host sets it) with `Movement::can_aim`, `take_aim_lowered` and `take_sprint_started` telling the host when to call them; and the blend's value (`Firearm::tactical_reload`). What the blend does to the weapon bone and the camera is **not built**: the recorded character's mastering of the weapon is 0, where the game never shows it.

**Compared with the running game** (sixth recording, recorder 0.3.0, which writes the firearm controller's own `IsAiming`, whether the movement state allows aiming, and the character's mastering in the header: 0). The simulation's sights are up on exactly the game's frames in every one of these:

| Scenario | What the game does | Simulation |
|---|---|---|
| Reload with the aim key held (29) | sights down on the reload's first frame; they stay down after it though the key is still held | the same on every frame |
| Aim key pressed and let go during a reload, pressed again after (30) | nothing during the reload; up on the later press | the same |

## The skill's two speeds (`SkillManager.GetWeaponInfo`, `FirearmController.SetAnimatorAndProceduralValues`, `FirearmsAnimator.SetSpeedParameters`; read and built on 2026-10-12, when the user asked that the weapons' animations go by the skills)

The weapon's animator has two parameters, `SpeedReload` and `SpeedDraw`, and each of its states may name one of them as the multiplier of its own speed. Which states go by which is the controller's own: a reload's states by the first, the draws and the put-aways by the second.

The game sets both when a weapon is taken in hand and whenever the character's skills are read again, from the level (0 to 51) of **the skill of the weapon's class** (Pistols, Assault rifles, Shotguns, Sniper rifles and so on):

- `SpeedReload = 1 + 0.004 * level` (the buff `WeaponReloadBuff`; a bot adds a buff of its own),
- `SpeedDraw = 1 + 0.008 * level` (`WeaponSwapBuff`),

both figures written in the game's code. The body's animator is told the same two. With either arm damaged both are 1. A pistol drawn fast from the holster on a double press of its key gets a draw speed from the settings object instead (`WeaponFastDrawGlobalSettings`).

**Built:** `trp_sim::firearm::weapon_skill_speeds`, `FirearmSettings::reload_speed` and `draw_speed`, told to the animator each time the weapon is taken in hand. The animator already went by a state's speed parameter; nothing had set the two, so every state ran at 1. The app takes one level for every weapon (`--weapon-skill <0..51>`), and 0, an untrained character, unless told.

**On the install's own controllers** (`a_weapon_skill_speeds_the_reload_and_the_draw`), level 0 and level 50:

| Weapon | Drawn | Reloaded |
|---|---|---|
| AKM | 0.96 s and 0.69 s | 3.30 s and 2.75 s |
| FN Five-seveN | 0.97 and 0.69 | 3.41 and 2.84 |
| Remington Model 700 | 1.00 and 0.72 | 3.82 and 3.18 (one round into the magazine) |

which is 1.40 times as fast drawn and 1.20 times reloaded, the parameters' own factors.

**Compared with the game:** nothing. Every kept recording replays with the weapon's animator at a speed of 1 and has the game's time through every state on every frame, so the recorded character had level 0 in each weapon's skill: no recording shows a skilled speed. **Not built:** the body's animator's two speeds; the damaged arm; the fast draw; the level read from a profile; what else the skill changes (the recoil, the ergonomics, the aiming).

## Three more keys (`FirearmHandsInputTranslator`, `Idling.CheckFireMode`, `Idling.ExamineWeapon`, `UtilityOperation`, `FireModeComponent.GetForceAutoFireMode`; read and built 2026-10-12, night)

All three are the resting operation's to take; in anything else they do nothing.

- **The look at the selector** (`CheckFireMode`; `B` with `Left Alt` in the install). Only a weapon with more than one way of firing. The sights go down, the animator is given `FiremodeCheck`, and the look is the same operation as the looks at the magazine and into the chamber (`UtilityOperation`): ready to end at its state's first mark, over at the resting state's, given up after 2.5 s with no mark. The AKM's state for it lasts 0.87 s. (The game also shows the mode's name on its screen; the app's top line always has it.)
- **The look all over** (`ExamineWeapon`; `L`). **Refused with the sights up**, which it does not put down. The animator is given `Look`; the AKM's and the M3's states for it last 3.33 s. It is the one look **the trigger ends**: pressed or let go, the look is over at once and the resting operation takes the trigger from there (a press with a round chambered fires out of the look's state, which the animator has a way out of for it).
- **The fullest way of firing** (`ForceAutoWeaponMode`; `B` twice). Of the weapon's ways of firing the first there is of automatic, then bursts (then, of weapons not built, the doublet and the double action); a weapon with one way, or with none of those, stays as it is (the M3: pumped or self-loading). The change itself is the fire mode's own (above, "the mode changes at once and the animator plays the switch when it is next at rest").

The fire mode's own key waits out the double click's time since the fullest way of firing is that key's double click (`settings.md`).

**Built:** `Firearm::check_fire_mode`, `examine`, `force_auto_fire_mode`; the look all over is the utility operation with a flag of its own (`Operation::Utility { examine }`). **Compared with the game: nothing**; no recording has any of the three. Tried against the install's own animators (`a_weapon_is_looked_over_and_put_on_automatic`): the AKM's `FIREMODE CHECK` and `LOOK` are entered and left for `IDLE`, the look lasts the clip's 3.33 s, a press of the trigger a second into it fires, the sights refuse it, automatic is chosen from single and a second asking changes nothing, and the M3 stays in its mode.

### How the look all over is drawn: on curves without corners (the user, 2026-10-13, late: "Can we add interpolation the inspect to make it smooth?")

**This is a departure from the game in what is drawn, made at the user's word, and in nothing else.**

- **What the engine does.** A clip keeps most of its curves as samples, 30 a second, and joins them by straight lines; some clips keep the same thing as a key at every sample with a straight span from each to the next (the AKM's look is the first kind, the Five-seveN's the second). Through a look the weapon is turned fast in the right hand while the left is off it, and the left hand's place is kept by a curve on the weapon that undoes the turn: two things straight between samples, multiplied, are right at each sample and up to 6 mm off between them. The hand stutters 30 times a second (`docs/findings.md`, 2026-10-13, late). That is what the simulation has, and what the app drew. **The game's own does too**: in the user's two recordings of the look (course 5, read 2026-10-16) the left hand's velocity holds for four frames at 120 a second and steps on the next (`docs/findings.md`, 2026-10-16, night).
- **What is drawn instead, by a switch of the options since 2026-10-14** (below, "Every animation of the thing in hand"). Where asked, the animator works out a second pose beside its own (`Animator::set_drawn_smooth`): the same states at the same moments, with the clips' sampled curves taken through their samples on a curve without corners (`Clip::sample_smooth`). Between two samples the curve is the cubic that leaves the one and reaches the other with the slope there, and the slope at a sample is the mean of the two steps either side of it; none where the curve turns back at that sample, and never more than three times the smaller step, so that a jump from one sample to the next is not overshot before or after it. A straight line stays a straight line. A curve kept as keys is taken so only where it is samples under another name: a span that is straight and no longer than a sample and a half. A span its maker curved, and a straight line across many samples, are left.
- **What it leaves alone.** The animator's own pose, its states, their times and its marks are bit for bit what they are without it, and the simulation reads those: the weapon's moved bone, the sights' line, the camera, the hands' hold as others see it. Only the bones of the rig as they are drawn come from the second pose, and the arms are solved onto them as drawn. The regression over the kept recordings is unchanged.
- **Only the look all over** (`L`), from 2026-10-13, late, to 2026-10-14. The looks at the selector, the chamber and the magazine, the reloads and everything else were drawn on the engine's straight lines, as the game has them.

The host asks each frame (`Firearm::set_drawn_smooth`, `EmptyHands::set_drawn_smooth`); unasked, as for the replay and the recorder's scripts, nothing is smoothed. The body's animator, whose legs the character's own eyes see, is not among them. **What it leaves alone is as above**: the simulation's pose, states, times, marks and events are bit for bit the same either way (`tests\look.rs`, `the_weapon_draws_smooth_when_asked_and_only_so`: two AKMs, one asked and one not, through a draw, the rest, a reload and the look, the same on every frame of 1.67 ms; the regression `after47`, every report the same as `after46`'s but for how long reading the clips took).

## What the simulation does and does not do

`crates/trp-sim/src/firearm.rs` implements the above as the game's operations on the weapon's own animator (`crates/trp-sim/src/weapon_animator.rs`, which reads every state, transition, blend tree, layer and mark of the weapon's controller): "The weapon goes by its animator".

- The states, their order, their lengths and their blends are the animator's. The logic keeps the operation in hand and answers the animator's marks, each with its conditions tested when it is delivered. Where a state chooses its clip by a parameter the animator's blend tree does the choosing.
- The app and the replay draw the animator's pose: every bone of the weapon's rig as the layers leave it, the selector's and the hammer's layers included. The app draws it between the simulation's frames, a physics step behind, as it draws the body (`Controller::rig_shown`): the simulation's own results are those of its frames.
- In automatic fire the clock waits for the fire state's first `FiringBullet`, as the game's does: the mark is delivered at the end of the frame of the press, so the clock first runs a frame after it. The animator goes through `IDLE` between shots by its own ways.
- A press of the trigger is dropped by a weapon at rest while the movement state gives the hands no say (`Firearm::set_trigger` is told by `Movement::can_press_trigger`; "Pressing the trigger"). The state asked is the one the frame began in, as for the aim key, where the recordings bear that out; for the trigger nothing is recorded.
- The fire mode changes when the switch is asked for and the animator plays the switch when it is next at rest; the weapon idles throughout. The reload key and the trigger both work through it (recorded: the fifteenth recording, `64` and `65`).
- A reload fits a full magazine unless the host says how many rounds the one going in holds (`Firearm::reload_with`). What the player carries belongs to the host game.
- A reload is in two parts, as in the game: in hand until the magazine is in or the round chambered (`Firearm::reloading`), then playing out with the logic idling. In the second part the aim key works and the trigger does not. A reload key pressed in it begins a second reload (recorded: the fifteenth recording, `69`).
- An empty magazine going into a weapon with an empty chamber ends through `RELOAD END`, with nothing to chamber, as the animator's conditions have it.
- **The inventory open** (`inventory.md`, "The inventory open"). The logic is told (`Firearm::set_inventory`) and the operation in hand makes of it what the game's does; the hands go down, are held and come back by the animator's own states, from rest or from a reload, and the logic idles through all of it.
- `Firearm::step` is called once a frame, as the game runs it: the operation's own update, the animator's, then the marks. The replay's frames are the recording's; the app's are those of its own fixed frame clock (120 a second, `trp_sim::Pacer`), so that shots and stages do not land differently at different rates of drawing.
- The wait between single shots is counted from the press; the shot ends when the wait is out and the animator has been back at rest (`IdleStart`), whichever is later.
- A weapon whose magazine stays in it is its own spec: the pumped and the self-loading shot, the tube filled a shell at a time, the bolt caught open and the look into the chamber are in `shotgun.md` (the Benelli M3 Super 90, built 2026-10-12 and set beside the sixteenth recording).
- A rifle whose bolt is worked by hand is in `bolt-action.md` and a pistol in `pistol.md` (the Remington Model 700 and the FN Five-seveN, built 2026-10-12 from the code and the install; on no recording).
- Not covered: malfunctions and overheating, revolvers, tactical and quick reloads, grenade launchers, shot dispersion and ballistics.
