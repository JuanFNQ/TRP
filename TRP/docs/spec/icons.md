# The inventory's pictures: an item's own model, drawn once and kept

How the game makes the picture of an item in its inventory, read from its code on 2026-10-14 (`IconCreatorBase`, `ItemIconCreator`, `ItemIconCache`, `ItemViewFactory`, `PreviewPivot`, `IconShadow`, `ShaderReplacer`, `WeaponPreview.GetBounds`) and from its files (each item's prefab; the `IconCamera` prefab in `EscapeFromTarkov_Data\resources.assets`). The user, 2026-10-14: "Yes, let us copy what tarkov does." What is built, and what was compared with what, is at the end.

## What the game does

- **The picture is the item's own model, drawn once and kept.** Asked for an item's picture (`ItemIconCreator.GetItemIcon`), the game looks for it in memory, then in a cache on disk (`Icon Cache` in the game's temporary folder: an `index.json` from a hash of the item and its parts to a numbered PNG), and only then draws it: it makes the item's model as it would lie on the ground (`CreateCleanLootPrefabAsync`, the item with all its parts), poses it, draws it, keeps the picture in memory and writes it to the cache. A later run reads the PNG and makes no model at all.
- **Its size**: so many cells, at 63 pixels a cell and one more (`ItemViewFactory.GetCellPixelSize`: a weapon of five cells by two is 316 by 127). It is drawn at **twice that** with eight samples to a pixel (`IconCreatorBase`: a render texture of double size, anti-aliasing 8), on a clear background, and halved into the picture.
- **The pose is the prefab's own** (`PreviewPivot.Icon`, on the prefab's root object): a turn, a shift used only where `hasOffset` is set, and `boundsScale`. The model is made a child of the icon camera, given that turn **in the world** (its own turn is replaced), and moved so that the middle of its bounds is where the camera looks (`ItemIconCreator.CenterByBounds`). The camera is orthographic, its half height the bounds' half height over `boundsScale`, or, for a model wider than the picture, its half width over the picture's shape and `boundsScale`. A prefab with no `PreviewPivot` is drawn as it stands, the half height its bounds' (`PoseModelByBounds`).
- **The bounds** are those of every renderer that is on, but for lenses (`linza` in the name) and the muzzle's flash (`MuzzleJetCombinedMesh`): each renderer's own box in the world, all together (`WeaponPreview.GetBounds`).
- **For a weapon** the pose is on its `_container` object, and the weapon with every part hangs from it. In the install every weapon and magazine looked at has the same turn, a quarter and more about the upright (0, 0.8434, 0, -0.5373), and fills nine tenths of the picture; a grenade, the PACA have turns of their own.
- **The camera** (`IconCamera`): at (0, 0, -1) in its own space, not turned, orthographic, the picture cleared to nothing. **Its lights** are three of its own, directional, none casting a shadow, and the scene's ambient light is put to black while it draws (`IconRenderSettings`):

  | Light | Turn (x, y, z, w) | Colour | Intensity |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Key Light | 0.4387, 0.0302, 0.1424, 0.8868 | white | 1.24 |
  | Fill Light | 0.2375, -0.4461, 0.2678, 0.8203 | white | 0.39 |
  | Hair Light | -0.1759, -0.5585, -0.8010, 0.1251 | 0.515, 0.900, 1.0 | 0.91 |

- **A shadow under the model** (`IconShadow`, on the camera): the drawn model's own outline, in the shadow's colour, blurred (drawn at a quarter of the size and back eight times) and put behind the model. The camera's has the colour 10/255 grey, wholly opaque, and no offset: a soft dark rim all round.
- **The shaders** are swapped for each shader's `_Icon` kind while the picture is drawn (`ShaderReplacer`); those are not read here.
- **A thing turned in its grid** has its picture turned a quarter (`ItemViewFactory.VerticalRotation`, 270 degrees about the screen's axis).
- **A fold** (read 2026-10-17, night; `FoldableStockView`, `ObjectsFactory.CreateItem`): every model of an item made not animated (a picture, a thing on the ground) has its dresses set up with its own item (`IDress.Init(item, isAnimated: false)`), and a fold's view among them turns the bones it names, found by name breadth first below itself, to its folded or its unfolded turn by its own item's fold, at once (`BindableEvent.Bind` runs the turn as it binds) and whenever the fold changes; a picture is drawn afresh then (`RaiseRefreshEvent(refreshIcon)`). A view on a model whose item has no fold does nothing (the M3's sits on the weapon, whose fold is its stock's: the folded M3 is drawn as it lies, only to its folded size). A bone it names that is not on the model is passed over (the SCAR-L's names its stocks of both colours). In the install: **42 weapons have bones a fold turns**, by the weapon's own view or a folding stock's (the AKMS its stock's axis and pad; the TOZ-106's, T-5000's, SV-98's OV and TRG's stocks theirs); one view's unfolded turn is not how its model lies as made (the SR-3M's stock, 3.4 degrees), and the 9A-91's unfolded turn is written as all noughts.

### When a picture is asked for (read 2026-10-14, when the user asked: "can we also make icon loading lazy for first-time? Check how tarkov handles that when opening an invetnroy for the first time.")

- **When the screen first shows the item.** An item's view asks for its picture as the view is made and filled (`ItemView.UpdateStaticInfo`, `RefreshIcon`, `ItemViewFactory.LoadItemIcon`): nothing is asked for an item no screen has shown, and nothing at the raid's start.
- **Asked, the picture is there at once or later.** `GetItemIcon` hands back the picture's holder at once: filled from memory, else empty while the cached PNG is read (`LoadFromUserCacheAsync`), else empty while the model is made, posed and drawn (`FillIconWithNewSpriteAsync`: the item's bundles loaded, its loot model made, its pictures' sharpest level waited for, then drawn one model at a time, `_isIconCreating`), and the holder says when it is filled (`Changed`).
- **Until then the view shows its loader.** While the holder has no picture the view's `_iconLoader` is on and its picture is off (`ItemView.IconChangedHandler`); filled, the picture shows (if the item has been searched) and the loader goes.
- So **a first opening of a container full of new things draws them in play**, one after another, each after its model is loaded.
- **An opened container's panel makes views only for what is in sight** (read 2026-10-14, night, when the user asked: "See how tarkov handles icon loading. If they don't do anyhting special, can we make it so icons are only generated when the actual item appears/is called for?"). A grid can be "magnified" (`GridView.IsMagnified`, `MagnifyIfPossible`): its views are made only for the items in the part of it that its scroll area shows (`Grid.GetItemsInRect`), one a frame where few are there, and a view that leaves that part is killed; a `GridViewMagnifier` on the scroll area asks again whenever it scrolls. In the install the inventory screen's container panel (`InventoryScreen/Items Panel/Stash Panel/Simple Panel`, a `SimpleStashPanel` with a `SearchableItemView`) has one on its `Mask/Scroll Area`, and the view points to it (its fourth pointer, `_viewMagnifier`: `probe pointers level44 "Stash Panel/Simple Panel"`), so its grids are magnified; the same holds for the scav's inventory screen and the transfer screen. The panel of the worn containers (`Containers Scrollview`) has none: what is in them all has views. A picture asked for stays in memory for the rest of the run (`IconCreatorBase._memoryCacheIndex`, emptied only with the cache on disk): scrolled back, a thing has its picture at once. **A weapon's parts are not pictured on their own**: a weapon's picture is drawn from its whole model, the parts on it (`CreateCleanLootPrefabAsync`), which is what loading it reads, and a part has a picture only when it has a view of its own (in a grid, or a slot of a window that shows the weapon's parts).

## What is done here

- **A prefab's own picture** (0.104.2; `ItemIconCreator`: `sprite = component.Icon.overrideIcon ?? the model drawn`): where the prefab's `PreviewPivot.Icon.overrideIcon` names a sprite, that is the picture and the model is not drawn: an armour's built-in soft inserts, whose models (`armor_plates/soft_armor_front.bundle` and the rest) draw nothing. The sprite is cut from its texture in the bundle (`icon::override_icon`), put in the middle of a picture as large as the view's cells (63 a cell and one) at its own size, shrunk to fit where larger (`ItemView.SetNativeSize`, `UpdateScale`; nearest of its pixels, `PROVISIONAL`), and kept in the cache as a drawn one is. An empty prefab path gives the game's `What` (`ItemViewFactory.LoadItemIcon`: a red question mark, `rsprite What`); not built (what the app shows for one is its sort's plain shape).
- **The same model**: a weapon assembled with its parts as for the hands (`weapon::assemble`), hung from its container's turn; any other item's prefab from the object its `PreviewPivot` is on (`icon::model_of`). Only what is on is drawn, and only what is on, but lenses and the muzzle's flash, counts for the bounds.
- **A grenade and a melee weapon** are drawn from their prefab's tree as a weapon is (since 0.43.0): their container holds no model of its own, and the game draws the weapon prefab's `Hierarchy` for both (`ObjectsFactory.CreateItemAsync`: a `Weapon`, a `ThrowWeap` or anything with a `KnifeComponent`). Before, nothing of either was drawn ("nothing of it is drawn"). Seen whole (0.43.1, `local\shots\gcase_s88.png`, `gmelee_s88.png`): the thirteen grenades and twenty-four melee weapons of the range's `GRENADES` table.
- **A mesh that casts its shadow alone is not drawn** (0.143.1; `unity::extract::Renderer::shadows_only`, a renderer's `m_CastShadows` 3, `ShadowCastingMode.ShadowsOnly`): the engine draws no pixel of it, the repair kits' `shadow_LOD0` and `shadow_lod1` among them (a box round the kit in a plain material with no picture, which drawn covered the kit in white). It is left out of the picture and of its bounds; on the ground (`loose`) and in the hands (`weapon.rs`) it is drawn as its shadow alone (`shadow_only`). `PROVISIONAL`: whether `WeaponPreview.GetBounds` counts such a renderer was not read. The cache moved to `v5` with it.
- **Two kinds of mesh the reader had been leaving short** (0.43.1, `unity::extract::read_mesh`): a mesh on one bone keeps each vertex's bone and no weights (the weights add up to one, so each is all of it; the F-1's tripwire peg), and was drawn as it lay, at full size beside the grenade; a submesh of quads (`MeshTopology.Quads`, 2) was left out, which took the RGO's body, pin, ring and lever. Quads are now two triangles each on the quad's first corner.
- **A skinned mesh is drawn where its bones put it** (since 0.43.0, `render_data::pose_on_bones`), as the engine skins it, once, with the bones where the prefab has them: the F-1's tripwire kit hangs on bones its prefab shrinks to a hundredth, out of sight; drawn as it lay, it stood beside the grenade at full size. The same for a thing's model on the ground. The cache is `v3` since (a picture of `v2` may have had such a part).
- **The same camera, lights and framing**, in the engine's axes (z the other way), in a studio of its own the rest of the scene does not see and that sees nothing of it (a render layer of its own; the lights only light that layer; no ambient light). The intensities are the game's, scaled so that a white surface square to a light of intensity one is as bright as the game's: Bevy's diffuse light has a 1/pi the game's has not.
- **The same size and the same halving**, the same rim of shadow (blurred here three times at full size, which is close to the game's eight times at a quarter: `PROVISIONAL`), the same quarter turn for a turned thing.
- **The same keeping**: the picture is written to `local\icon_cache\` as a PNG, by the item's template and its size, and a later run reads it there. The game's own cache is no use here: it is under its own hashes, and on the user's machine it has no item in it (`docs/findings.md`).
- **Made away from the frame**: the model is read on a thread of its own (as a weapon's rig is: `weapon::Preparing`); the frame puts it in the studio, draws it once and reads it back. Until an item has a picture its box shows the plain shape of its sort it had before.
- **Not the game's**: the materials are the app's approximation of the game's shading (`render_data.rs`), not its `_Icon` shaders. A thing whose model is not the install's (a suit of the other game's, the StG 44) has the picture of the model it is drawn with (the StG 44's own parts on its donor's pose; a suit: its donor's, the PACA's). A part whose material cannot be read is left out of the picture (drawn, it is the stand-in's magenta: two specks on the Berkut). A skinned mesh is drawn in its own rest pose, as the game's loot model lies, without its bones.
- **An item of the other game's** (the user, 2026-10-14, evening: "for stalker items can we use the world model of the item? but the same icon setup"): a suit of S.T.A.L.K.E.R.'s is pictured from that game's own model of the item as it lies in the world (the item's `visual` in that game's configs, `dynamics\outfit\stalker_outfit` for the Sunrise and `scientific_outfit` for the SEVA; `world_visual` in its file here), read from Anomaly's packed archives (`docs/findings.md`) with its pictures from wherever they are found, loose or packed. The camera, its lights, the size, the framing by the bounds and the shadow are the game's, as for any item. **The turn is this work's** (`PROVISIONAL`): such a model lies flat, face up with its head towards minus z, and the game draws a model with no pose as it stands, which would be its edge; it is turned so the camera looks down on it with the head at the top (a half turn about the line between y and minus z: up to the camera, minus z up), at the bounds' own size (no `boundsScale`). Its material is its colour picture alone, as the suit is drawn worn. Without a model of its own, or one that cannot be read, a suit has its donor's picture, with a line saying why. The picture's name in the cache carries a checksum of the model's path (`<template>_<w>x<h>_<checksum>.png`; `_donor` for the donor's), so another model is another picture.
- **A switch** (the user, 2026-10-14, evening: "add icon generation to the switchable mechanics tab"): `ICON GENERATION` on the options tab (`settings.md`, "Features"). Off as a run begins, no picture is drawn or read, nothing waits for them, and every box has the plain shape of its sort. Restart required, as each switch is.
- **Asked for when the screen first shows the item**, as the game asks (above; since 2026-10-14, late, by the user's word: "can we also make icon loading lazy for first-time?"): what is worn and carried, and a container's contents when it is opened beside them; what lies in a container not yet opened, and on the ground, is not asked for. A picture in the cache is read away from the frame, as the game reads it; **until a box's picture is read or drawn, it shows a spinner, where the game's shows its loader** (the user, 2026-10-14, late: "Can we a add spinners when something is loading instead of the paper icon?"), and the picture is put in its place when it comes, the rest of the screen as it was; the plain shape of its sort is left for a thing whose picture cannot be made, and for every thing with `ICON GENERATION` off. Only what the screen shows is asked for: a thing and what lies in its grids, not a weapon's parts or a magazine's rounds. A drawn picture's finishing (the shadow's blur, the PNG, the turned copy) is done away from the frame too. The spinner's look (a ring a quarter open, fading to its tail, a turn in 0.9 s, 0.42 of the box's shorter side between 10 and 28 pixels) is the app's: the game's loader's picture is not read. **It turns inside its box, never the box itself** (since 0.106.0): every spinner of one picture is drawn by one material (`radial_fill`, its picture turned about the node's middle) whose angle is set once a frame. Bevy cuts a turned node at a clip's edge as if it were upright, which pulled a spinner out of shape at a scrolled panel's edge; 0.104.2 then held still any spinner whose turned corners passed its clip, and since an item's box clips what is in it, the game's loader never turned in a one-cell box (the user, 2026-10-23: "The spinner's aren't spinning now, i tested it on the medcase with no icon cache"). Upright, it is cut as any picture is. **The curtain no longer waits for them** (it had, from the evening of 2026-10-14 until then: 8.3 s on a first run, 2.9 to 3.5 s after). The first opening of a crate of new things draws them in play: with no cache, the crate of rifles' 83 pictures came in over some ten seconds, 56 of 2,260 frames over 16.7 ms and the worst 201 ms, each on the rendering side (the studio's first frame of a new model; the update under a millisecond); read from the cache, the opening frame takes 1.5 ms of update where reading in the frame had taken 41.7.
- **Asked for as the game's panel asks** (2026-10-14, night, 0.7.1): of the open container only the things in the rows its panel shows, asked again as it is scrolled (`inventory_view::LootInSight`, kept by `scroll_loot`); what is in the equipment's slots and in their grids; what is in the grids of a container opened in a window; nothing in a container that lies in another (that had been asked for: the vest's magazines in the crate) and nothing in a weapon's slots. The crate of rifles opened on an empty cache asks for 22 pictures where it had asked for all of its 83; scrolled down twelve rows, 14 more.
- **A picture whose reading back was dropped had stopped the studio** (found the same night, from the user's run: "the icon loading hangs"): the target a picture is drawn on had been made full of nothing, and so waited its turn behind every other picture going to the card (8 MB a frame, `main::UPLOAD_BUDGET`); with a rig's pictures going up at the same time it was not on the card when it was read back, the engine dropped the reading ("Unknown image for screenshot, skipping") and never answered, and every picture after it waited behind it for the rest of the run. The target is made with nothing to send now (`Image::new_uninit`), so it is on the card at once; a reading back not answered in 120 frames is asked again, three times, and then the picture is given up as an empty one is; and an answer to a reading asked before is never taken for the next picture's. With rigs made ahead meanwhile, the run that had drawn 2 pictures in 1,900 frames drew all 36 it asked for.
- **A picture read back before all of its model was on the graphics card was kept short of some parts** (found 2026-10-14, night, from the user's report: "some weapons sometimes fail to fully render their icons even after erasing the cache ... the ak-74 and the RPK-16 ... sv97, one other time it was the TX-15. Maybe this occurs if i scroll by too fast?"). The studio had waited 4 frames and one more for each 24 MB of the model's pictures, where the app sends 8 MB a frame (`main::UPLOAD_BUDGET`), counts the meshes besides, and shares that with every rig and picture going up; a part whose own meshes or pictures are not yet on the card is not drawn, and the picture, not being empty, was kept. Scrolling fast, with rigs made ahead under the mouse, made it likelier. **Now the studio waits for every mesh and picture of the model to be on the card** (`weapon::UploadWatch`, as a rig does), and reads it back 4 frames after that. Measured on an empty cache, the rifles' case scrolled through with a rig made ahead meanwhile: 26 of 38 models came on the card later than the old rule had waited (8 frames where it had waited 6, 13 where 6, 21 where 12), and all 38 pictures are whole (the AK-74 with its magazine, grip and stock). The cache's folder is `v2` since: a picture of `v1` may be short.
- **What a picture still waits on**: the studio reads a picture back only once the renderer has no pipeline left to build, anywhere (a part not yet drawable would be left out of it). A new kind of material takes the renderer 1 to 2.5 s to build on DirectX 12 (`TRP_ICON_TRACE`), and while it builds every picture waits.

- **A thing's parts and switches** (read 2026-10-24, from `IconsHash`, `ItemIconCreator.RenderModel`, `ObjectsFactory.CreateItemAsync`, `ToggleResult`, `Item.RaiseRefreshEvent`, `GridItemView`, `HelmetDress`). The picture's key in the game's cache is a hash of the thing and of every part below it, the thing's own slots and grids and theirs in turn, each by its template and where it lies, stopping at a thing that hides what is in it (`HideEntrails`); each thing's share has, besides its template, its switch where it has one (`TogglableComponent.On`), its fold, and a magazine's rounds that show. The model drawn is a copy of the thing with what it holds (`CloneVisibleItem`), made as any model of a thing is: every part in its slots hung on it (`AttachMods`, as a worn thing's: body animator spec, "A worn thing's parts and a helmet's hinge"; any thing with slots, not only a weapon) and the dresses of each model made set up with that model's own item, not animated: so a helmet's hinge stands at once where its face shield's switch puts it (`HelmetDress.Init`, its filter set at once). **What asks again**: a switch flipped refreshes the picture of the thing switched and of each thing it is fitted in, up through the slots (`ToggleResult.RaiseEvents`, `RaiseRefreshEvent(refreshIcon: true)`); a thing put into or taken out of a place below a box's thing refreshes that box's picture (`GridItemView.OnItemAdded`, `OnItemRemoved`, `CheckSlot`). A refreshed picture is looked for by its new key: in memory, the cache, or drawn.

## Built (2026-10-14)

- `crates/trp-data/src/icon.rs`: the pose (`Pose`, `pose_in`), an item's model and pose from its prefab (`model_of`), the icon camera read from `resources.assets` by the engine's own layouts (`Studio::read`; `Studio::as_read` is what it read, for an install where it cannot be), the finishing (`finish`: the shadow and the halving), the quarter turn (`turned`), PNGs (`save_png`, `load_png`).
- `crates/trp-app/src/icons.rs`: every item the screen shows asked for once by template and size, when it first shows it (`ask_icons`); the cache read, or the model made ready on the icon thread (`prepare_icons`); the studio, its camera and three lights on a render layer of their own, one model at a time, read back by the engine's screenshot of an image (`draw_icons`, `icon_captured`).
- `inventory_view.rs`: `Carried::pictures` by template, drawn before the sort's plain shape and filling the box (`Picture::rendered`).
- The evening of 2026-10-14: `icons::Worker::prepare_stalker` (a suit's model of the item on one node, turned by `LAID_FACE_UP`), `icons::signatures` (the cache's names), `Icons::on` (the switch); `PreparedLooks::stalker_material`; `outfit::Definition::world_visual`; `trp_data::xray_archive` (the other game's archives).
- `probe icon <item>` (an item's pose and the object it is on), `probe studio` (the icon camera as read), `probe resources <word>` (game objects of `resources.assets` by name, read by hand).

**A folded thing's picture** (2026-10-17, night; 0.47.2; `trp_data::weapon::fold_view`, `Weapon::fold_bones`, `fold_tree`, `icons::picture_key`): the picture is asked, kept and found by the thing's template marked `#folded` where its fold is folded, and drawn to its folded size with the fold's bones turned folded; an unfolded weapon's with them turned unfolded. A folding weapon's pictures carry the signature `fold` in the cache (drawn afresh once: the SR-3M's unfolded one had been drawn with its stock as made). `PROVISIONAL`: a turn of all noughts (the 9A-91's unfolded) taken as none. **Seen** (`local\shots\fold_mk16_zoom_s89.png`): the Mk 16 folded in the crate of rifles, its picture with the stock against the receiver, 5 cells for its 6 unfolded. **Tested** (`every_weapon_that_folds_has_its_fold_bones`): the 42 weapons' bones found, the AKMS's two, and only the SR-3M's unfolded turn off the model. **Not built**: a folded thing on the ground drawn folded (`loose`: as made).

**A thing's parts and switches** (2026-10-24, 0.118.0; `icons::picture_key`, `parts_key`, `switched_on`, `Worker::fit_parts`): a thing other than a weapon with something in its slots is asked for by those parts, named as a weapon's build is (`trp_data::weapon::build_key`: `helmet[mod_equipment=shield[]]`), and any thing by the places of its switches that are on (`#on:` and each place, its slots from the thing down joined by `/`, the thing itself `.`); the icon thread hangs the parts on the model by the worn thing's rule (`trp_data::dress::head_model`), shows those fitted, and turns each hinge at once by its face shield's switch. A new key is asked for as the screen next looks (`ask_icons`), so a flip, a fit and a removal each show their own picture, as the game's refresh does. **Departs in the key only** (nothing drawn differs): what lies in a thing's grids is left out of it (the game's hash has it, which redraws a backpack's same picture each time something goes in), and so is a fold or a magazine's rounds of a part. **Tested** (`a_helmet_s_picture_follows_its_shield`): a helmet bare, its shield fitted, and flipped down, three keys, each taken apart again into its template, parts and switches. **Seen** (`local\shots\helm_fit_menu_s126.png`, `helm_flip_s126.png` before: the ZSh-1-2M's picture bare after fitting and after FLIP DOWN; `helm_fit2_s126.png`, `helm_flip2_s126.png` after: the shield up over the brim, then down over the face). **Not built**: a thing's model on the ground with its parts (`loose`, still by `rig_key`: a helmet with its shield lies bare). **Not compared** with the game's own pictures.
