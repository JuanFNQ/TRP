# The inventory's pictures: an item's own model, drawn once and kept

How the game makes the picture of an item in its inventory, read from its code on 2026-10-14 (`IconCreatorBase`, `ItemIconCreator`, `ItemIconCache`, `ItemViewFactory`, `PreviewPivot`, `IconShadow`, `ShaderReplacer`, `WeaponPreview.GetBounds`) and from its files (each item's prefab; the `IconCamera` prefab in `EscapeFromTarkov_Data\resources.assets`). TRP copies what Tarkov does. What is built, and what was compared with what, is at the end.

## What the game does

- **The picture is the item's own model, drawn once and kept.** Asked for an item's picture (`ItemIconCreator.GetItemIcon`), the game looks for it in memory, then in a cache on disk (`Icon Cache` in the game's temporary folder: an `index.json` from a hash of the item and its parts to a numbered PNG), and only then draws it: it makes the item's model as it would lie on the ground (`CreateCleanLootPrefabAsync`, the item with all its parts), poses it, draws it, keeps the picture in memory and writes it to the cache. A later run reads the PNG and makes no model at all.
- **Its size**: so many cells, at 63 pixels a cell and one more (`ItemViewFactory.GetCellPixelSize`: a weapon of five cells by two is 316 by 127). It is drawn at **twice that** with eight samples to a pixel (`IconCreatorBase`: a render texture of double size, anti-aliasing 8), on a clear background, and halved into the picture.
- **The pose is the prefab's own** (`PreviewPivot.Icon`, on the prefab's root object): a turn, a shift used only where `hasOffset` is set, and `boundsScale`. The model is made a child of the icon camera, given that turn **in the world** (its own turn is replaced), and moved so that the middle of its bounds is where the camera looks (`ItemIconCreator.CenterByBounds`). The camera is orthographic, its half height the bounds' half height over `boundsScale`, or, for a model wider than the picture, its half width over the picture's shape and `boundsScale`. A prefab with no `PreviewPivot` is drawn as it stands, the half height its bounds' (`PoseModelByBounds`).
- **The bounds** are those of every renderer that is on, but for lenses (`linza` in the name) and the muzzle's flash (`MuzzleJetCombinedMesh`): each renderer's own box in the world, all together (`WeaponPreview.GetBounds`).
- **For a weapon** the pose is on its `_container` object, and the weapon with every part hangs from it. In the install every weapon and magazine looked at has the same turn, a quarter and more about the upright (0, 0.8434, 0, -0.5373), and fills nine tenths of the picture; a grenade, the PACA have turns of their own.
- **A weapon's container, whatever the item** (read 2026-11-02: the BTC had no icon, and other items may have lacked one). The model is made from a pool of the item's prefab (`ObjectsFactory.CreateItemAsync`, `PopOrCreate`); the pool takes the prefab's own `AssetPoolObject` component (`AbstractAssetsPool.RunInitialStageBody`, `GetComponent`), and where that is a `WeaponPrefab` (or its two kinds, `UsableHandsPrefab` and `GrenadePrefab`, the only classes made from it) its own setting up copies the object it names (`_weaponObject`) and hangs it under the container, unturned at its middle (`OnCreatePoolRoleModel`, `CacheInternalObjects`, `CreateObjectInstance`). So it is the component, not the item's class, that decides: the pool's kind goes by the template (`ItemTemplates.GetResourceType`: a weapon's, a grenade's, a knife's and a launcher's are `Weapon`, a thing whose `Prefab` is its `UsePrefab` `UsablePrefab`), but the rangefinder's and the radio transmitter's prefabs, pooled as plain items (no item of the install has its `Prefab` for its `UsePrefab`), carry a `WeaponPrefab` too, and `CreateItemAsync` takes their `Hierarchy` as a weapon's. The under-barrel launchers (`Launcher`, a gear mod: the GP-25, the GP-34, the M203) and the radio transmitter's two templates are such containers, with nothing drawn of their own.
- **A model with nothing to draw** (read 2026-11-02): with no renderer switched on, the bounds are of no size (`WeaponPreview.GetBounds` returns `default(Bounds)`), the camera's half height is nought (`CenterByBounds`), and nothing is drawn; the empty picture is made into a sprite, kept in the cache and shown (`FillIconWithNewSpriteAsync`: only a sprite of none is "Something went wrong"). In the install: the TKPD muzzle (one object, no renderer; in no build of the install), the zombies' hands (`weapon_InfectionArms`), the Utes' belt (`mag_all_empty_mesh`), a suitcase of the levels.
- **A turn of no length** (2026-11-02): the 0.2 BTC's pivot saves its turn as 0, 0, 0, 0, a quaternion never set. The engine's transform takes it as no turn (`PROVISIONAL`: Unity's own normalising, `Quaternion.Normalize` in its published C#, gives the identity under its epsilon; the transform's setter, native, is not read): the coin is drawn as its mesh lies, face on to the camera, which is the game's picture of it.
- **The camera** (`IconCamera`): at (0, 0, -1) in its own space, not turned, orthographic, the picture cleared to nothing. **Its lights** are three of its own, directional, none casting a shadow, and the scene's ambient light is put to black while it draws (`IconRenderSettings`):

  | Light | Turn (x, y, z, w) | Colour | Intensity |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Key Light | 0.4387, 0.0302, 0.1424, 0.8868 | white | 1.24 |
  | Fill Light | 0.2375, -0.4461, 0.2678, 0.8203 | white | 0.39 |
  | Hair Light | -0.1759, -0.5585, -0.8010, 0.1251 | 0.515, 0.900, 1.0 | 0.91 |

- **A shadow under the model** (`IconShadow`, on the camera): the drawn model's own outline, in the shadow's colour, blurred (drawn at a quarter of the size and back eight times) and put behind the model. The camera's has the colour 10/255 grey, wholly opaque, and no offset: a soft dark rim all round.
- **The shaders** are swapped for each shader's `_Icon` kind while the picture is drawn (`ShaderReplacer`; the eight twins: `p0/Reflective/Bumped Specular SMap_Icon`, which stands for any shader with none, `p0/Bumped Specular_Icon`, `p0/Reflective/Bumped Specular_Icon`, `p0/Cutout/Bumped Diffuse_Icon`, `Transparent/DepthZwrite_Icon`, `Transparent/DepthZwriteDithered_Icon`, `Custom/Matcap_Icon`, `VFX/Hologram_Icon`). What the first does is the next section; the others are not read.

### The game's surface in a picture (read and built 2026-11-04)

With the game's inventory beside the app's, most icons did not match: their lighting differed, in the icons and on the inspect screen, the Salewa very dull in the app and very bright in SPT. Read from the icon camera's prefab and the shader's programs (`local\icon_shader_notes_s209.md`; the programs, their parameters and parsed form in `local\icon_shader_s209\`):

- **The camera renders forward** (`IconCamera`'s rendering path set on it), HDR off, into a target of eight bits a channel and no sRGB (a gamma project), eight samples a pixel, cleared to nothing; its three lights are Auto. **All three are lit per pixel** where the game's shadows quality allows three pixel lights or more (`QualitySettings.pixelLightCount` from it: low 2, medium 5, high 8, ultra 10; the recorded `Graphics.ini` has medium): the key in the base pass, the others each in an added pass (blended one to one). At low the fill would go into the harmonics (`PROVISIONAL`: the engine's rule for which light is dropped, not read). `IconRenderSettings` puts the ambient flat and black and the fog off; nothing else.
- **What `p0/Reflective/Bumped Specular SMap_Icon` makes of a pixel**, every figure as stored (no conversion to linear anywhere): with M the colour picture's texel, Sg the gloss picture's red, n the normal (the normal map's X its red times its alpha, Y its green, Z rebuilt, sampled at the UV times `_BumpTiling`, taken to the world), V the way from the pixel to the camera's place (1 m before the model's middle, not the orthographic view's way), c = V·n:
  - a rim, f = (1 − c)²: the colour's scale d = `_DefVals`.x + `_DefVals`.y·f, the specular's s = `_SpecVals`.x + `_SpecVals`.y·f;
  - the colour A = M.rgb × `_Color` (drawn toward `_BaseTintColor` by `_TintMask`, black and so nothing unless a material has one) × d;
  - each light, C its colour times its intensity and L the way to it, H halfway between V and L: A·C·max(0, n·L) + C·`_SpecColor`·max(0, n·H)^(128·Sg·`_Specularness`)·M.a·`_Glossness`·s. The engine's old Blinn-Phong: no Fresnel, no 1/π, no n·L on the specular; the colour picture's alpha masks the specular;
  - in the base pass, the material's cube (`_Cube`, by the reflection of V about n) × M.a × `_ReflectColor` × s; where a material names no cube the engine binds its default, a flat grey of 0x80 (`UnityDefaultCube`, read in the engine with Ghidra);
  - the sum held to one, alpha one.
- **The two figures' names are crossed** in the shader's own words: `_Specularness` scales the exponent (the gloss picture), `_Glossness` the specular (the colour's alpha), as the registers have them. **Not read in a picture**: `_NdotLOffset`, `_Fresnel`, `_Shininess` (this shader has none of them). The shader's defaults, for a material that saves none: `_Color` and `_BaseTintColor` white, `_SpecColor` 0.5, `_Glossness` 1, `_Specularness` 0.078125, `_ReflectColor` white, `_SpecVals` (1.1, 2), `_DefVals` (0.5, 0.7), `_BumpTiling` 1, `_SpecMap` white.
- **Worked by hand** for the Salewa's cloth (its material's `_DefVals` and `_SpecVals` both (1, 1), `_Glossness` 1.44, `_Specularness` 1.05, `_SpecColor` 0.64, `_ReflectColor` 0.71, its cube `patron_cubemap_metall_matte`): facing the camera (72, 28, 28) in 255, where the game's own picture of it (`174.png` of its cache) has (70.5, 29.5, 27.9).
- **The dithered glass's own twin** (read 2026-11-08 from the install's programs of `Transparent/DepthZwriteDithered_Icon`, `local\glass_icon_brief_s240.md`; checked again against the programs while building). The swap that gives every material its twin (`ShaderReplacer.Replace`, the first thing `IconCreatorBase.CaptureSpriteOfModel` does, which `ItemIconCreator` calls for every picture; undone after) takes each enabled renderer's material whose shader is not `Diffuse`, not `Standard` and not already a twin, to the shader of its name with `_Icon` added, the `p0` twin where none such is registered; the install's `shaders` bundle holds eight twins, the dithered glass's among them, so a window magazine's and a face shield's glass are drawn with their own. It is drawn after what is opaque, every pass culling back faces whatever the material's `_Cull`, and no `_OpacityScale` is read. Pass 0 writes the vertex colour's alpha (held to nought and one) into the picture's alpha alone, its depth tested and written. Pass 1, for the base light, blends by its own alpha (source alpha and one less it) and writes the colour alone, its depth not written; pass 2 adds each other light, times its alpha. With c the colour picture's texel times `_Color` (alpha too), s the specular picture's texel, k its red times `_SpecPower`, g its alpha times `_Glossness`, n the bumped normal, V the way to the eye (1 m before the model, as the `p0` twin's), R the reflection of V about n in the world, C a light's colour times its intensity, L the way to it and H halfway between V and L: a light's part is c.rgb·C·max(0, n·L) + C·`_SpecColor`·max(0, n·H)^(128k)·g; the base pass adds the reflection, 2.5 times the cube at R times `_ReflectColor`, plus s's red times the mix from white to c.rgb by c.a times a fixed blue of the program's (0.254, 0.310, 0.388), and the ambient times c.rgb (black in a picture); each pass's alpha is c.a. A tinted glass (`_Tinted` over a half) would take 0.05 of the specular and the reflection (none is). So where the glass is in front, the picture is c.a times the glass's own light plus one less c.a times what the opaque things drew behind it, and its alpha the vertex colour's. The PMAG GEN M3 Window's glass takes its body's own colour picture, whose alpha is 0 to 107 (at most 0.42): in its picture its rounds show through; the ZSh-1-2M's shield's colour alpha averages 0.57.
- **A thing turned in its grid** has its picture turned a quarter (`ItemViewFactory.VerticalRotation`, 270 degrees about the screen's axis).
- **A fold** (read 2026-10-17, night; `FoldableStockView`, `ObjectsFactory.CreateItem`): every model of an item made not animated (a picture, a thing on the ground) has its dresses set up with its own item (`IDress.Init(item, isAnimated: false)`), and a fold's view among them turns the bones it names, found by name breadth first below itself, to its folded or its unfolded turn by its own item's fold, at once (`BindableEvent.Bind` runs the turn as it binds) and whenever the fold changes; a picture is drawn afresh then (`RaiseRefreshEvent(refreshIcon)`). A view on a model whose item has no fold does nothing (the M3's sits on the weapon, whose fold is its stock's: the folded M3 is drawn as it lies, only to its folded size). A bone it names that is not on the model is passed over (the SCAR-L's names its stocks of both colours). In the install: **42 weapons have bones a fold turns**, by the weapon's own view or a folding stock's (the AKMS its stock's axis and pad; the TOZ-106's, T-5000's, SV-98's OV and TRG's stocks theirs); one view's unfolded turn is not how its model lies as made (the SR-3M's stock, 3.4 degrees), and the 9A-91's unfolded turn is written as all noughts.

### When a picture is asked for (read 2026-10-14: how Tarkov loads icons when an inventory is first opened, for lazy loading)

- **When the screen first shows the item.** An item's view asks for its picture as the view is made and filled (`ItemView.UpdateStaticInfo`, `RefreshIcon`, `ItemViewFactory.LoadItemIcon`): nothing is asked for an item no screen has shown, and nothing at the raid's start.
- **Asked, the picture is there at once or later.** `GetItemIcon` hands back the picture's holder at once: filled from memory, else empty while the cached PNG is read (`LoadFromUserCacheAsync`), else empty while the model is made, posed and drawn (`FillIconWithNewSpriteAsync`: the item's bundles loaded, its loot model made, its pictures' sharpest level waited for, then drawn one model at a time, `_isIconCreating`), and the holder says when it is filled (`Changed`).
- **Until then the view shows its loader.** While the holder has no picture the view's `_iconLoader` is on and its picture is off (`ItemView.IconChangedHandler`); filled, the picture shows (if the item has been searched) and the loader goes. The loader is the prefab's `Small Loader` (22 units; `Loader`, 44, is the drain's: 0.162.0). **Both draw `spinner_big`, packed trimmed** (`probe sprite resources.assets 3389`: its cut 42.97 pixels square at (0, 2.03) of its whole 45 by 45): the engine draws the cut in its place within the object's square and turns the square about its middle, the ring's. Drawn over the whole square, the app's turned about a point some 1.5 texels off the ring's middle, the ring orbiting it; since 0.162.1 the cut lies in its share of the square (`RadialFill`'s `covers`), and the edit build screen's loader, turned as a piece placed at its cut, is moved by as much as turning about the square's middle moves it (`edit_build`). **The picture takes the loader's place in the view's order**: both are the view's own objects, switched on and off, never moved (`IconChangedHandler`), so a picture that comes late is drawn where the view always draws it: over a thing not known's noise and colour panel (`NotExaminedBackground`, `Color Panel`), under its words. The app spawns the picture when it comes, in its loader's place among the view's children (`inventory_view::fill_pictures`); until 0.185.1 it went first among them, under the noise and the panel, and a thing not known whose picture was drawn afresh showed light grey, its black picture seen through them, until a move built its view anew.
- So **a first opening of a container full of new things draws them in play**, one after another, each after its model is loaded.
- **An opened container's panel makes views only for what is in sight** (read 2026-10-14, night: how Tarkov loads icons, so that one is made only when its item appears). A grid can be "magnified" (`GridView.IsMagnified`, `MagnifyIfPossible`): its views are made only for the items in the part of it that its scroll area shows (`Grid.GetItemsInRect`), one a frame where few are there, and a view that leaves that part is killed; a `GridViewMagnifier` on the scroll area asks again whenever it scrolls. In the install the inventory screen's container panel (`InventoryScreen/Items Panel/Stash Panel/Simple Panel`, a `SimpleStashPanel` with a `SearchableItemView`) has one on its `Mask/Scroll Area`, and the view points to it (its fourth pointer, `_viewMagnifier`: `probe pointers level44 "Stash Panel/Simple Panel"`), so its grids are magnified; the same holds for the scav's inventory screen and the transfer screen. The panel of the worn containers (`Containers Scrollview`) has none: what is in them all has views. **The part shown is rounded to whole cells** (`MagnifyIfPossible`: the scroll area's rectangle in the grid's units, its foot and its height each divided by 63 and rounded, `Mathf.RoundToInt`), and a thing that takes none of those rows has no view at all, however much of it the mask would show: one more than half out of the area is not drawn. Only the first grid is given the magnifier (`SearchableItemView.Show`: `InitGridView(GridViews[0])`). Built 0.160.4 (`inventory_view::magnified_rows`): the app draws every view and hides those out of the rounded rows of the first grid as it scrolls; its pictures are asked for by the same rows (`LootInSight`, floored and ceiled until then). A picture asked for stays in memory for the rest of the run (`IconCreatorBase._memoryCacheIndex`, emptied only with the cache on disk): scrolled back, a thing has its picture at once. **A weapon's parts are not pictured on their own**: a weapon's picture is drawn from its whole model, the parts on it (`CreateCleanLootPrefabAsync`), which is what loading it reads, and a part has a picture only when it has a view of its own (in a grid, or a slot of a window that shows the weapon's parts).

## What is done here

- **A prefab's own picture** (`ItemIconCreator`: `sprite = component.Icon.overrideIcon ?? the model drawn`): where the prefab's `PreviewPivot.Icon.overrideIcon` names a sprite, that is the picture and the model is not drawn: an armour's built-in soft inserts, whose models (`armor_plates/soft_armor_front.bundle` and the rest) draw nothing. The sprite is cut from its texture in the bundle (`icon::override_icon`), put in the middle of a picture as large as the view's cells (63 a cell and one) at its own size, shrunk to fit where larger (`ItemView.SetNativeSize`, `UpdateScale`; nearest of its pixels, `PROVISIONAL`), and kept in the cache as a drawn one is. An empty prefab path gives the game's `What` (`ItemViewFactory.LoadItemIcon`: a red question mark, `rsprite What`); not built (what the app shows for one is its sort's plain shape).
- **The same model**: a weapon assembled with its parts as for the hands (`weapon::assemble`), hung from its container's turn; any other item's prefab from the object its `PreviewPivot` is on (`icon::model_of`). Only what is on is drawn, and only what is on, but lenses and the muzzle's flash, counts for the bounds.
- **A grenade and a melee weapon** are drawn from their prefab's tree as a weapon is: their container holds no model of its own, and the game draws the weapon prefab's `Hierarchy` for both (`ObjectsFactory.CreateItemAsync`: a `Weapon`, a `ThrowWeap` or anything with a `KnifeComponent`). Before, nothing of either was drawn ("nothing of it is drawn"). Seen whole (`local\shots\gcase_s88.png`, `gmelee_s88.png`): the thirteen grenades and twenty-four melee weapons of the range's `GRENADES` table.
- **Any item whose prefab is a weapon's container** is drawn so (2026-11-02; `trp_data::weapon::is_weapon_container`, used by `icons::Worker::model_switched`, so by the picture and the model on the ground alike): a `WeaponPrefab`, `UsableHandsPrefab` or `GrenadePrefab` on the prefab, as the game's pool decides (above, "A weapon's container, whatever the item"). It had gone by the item's class (a grenade, a knife, the rangefinder), which left the three under-barrel launchers and the radio transmitter's two templates with "nothing of it is drawn". No item drawn before is drawn otherwise (the sweep below: the five changed are those five).
- **A model with no renderer switched on is an empty picture** (2026-11-02; `Worker::prepare`, `framed`): of its size, kept in the cache and shown, as the game's (above). A renderer switched on with nothing to bound (a mesh the app could not read) is still the app's failure, said in the log and not kept.
- **A weapon with no stock build** (2026-11-02; the RSP-30s, the SP-81, the AGS, the Utes, the PKTM, the launchers' own `_settings` weapons): its stock key is the weapon alone (`trp_sim::inventory::Kind::stock_key`, `trp_data::weapon::alone`), so bare it is asked for by its template, and with parts by its build; by its template it is assembled with nothing in its slots (`weapon::assemble_key`), as the game makes any model of what the item's slots hold. It had failed with "no default preset", in the picture and in the hands.
- **What lies in a container's special slots is asked for** (2026-11-02; `icons::ask_icons`, `in_special_slots`): every slot of the four containers but an armour's, shown beside their grids (the pockets' three), and those of a container open in a window and of a body's containers, as the game makes a view of each (`SearchableSlotView.CreateSlots`). It had not been, and what lay there kept its spinner (an RSP-30 in the pockets' first, seen).
- **A mesh that casts its shadow alone is not drawn** (`unity::extract::Renderer::shadows_only`, a renderer's `m_CastShadows` 3, `ShadowCastingMode.ShadowsOnly`): the engine draws no pixel of it, the repair kits' `shadow_LOD0` and `shadow_lod1` among them (a box round the kit in a plain material with no picture, which drawn covered the kit in white). It is left out of the picture; on the ground (`loose`) and in the hands (`weapon`) it is drawn as its shadow alone (`shadow_only`). The cache moved to `v5` with it. It counts for the bounds since 0.160.0 (below).
- **The bounds are each renderer's whole box** (read 2026-10-31 in `WeaponPreview.GetBounds`, `ItemIconCreator.CenterByBounds`): the game takes every `Renderer` component on an object that is on (`GetComponentsInChildren<Renderer>(includeInactive: false)`) and its `bounds`, which is the renderer's whole mesh's box (`m_LocalAABB`, every submesh together) turned into the world; one switched off has a box of no size, which it leaves out (`b.extents != Vector3.zero`). So a renderer that is on counts whether it draws here or not: one that casts its shadow alone (a backpack's `_SHADOW`), and a lower level of an LOD group (`AR_bagarii_lod1`, kept on the hierarchy's node for it: `unity::extract::Node::bounded`). Until then the app boxed each submesh on its own, whose union turned is tighter than the whole box turned: the Bagariy (4 by 4 cells, its pose's `boundsScale` 1.243) was framed 0.62 m across where the game frames 0.75, drawn a fifth too large and cut by its square. `probe iconbounds <template>` prints an item's renderers, their boxes in the studio and the camera's half height by the game's rule. The cache moved to `v7` with it. A skinned mesh's box is still its posed vertices' (`PROVISIONAL`: the game's is the renderer's own `m_AABB` about its root bone; none of the items compared draws one).
- **Two kinds of mesh the reader had been leaving short** (`unity::extract::read_mesh`): a mesh on one bone keeps each vertex's bone and no weights (the weights add up to one, so each is all of it; the F-1's tripwire peg), and was drawn as it lay, at full size beside the grenade; a submesh of quads (`MeshTopology.Quads`, 2) was left out, which took the RGO's body, pin, ring and lever. Quads are now two triangles each on the quad's first corner.
- **A skinned mesh is drawn where its bones put it** (`render_data::pose_on_bones`), as the engine skins it, once, with the bones where the prefab has them: the F-1's tripwire kit hangs on bones its prefab shrinks to a hundredth, out of sight; drawn as it lay, it stood beside the grenade at full size. The same for a thing's model on the ground. The cache is `v3` since (a picture of `v2` may have had such a part).
- **The same camera, lights and framing**, in the engine's axes (z the other way), in a studio of its own the rest of the scene does not see and that sees nothing of it (a render layer of its own; the lights only light that layer; no ambient light). The intensities are the game's, scaled so that a white surface square to a light of intensity one is as bright as the game's: Bevy's diffuse light has a 1/pi the game's has not.
- **The game's surface** (`game_surface`, `game_surface.wgsl`, "The game's surface in a picture" above): every material of the install is drawn by the `_Icon` twin's sums, in the game's numbers, under the studio's three lights handed to it as the game's figures (colour times intensity, the way to each) and the eye 1 m before the model's middle; the standard material is kept for its pictures and its normal map. The colour pictures are read as stored (`render_data::Role::RawColor`), so that they are filtered in the game's figures; the gloss picture's red is one less its roughness; each material's cube is read whole from the `cubemaps` bundle, which the icon thread opens (`icons::reader`). The camera draws in half floats with no tonemap into a picture not sRGB, so its samples are averaged in the game's numbers as the game's eight-bit target averages them. A material of the app's own (a weapon of one's own's, the other game's suits) keeps the standard surface's light, handed on as the figures an sRGB picture would have kept. Until then every picture was Bevy's surface in linear light: its 0.04 specular, a Fresnel under the hair light, greyed and paled every one (the Salewa's cloth (71, 47, 46) where the game's is (70.5, 29.5, 27.9); a cyan rim along its foot). The cache moved to `v10`. **Not built**: the other six twins, `_TintMask`, `_BumpTiling` other than one, the fill light in the harmonics at low shadows, eight samples a pixel (four here).
- **The dithered glass's twin** (with `SEE-THROUGH GLASS`; `game_surface`'s `glass` and `cover`, `icons`; "The dithered glass's own twin" above): a material noted as the glass (`glass_view::glass_note`) is drawn with its twin's base pass and other lights in one draw, blended by its alpha over what the opaque things drew, the colour alone written, each pass's sum held to one as the eight-bit target holds each pass, the base pass's light the studio's brightest; its mesh is drawn a second time after the opaque things, writing its vertex colour's alpha (one where it has none) into the alpha alone. With the switch the cache moved to `v12`; without it the pictures are `v11`'s and stay there (the ZSh-1-2M's with its shield down, drawn again off, the same as the kept one to the pixel). `PROVISIONAL`: a mesh with no vertex colours (the window magazine's) writes an alpha of one, the engine's default for a missing colour taken as white; each pass held to one where the blend itself is in half floats; two of the glass's faces in front of one another both blended (Bevy's blended pass writes no depth, where the game's pass 0 leaves only the front one to pass 1); the base light the brightest. **Seen** (`local\shots\glass_icon_mag_r30_cmp_s246.png`, `glass_icon_mag_empty_cmp_s246.png`, `glass_icon_zsh_cmp_s246.png`, each `v11` beside `v12`, four times): the loaded GEN M3 Window's rounds seen through its window (before, an opaque strip), the empty one's inside dark through it, the ZSh-1-2M's shield half clear with the helmet's inside behind it (before, a shiny opaque plate). Not compared with the game: neither picture is in its cache here (the verification log, item 109).
- **The same size and the same halving**, the same rim of shadow (blurred here three times at full size, which is close to the game's eight times at a quarter: `PROVISIONAL`), the same quarter turn for a turned thing.
- **The same keeping**: the picture is written to `local\icon_cache\` as a PNG, by the item's template and its size, and a later run reads it there. The game's own cache is no use here: it is under its own hashes, and on the machine read it has no item in it.
- **Made away from the frame**: the model is read on a thread of its own (as a weapon's rig is: `weapon::Preparing`); the frame puts it in the studio, draws it once and reads it back. - **A mirrored object** (built 2026-11-07: the Vltor CASV KeyMod 6-inch rail had been drawn wrong).
