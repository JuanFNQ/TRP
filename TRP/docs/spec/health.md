# Health

The character's health: its body parts and their hit points, energy and hydration, the effects that hurt it, heal it or change what it can do (bleeding, fractures, pain, tremor, painkillers, hunger and thirst, medicine at work), death, falls, barbed wire, and what each of those does to movement, stamina and the weapon. Written from a reading of the game (0.16.9.5, SPT 4.1.6 assembly) on 2026-10-14, night, in my own words: `PlayerHealthController`, `ActiveHealthController` and `BaseHealthController` with their effects, `HealthValue`, `DamageHelper`, `EffectsSettings`, `Player`'s handlers of health, `MovementContext`, `Physical`, `ProceduralWeaponAnimation.PhysicalConditionUpdated`, `BreathEffector`, `BarbedWire`, `DamageTrigger`, `BodyPartCollider`, `Player.MedsController`, `InventoryScreenHealthPanel`. Figures marked "here" are this install's (`globals.json`, `config.Health` unless another key is named). Class and field names are the game's.

## Whose it is

Every character of a raid is a `Player`, and in this install each is made by `LocalGame` as a `LocalPlayer`, which gives it a **`PlayerHealthController`** (`LocalPlayer.Create`): the one-player kind of `ActiveHealthController`, which does the work. The other controllers are not a raid's: `OfflineHealthController` is the menus' between raids (made by `MainMenuShowOperation` and `SessionResultShowOperation`), `ClientPlayerHealthController` an online client's, `ObservedPlayerHealthController` another player seen over the network.

A `PlayerHealthController` made for the one at the keys (not a bot):

- is filled from the profile's health (each part's current and maximum, energy, hydration, temperature, poison, and the lasting effects the profile lists, each made again with the time it had left);
- gets the **existence** effect on the head at once (hunger and thirst; below);
- is given the profile settings' starting buff if it names one (`ProfileHealthSettings.DefaultStimulatorBuff`: none here).

A bot's is started at once and gets neither.

## The body

Seven parts (`EBodyPart`): head, chest, stomach, left arm, right arm, left leg, right leg; and **common**, which is no part but the sum of all seven (each part's current and maximum added; `GetBodyPartHealth(Common)`). Each part has a value with a least and a most (`HealthValue`: 0 and its maximum) and a flag, **destroyed**.

The maxima here (`ProfileHealthSettings.BodyPartsSettings`, each `Default` and `Maximum` alike): head 35, chest 85, stomach 70, each arm 60, each leg 65; **440 in all**. Each part has two more figures there: what environmental damage is multiplied by on it (`EnvironmentDamageMultiplier`: head 0.125, chest 0.25, stomach 0.17, arms and legs 0.5), and what share of over-damage it takes (`OverDamageReceivedMultiplier`: 0.7 for all).

A change of a part's value is clamped to its least and most; a fall in it is first multiplied by the part's `DownMult` (the controller's damage coefficient, 1 in a raid). A change to a destroyed part does nothing, and nothing changes once the character is dead.

## Energy, hydration, temperature, poison

Four values of the same kind (`ProfileHealthSettings.HealthFactorsSettings`): **energy** 0 to 100 and **hydration** 0 to 100, both starting at 100 here; temperature 28 to 40 (36.6); poison 0 to 100 (0). Energy at its least starts **exhaustion**, hydration at its least **dehydration** (below), unless metabolism has been turned off (`DisableMetabolism`, which nothing in a raid of this install calls for the player).

The stamina reads two of them (`stamina.md`, "Capacity and refill"): the refills by how fed the character is, the sprint's and the jump's costs and the refills by how watered, and the oxygen's capacity by the fed fraction and **the common health's fraction**. That last is kept with the capacity and worked out again only when energy changes, so the health in it is as it was at the last change of energy (at most a minute old, since hunger takes energy in steps a minute apart).

## An effect

Almost everything is an **effect** on a part (on the head when it is the whole character's). An effect goes through four states:

1. **Added**, for a delay (its `DelayTime`).
2. **Started**, for its working time (`WorkStateTime`, often without end). Its strength rises from 0 to its full strength over a build-up (`BuildUpTime`: 1 s unless the kind says otherwise, never longer than the working time), in a straight line.
3. **Residual**, for its residue time. Its strength falls toward 0 over what is left of the state, and whatever rates it had set (health, energy, hydration a second) are set to 0 as it enters.
4. **Removed**: taken off the list. If it had made another effect on its way (a **side effect**: a fracture's pain, pain's tremor), that one is ended too: cut to the end of its working time if it has started, removed if it has not.

A state whose time is 0 passes at once. Each frame every effect on the list is stepped by the frame's time (`ManualUpdate`): the time is cut short at the end of the state, and the state's own work is done (`RegularUpdate` while started, `ResidualUpdate` while residual) before the next state is entered. A **forced residue** (`ForceResidue`) moves an added or started effect straight to residual; a **forced removal** goes on to removed.

**Adding an effect** (`AddEffect`): if the part already has one of the kind (added or started) and the kind does not stack, that one is kept: its working time is made the longer of the two (or, once started, extended to *now plus the new time*), and its strength raised if the new one is stronger; nothing new is made. Otherwise any of the kind on that part that is still residual is removed, and a new one made. Contusion, tremor and medicine in use stack (`IStackable`); painkillers and stimulators stack when they come from different items, and a second one from the same item adds to the first instead (`AddStackableEffect`, `UpdateWithSameOne`: the time left plus the item's whole duration times the new strength, to that duration at most).

Each effect may say, while started, how fast it changes health, energy, hydration and temperature; those rates are summed for the screen (`HealRate`, `DamageRate`, `EnergyRate`, `HydrationRate`). They change nothing themselves: the effect does its changes in steps of its own.

## Damage (`ApplyDamage`)

Damage to a part, of a kind (`EDamageType`), while alive:

1. Multiplied by the damage multiplier (stimulators' `DamageModifier`; 1 otherwise; enemy damage to the head is not multiplied unless the multiplier is above 1), and **environmental damage** (barbed wire, fire) by the part's `EnvironmentDamageMultiplier`.
2. The part's value lowered by it. A part brought to 0 is **destroyed**. A destroyed head or chest **kills**, unless the damage is bleeding.
3. Unless the damage is self-inflicted (bleeding, poison, dehydration, exhaustion, radiation, a stimulator, existence, a lethal toxin): **over-damage**. What the damage exceeded the part's value by, times the part's over-damage factor (head and chest 1, stomach `StomachOverdamage` 1.5, arms `HandsOverdamage` 0.7, legs `LegsOverdamage` 1, all at the top of `globals.json`'s `config`), is shared among every other part not destroyed in proportion to their maxima, each share times 0.7; a part brought to 0 so is destroyed (and a head or chest so destroyed kills).
4. Unless self-inflicted, and if the damage was 1 or more and not barbed wire: **pain** on the part, starting at once, for no working time, with a residue of `15 * damage / maximum` seconds (1 to 10) and a strength of `2 * damage / maximum` (0.33 to 2).
5. Unless self-inflicted, and unless a stimulator stops blood loss: **bleeding**, by chance, with `x` the damage over the part's maximum. Heavy only from weapons (a bullet, a blow, a landmine, a sniper, a blunt hit) or a grenade's fragment, at the odds of `HeavyBleeding.Probability`; failing that, and if the part has no heavy bleeding already, light at `LightBleeding.Probability`. The odds' top is raised by the Vitality skill's buff.
6. Unless self-inflicted: a **fracture**, by chance on the same `x`: a fall's at `Fracture.FallingProbability`, anything else's at `Fracture.BulletHitProbability`, the odds' top raised by the Health skill's buff.
7. Common at 0 **kills**. Otherwise common at or under 130 (`LowEdgeHealth.StartCommonHealth`) adds **low health** on the head.

It returns how much common health was lost.

**The odds** (`ProbabilitySetting.Try(x, top, bias)`): nothing under the threshold; otherwise a draw between 0 and the top (1 untrained) is under `K * f(x) + B`, where `f` is `x`, `x²` or `√x` as the setting says. Here:

| | f | K | B | threshold |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| heavy bleeding | x | 0.45 | -0.17 | 0.5 |
| light bleeding | x | 0.5 | -0.125 | 0.35 |
| fracture, a fall | x | 1 | 0.38 | 0.2 |
| fracture, anything else | √x | 0.3 | 0.05 | 0.3 |

So a fall that takes a fifth of a leg's maximum or more breaks it 58% of the time or more, and one that takes 62% breaks it for certain.

The player around it (`Player.ApplyDamageInfo`): before the damage, any **wound** on the part is pushed by the damage (below); after it, a hurt voice (not under painkillers unless more than 4); a weapon's hit slows the character for a moment and takes stamina (`ApplyHitDebuff`), which no damage on the range is.

## The effects

Those that the range can bring about, with their figures here:

- **Light bleeding** and **heavy bleeding** (on a part). Delay 0.4 s, residue 0.4 s, working without end (with the elite Vitality buff, light 22 s and heavy 33 s, each bleeding's `EliteVitalityDuration`). Every `HealthLoopTime` (light 6 s, heavy 4 s) it takes `DamageHealth` (light 0.8, heavy 0.9) **from every one of the seven parts**, as bleeding (so no over-damage, no pain; a destroyed part loses nothing), and every `EnergyLoopTime` (light 6 s, heavy 5 s) 0.5 energy, starting exhaustion if that empties it. Dehydrated, the figures are the dehydrated ones (0.4 every 6 s; light bleeding then ends after 30 s). Nothing while berserk. Only one bleeding a part: a heavy one replaces a light one, and a new bleeding takes away the part's wound. **A heavy bleeding that ends leaves a wound.**
- **Wound** (on a part, 480 s). On being made it draws a threshold between 25 and 60; each push adds to a tally (a hit's damage on that part's wound; 1 on every wound at the start of each jump; a tenth of a sprint's distance on every wound, at the sprint's end), and past the threshold the part bleeds lightly again, if it is not bleeding and nothing stops blood loss.
- **Fracture** (an arm or a leg not destroyed). No delay when made by damage; working without end; residue 5 s. Starting, it gives the part **pain**, a second later, for as long as the fracture lasts.
- **Pain** (on a part). Build-up 0.1 s, residue 15 s unless told; its working time, residue and strength are cut by the Stress Resistance skill's pain buff. Thirty seconds after it starts (`Pain.TremorDelay`) it gives the head a **tremor** for as long as the pain lasts (which itself waits its 15 s: so 45 s from the pain's start). A forced residue lasts 2 s. Hidden from the screen by a painkiller.
- **Tremor** (head; stacks). Delay 15 s (`Tremor.DefaultDelay`), working without end, residue 0.2 s.
- **Painkiller** (head; stacks by item). From a medicine's `Pain` entry: its delay, a working time of its duration times the amount used (lengthened by the Immunity skill's buff), a residue of its fade.
- **Low health** (head). Delay 0.4 s, residue 5 s; ended when common health is back to 130 or more. With the elite Stress Resistance buff it starts a berserk.
- **Existence** (head; the player's from the start): every `EnergyLoopTime` (60 s) it takes `EnergyDamage` (3.2) energy, and every `HydrationLoopTime` (60 s) `HydrationDamage` (2.6) hydration, each times one less a skill's buff (`HealthEnergy`, `HealthHydration`: 0 untrained); with the stomach destroyed the loops are five times shorter. So a character goes from full to empty in 31 minutes for energy and 38 for hydration.
- **Exhaustion** (head), when energy reaches 0: after a delay of 30 s, 1 damage to every part every 5 s (none with the elite Metabolism buff), as exhaustion; ended (residue 5 s) as soon as energy is above 0.
- **Dehydration** (stomach), when hydration reaches 0: on being added it gives the head **pain**, ten seconds later, for as long as the dehydration lasts; after 50 s, 1 damage to every part every 15 s, and every bleeding goes over to its dehydrated figures; ended (residue 5 s) at the first step with hydration above 0, the bleedings going back.
- **Chronic stamina fatigue** (chest), which the stamina adds after 15 s of spent legs (`stamina.md`): 180 s, its strength one more each time it is added again; energy taken every 10 s at `0.0001 + 0.0063 * strength` a second.
- **Encumbered** and **over-encumbered** (chest), while over the first and the second weight limits: the screen's notice of what the weight already does (`movement.md`, `stamina.md`).
- **Medicine at work** (below).
- **A contusion** (head; `DoContusion`, `Contusion`; read 2026-10-17, night): from a blast (grenade spec, "The blast"), for so many seconds and a strength: 0.1 s before it starts, working those seconds, fading for half of them; it stacks (a second is a second). Its forced residue (medicine that cures it, `Contusion` in a cure's list) is 2 s. It changes nothing of the character's movement or health: what it does is to the screen (`EffectsController`: a wiggle, a motion blur and double vision, by its strength) and to the ears (`Player.TryStartContusion`: the tinnitus for its working time, `BetterAudio.StartTinnitusEffect`, unless headphones are worn). A blunt hit on the head that armour absorbed 10 or more of makes one too (`BluntContusion`). **Built** (2026-10-17, night, 0.52.0; `Kind::Contusion`, `Health::do_contusion`): the effect with its times, stacking and forced residue, a cure of it reaching it, its icon and words ("Concussion") on the HEALTH tab; from a grenade's blast. Its tinnitus since 0.57.0 and its screen effects since 0.58.0 (below). **Not built**: a blunt hit's (armour's absorption is not built).

The rest come from what the range has none of, and are read only as far as their names: stun, flash, sanding screen, disorientation, tunnel vision (explosions, flashes, sand); intoxication, lethal intoxication, radiation, body temperature, frostbite (toxins, zones, cold); berserk, panic, misfire (events); health boost, full regeneration, the scavs' regeneration; zombie infection; muscle pain; the stimulators' buffs (`Stimulator`: a list of timed changes per buff name, `config.Health.Effects.Stimulator.Buffs`, 51 lists here, which some food and drink also carry).

## A blunt hit's concussion (`ActiveHealthController.BluntContusion`, from `Player.ApplyDamageInfo`; read 2026-10-26, built 0.138.0)

After a hit's damage is applied (a bullet, a fragment, a blow: what reaches a character's parts through its armour), the health is told the part and what the armour took off the hit (`absorbed`: the damage before the armour less after). Alive, a hit to the **head** whose armour took **10 or more** concusses: a contusion on the head after 0.1 s, at strength 0.5, working a quarter of what was taken and fading over another quarter. So a helmet that stops a round (its blunt throughput letting a little through) still concusses, longer the harder the round. As any contusion it blurs and wiggles the view and, for the player, rings the ears ("A contusion's tinnitus"). **In TRP** (`Health::blunt_contusion`, called by `armor::apply_shot` after the damage): every character shot through its armour, so the range's dummies; the player is shot by nothing in the range (no bots), so neither its screen nor its ears have had one (the app's tinnitus is told of a blast's contusion only). **Compared with**: nothing recorded; tested (`tests/sim/armor.rs`, `a_helmet_that_stops_a_round_still_concusses`: Tagilla's welding mask stops a 60-damage round, 2.4 through, 57.6 taken, a contusion of 14.4 s). A recording of the player shot in a helmet (a bot, with the recorder's effects) would settle it.

## A contusion's tinnitus (`Player.TryStartContusion`, `BetterAudio.StartTinnitusEffect`, its coroutine; read and built 2026-10-18, 0.57.0)

When a contusion starts working on the character's own (`OnHealthEffectAdded`, with its working time), unless headphones are worn in the earpiece slot, the ears ring: `StartTinnitusEffect(time, clip)` with the clip `Sounds.TinnitusSound` (the movement sounds' bundle). Its length is the longer of 15 s and twice the time; a second contusion while one rings sets a new end from now and length, without starting another.

Each frame, by the share of its length gone (0 to 1), from the hard settings:

- **The ringing**: the clip looping on a source of the `Speech` kind sent straight to the master group, unplaced, at the volume `TinnitusSound` gives the share (up to 0.3 by 5%, held to 25%, down to nothing at the end).
- **The world's level**: the guns' group (`GunsMixerVolume`: the headphones' dry level plus the curve) and the main group (`MainMixerVolume`: its level at the start plus the curve) at `MainChannelLevel` of 2.5 times the share (`TinnitusEffectSpeedMultiplier`): about 0 dB at first, down to −80 dB by 18% of the length, held there to about 24%, and back to 0 dB by 40%.
- **The send**: both groups' tinnitus sends at `TinnitusLowpas` of 2.5 times the share, set by the names `Tinnitus1` and `Tinnitus2` (`AudioMixerDataContainer`). **This install's mixer exposes no parameter of either name** (its exposed parameters are kept by the CRC32 of their names, which finds `GunsVolume`, `MainVolume` and the rest), so the game's sets of them do nothing.

At the end the groups go back (the guns' to the headphones' dry level, the main to 0 dB), the sends to −80 dB, and the clip stops.

**Built** (2026-10-18, 0.57.0; `trp_data::sound::tinnitus_sound`, the app's `tinnitus.rs` under `HEALTH SYSTEM`, and the mixer's group levels, `ui_sound::Group`): a contusion from a blast starts it, unless headphones are worn (the earpiece slot holds a `Headphones` thing: the Standard USEC loadout's do); the ringing and both groups as above, each frame on the game's clock; the guns' at 0 dB plus the curve (no headphones: the default template's dry level, 0), the main's at 0 dB plus it (`PROVISIONAL`: the main group's level in a raid taken as 0 dB). In the app's mix the `Guns` group holds the shots, the blasts and the hands' sounds, the `Main` group the steps, the kit and the spent cases; the inventory's and the interface's are in neither. **Heard** on a run's tape (a Zarya at the feet, a contusion of 19.4 s, the ringing for 38.5 s): the bang cut short as the world's level falls, the ringing up to about −15 dB within 2 s and held, fading from about 10 s on; **tested** (a sound following its group's and its own level). **Not compared with the game.**

## A contusion's screen effects (`EffectsController`, `CC_WiggleAccumulator`, `MotionBlurAccumulator`, `CC_DoubleVisionAccumulator`; `CC_Wiggle`, `CC_DoubleVision`, `MotionBlur`; read 2026-10-18)

The camera (`Cam2`) has three image effects a contusion drives, each by the sum of the current strengths of the effects of its kinds on the character (`EffectAccumulator.CurrentAmount`: an effect counts from when it is added, at strength 0 until it starts, to when it is removed), held within 0 and 1, and each worked out every frame while it is on (`EffectsController.Update`). Their figures are the effects prefab's (`EffectsPrefab` in `resources.assets`). They are applied in the camera's order: the double vision, then the wiggle, then the motion blur.

- **Double vision** (`CC_DoubleVision`, also by a stun): on while any is listed, its amount the sum times 1 (the prefab's), its displacement 1.88 and 0.22 pixels (over the screen's width and height). Each pixel (shader `Hidden/CC_DoubleVision`, read from its compiled program): the picture there, plus half of it 8 displacements over, 0.3 of it 16 over and 0.2 of it 24 over, all halved, blended in by the amount (none: the picture unchanged).
- **The wiggle** (`CC_Wiggle`, also by low health): on as an effect is added, its strength 1, its speed and scale the sum times the largest of the kinds' (a contusion's 0.8 and 8; low health's 0.4 and 5); its timer goes on by the speed each second. With none left its strength is drawn toward nothing (`Mathf.Lerp(str, 0, Time.deltaTime)` each frame) and the effect goes off below 0.01. Each pixel (`Hidden/CC_Wiggle`): the picture 2% larger about its middle, then moved across by sin(x × scale + timer) × strength × 0.01 and up by cos(y × scale + timer) × strength × 0.01 of the screen (x and y from its lower left).
- **The motion blur** (`MotionBlur`, Unity's Standard Assets colour accumulation, also by low health): on while any is listed, its amount the sum times a figure that eases each frame toward the largest of the kinds' (a contusion's 0.8, low health's 0.3) by the frame's time, from the prefab's 0.8; held to 0.92 at most. Its own picture (made as it comes on, a copy of the frame) takes the frame by `1 − amount` each frame (the shader's colour written with that as its alpha, blended over by it), and is what is shown.

Since 0.59.0 low health (`LowEdgeHealth`, the wiggle's 0.4 and 5, the blur's 0.3) and a stun (the double vision; grenade spec, "A flash") drive them too; a berserk (`IBerserk`) and a stimulator's buffs are not built.

**How the shaders were read**: the game's own shaders bundle (`shaders`) keeps each compiled program (`probe shaders <name> <folder>` writes a shader's DirectX 11 programs out; the SDK's `fxc /dumpbin` disassembles them), and which constant is which by the parsed form's parameters (each program's `m_CommonParameters`: `_Timer`, `_Scale`, `_Str` at bytes 32, 36 and 40 of its buffer; `_Displace` at 32, `_Amount` at 40). The motion blur's script is Unity's (`UnityStandardAssets.ImageEffects.MotionBlur` in `Assembly-CSharp-firstpass.dll`, decompiled), its shader's blend states from the parsed form (its first pass `SrcAlpha OneMinusSrcAlpha`, colour only).

**Built** (2026-10-18, 0.58.0; `trp_sim::screen_effects`, the app's `contusion_view` under `HEALTH SYSTEM`): the three by the accumulators' rules from the contusions on the character, drawn after the picture's tonemapping and before the inventory's blur, in the camera's order, the interface over them untouched; since 0.62.0 in gamma space, as the game's project is ("The camera's effects in gamma space" below). `PROVISIONAL`: the wiggle's timer moved on after the accumulator's update in the same frame (the scripts' order not read). **Seen** in captures: a Zarya at the feet, the doubled, smeared and wavering view (`local\shots\contusion_1500_s92.png`) and the clean one after (`contusion_late_s92.png`); **tested** (the accumulators). **Not compared with the game.**

## A hit and health's other screen effects (`EffectsController.OnPlayerDamaged`, `ForceEffector.AddForce`, `FastBlur`, `CC_RadialBlurAccumulator`, `CC_FastVignetteAccumulator`, `CC_BlendAccumulator`; read 2026-10-18)

**What counts as a hit** (`Player.ReceiveDamage` → `OnDamageReceived` → `EffectsController.OnPlayerDamaged`): a shot (`ApplyShot`: its damage after armour, and what armour took off it), and any damage given to the player that no weapon did (`ApplyDamageInfo`, for a kind outside `DamageHelper.WeaponDamage`: barbed wire's cut, a grenade's fragments; nothing taken off), while alive. Not a fall (health's own, `HandleFall` → `ApplyDamage`), a bleeding, dehydration, exhaustion or what medicine does, which go to health alone. Nothing for a kind the character does to itself (`IsSelfInflicted`) or `Undefined`. With the damage and what armour took (`num`):

- **The push** (`ForceEffector.AddForce`, the player prefab's `ForceReact`): its strength `sqrt(num) / 11.5` (`AIM_PUNCH_COEFFICIENT`), held to 1, with shares for the hands and the camera by the part: 0.05 and 0.4, the head's 0.1 and 1.3, an arm's 0.15 and 0.5, a leg's 0.05 and 0.3. A turn is drawn in the box of ±2.5, ±2.5, ±5 degrees (`_eulersNoiseXYMaxValue`, `_eulersNoiseZMaxValue`), made of a length drawn between `1 − _deltaRandom` (0.3) and 1, and times `WiggleMagnitude` (the globals' `AimPunchMagnitude`, 5.76) and the strength it is added as an acceleration, within the spring's limit, to the camera's rotation spring times the camera's share and to the hands' rotation spring times the hands'.
- **The hit's blur** (`FastBlur` on `Cam2`, off until a hit; turned on, then `Hit`): its power `num` on painkillers, else `num × 6` to the head and `num × 3` elsewhere. From the hit, each frame (`FastBlur.Update`) its value is `clamp01(_hitCurve(t / 0.65) × power)` (`_hitTime` 0.65 s; the curve's keys at 0, 0.25 and 1 of the time, −0.0023, 0.33 and −0.0023, flat but for the last's slope −0.028), and it goes off once its time is up (not drawn on that frame). A hit after a death is not blurred (the death's own blur, `Die`, takes it). Drawn as the inventory's blur is (the same material and shader, "What lies behind the screen" in `inventory.md`) with squares of 1024 and 512, three times down and back, and mixed in by the value; first of the camera's effects. A value of 1 is the blurred square alone: a hit of a few points blurs the view whole for most of its 0.65 s.
- Blood on the screen (`BloodOnScreen.Hit`, for any but a fall of at least 0.1, a second for the head; and a bleeding's damage, `HitBleeding`): "Blood on the screen" below (built since 0.62.0).

**Pain's blur** (`CC_RadialBlur`, by pain, dehydration and a painkiller): on while any of them is listed. Its amount each frame: nothing while a painkiller is at work (not residual), else the sum of their strengths held within 0 and 1; while a pain is at work, and no painkiller at work before it in the order they came, plus `(cos 7t + sin 3t) × 0.2` (t the game's time), else eased from its last amount (over its most) toward it by 0.15 a frame; times its most, the effects prefab's 0.035, and the share the stress-resistance skill leaves (`1 − StressPain`, all of it untrained). An amount of 0 leaves the picture as it is; a pulse can take it below 0, which blurs outward. Shader `Hidden/CC_RadialBlur`'s pass 1 (the prefab's `quality`): eight samples, the i-th at `(uv − centre) × (1 − amount × i / 7) + centre`, averaged; the centre the middle of the screen. After the double vision.

**Exhaustion's vignette** (`CC_FastVignette`, by exhaustion and tunnel vision): its darkness `clamp01(sum) × 74.9 × flicker(the time since the start)` (the prefab's darkness; `EffectsCurveData.FastVineteFlicker` looped over its 5.64 s: 1, 0.558 at 1.04 s, 0.561 at 3.44 s, 1.002 at the end); `_Data` = (the centre 0.5, 0.5, the sharpness 10 × 0.01, the darkness × 0.02). Shader `Hidden/CC_FastVignette`'s pass 0 (no desaturation): with `d` the distance from the centre in the screen's terms (0 to 1 each way), `t = saturate((d × (z + w) − 0.8) / (z × 0.799 − 0.8))`, the colour times `t²(3 − 2t)`, its alpha kept, all times `_Color` (white: nothing sets it). After the eyes' burn.

**Near death** (`CC_Blend`, by low health): the effects prefab's second blend, its picture `screen_neardeath_MULT` (`resources.assets` 951, 1920 by 1080, `DXT1Crunched`: white in the middle, red veins to the edges, redder at its foot), its amount 1, its mode 1. As the first low health comes, its amount goes from 0 to 1 over 10 s; as the last goes, from 1 to 0 over 10 s whatever it had reached, and it goes off at 0; another coming while it fades starts again from 0. Shader `Hidden/CC_Blend`'s pass 1: `lerp(picture, picture × overlay, amount)`, the overlay at the same place. `Init` adds it to the camera (`AddComponentCopy`) after all the camera has, so it comes after the motion blur and after the inventory's blur.

## Falls

Every frame on the ground the movement asks the health to take a fall (`MovementContext.CheckFlying` calls `HandleFall` with the height fallen since the character last stood: 0 on every frame but the landing's). The heights are read in the frame's ground check, before the frame's move (`movement.md`, "Speed"): the landing's is where the feet were when the ground was found, up to a frame's fall above where they come to rest (recorded: 6.6 cm in a walk off a ledge of 4.3 m, which the damage shows to the fourth decimal). The part of the height over **3 m** (`Falling.SafeHeight`) is cubed and square-rooted (`h^1.5`), times **9** (`Falling.DamagePerMeter`), times the weight's fall multiplier (`Physical.FallDamageMultiplier`: from 1 unburdened toward `Stamina.FallDamageMultiplier`, 3.9 here, at full overweight), and that is done to **each leg** as fall damage, with the fall's odds of a fracture. A fall of 4 m does 9 to each leg; of 5 m, 25; of 6 m, 47; of 7 m, 72, which destroys both legs.

A leg damaged (broken or destroyed) and not under painkillers: **each sprint costs it 2 every 1 to 1.5 s** (`InflictSelfDamage`, as fall damage), and **each landing of a jump 3**.

## Barbed wire (`BarbedWire`, a `DamageTrigger`)

A box of the world the character's body passes into:

- from the moment the character's capsule enters it, the speed is held to **0.15** of the top (`ESpeedLimit.BarbedWire`, the trigger's `AddPenalty`);
- every physics step, each of the character's hit colliders inside the box ends a sprint (`ProceedDamage`: so a sprint goes on until a hit collider is in, recorded ten frames after the speed was held down) and is cut by how far that collider has moved since the step before: **2 a metre** (`BodyPartCollider.ProceedBarb`), tallied, and once the tally is 1 or more **the whole tally is done** as barbed-wire damage on that collider's part and its fraction over a whole point kept for the next (so the fraction counts twice: recorded cuts of 1.000 to 1.064). A collider that moved half a metre or more in one step (a jump through) starts its tally again instead.

As environmental damage it is multiplied by the part's figure (a leg's half point a cut), it gives no pain, it can start a light bleeding but not a heavy one, and a fracture only at the bullet's threshold (a third of the part at once, which a cut never is). A character that stands still in the wire loses nothing; one that walks through it (both legs, the hands with the weapon swinging) a little on each part that moves. The game's own wire has its box and its sound in a map's scene; the rule above needs neither.

## Using medicine, food and drink

**What an item can be used on** (`TryGetBodyPartToApply`, `BodyPartHealHelper`). Food and drink go to the head, if anything is left in them. A drug or a stimulator goes to the part asked for, or the head. Medicine otherwise goes to the best part it can help: a part not destroyed and not full, if the item heals hit points (a medkit with resource left); a destroyed part other than head and chest, if the item mends destroyed parts; or a part with an effect the item cures (bleeding, fracture, pain, contusion, intoxication, radiation), each cure costing the item's resource where it has one. Parts are ranked: those it can help first; then those with cures to do, the worse cures first (the order of `EDamageEffectType`) and more of them first; then the emptier part first; then the parts' own order. An item with a priority of parts (`BodyPartPriority`: the surgical kits' stomach, legs, arms) takes them in that order. An item that can do nothing is refused (`Inventory/IncompatibleItem`), as is any item while one is already at work or the hands are busy.

**The hands** (`Player.MedsController`; read further 2026-10-15, latest at night, built 0.25.0). ## Buffs: stimulators', food's and drink's (`Stimulator` and its `Buff`, `StimulatorSettings`, `StimulatorHelper`, `BaseHealthController.UpdateWeightModifier`, `PhysicalBase.UpdateWeightLimits`, `Skill.Buff`, `BaseSkill.SummaryLevel`; read 2026-10-25, built 0.135.0)

**The sets.** The install keeps every set of buffs in `config.Health.Effects.Stimulator`: a loop time (`BuffLoopTime`, 0.2 s) and 51 sets by name, 219 buffs in all. An item names its set by its `StimulatorBuffs` (42 of the install's items do: the stimulators, and food and drink such as the vodka, the energy drinks, the MRE). Each buff has a kind (`BuffType`, some cut to thirteen letters in the game's enum: either name is read), a chance, a delay and a duration in seconds, a value, an "absolute" flag, the skill a skill buff acts on, and the parts it may act on. A buff is wanted or a side effect by its kind and the sign of its value (`IsBuff`): the rates, skills, stamina and weight are wanted when not negative; the damage taken and the body's temperature when not positive; tremor, tunnel vision, pain, contusion, bleedings and toxins never; the blood loss's stop and the antidote always. The range's medicine crate holds every one of the 42 items (the medical table puts in each medical item, food and drink of the install).

**Who makes it.** A use of an item that names a set and is not cut short (`MedEffect.Residue`, after its cures) puts a stimulator effect on the head, its strength the share of the item used (1 for medicine; for food and drink the share eaten). It has no delay of its own, works without end and leaves at once when done. Each buff gets its own copy of its figures as the effect is made (`GetPersonalBuffSettings`): a side effect's duration is cut by Metabolism (0.01 a level) and its value by Immunity (0.01 a level); then every duration is times the strength. The delay is never cut. **The same item again** while its stimulator is still there makes no second one (`UpdateWithSameOne`): a buff at work runs from now for what it had left and the new dose's time, at most its whole; a buff done waits its delay again from now and rolls its chance again; one still waiting is left. Different items each make their own, and their buffs add up.

**Its life.** At its start every buff of no delay goes to work; then every frame its own clock runs: a buff whose delay is up goes to work, one whose delay and duration are up is undone, and every 0.2 s the buffs at work that are rates act once by their value times 0.2. When every buff is done the stimulator ends, undoing any still at work. A buff going to work first rolls its chance (it is done, doing nothing, when a draw from nought to one is at or above it), then a side effect may be dodged (Immunity's elite: nine times in ten); then its action. A one-off (a bleeding, a fracture, a contusion, a toxin) is done at once after it acts. A rate's or a skill's value is its value where it is absolute, else one more than it times its reference (the head's maximum, energy's and hydration's maximum, the skill's level, the stamina's 115 and 4.5); the install's items have none of the second kind.

**What each kind does.**
- **The rates** (`HealthRate`, `EnergyRate`, `HydrationRate`): every 0.2 s, a gain goes to one part neither full nor destroyed, the seven parts taken in a shuffled order each loop; a loss comes off one part not destroyed, and a part brought to nought is destroyed by it (a stimulator's damage, self-inflicted: a head or chest so destroyed kills). Energy and hydration change as any change does (dehydration and exhaustion at nought).
- **Skills** (`SkillRate`): the skill's buff, its value held to what takes the skill from nought to 60 (against its level rounded up) and rounded. A skill's summary level is its level and its buffs, at most 60 where they add up above nought and 51 otherwise; every rule of a skill reads the summary, so a buff can make a skill elite while it lasts (Strength's top speeds, jump, lift and inertia; Endurance's stamina; Stress Resistance's pain; Vitality's, Health's, Metabolism's and Immunity's figures; Mag Drills and Perception).
- **Stamina** (`MaxStamina`, `StaminaRate`): the legs' and the arms' capacity raised by the value (the breath's not); a flat refill a second for the legs and the breath, half of it for the arms, given whenever the pool is below its capacity, before the ordinary refill and whether or not its refill is held off.
- **Weight** (`WeightLimit`): a weight effect on the head (`Endurance`) whose strength raises every overweight limit (walk, base, sprint, the walk's speed) by that share, their factors multiplied over the weight effects at work, and the limits worked out again as one starts or ends. The game never makes such an effect absolute, so the one item whose weight buff the install marks absolute (Obdolbos's 0.1) is a tenth more too, though the game's screen writes "kg".
- **Damage taken** (`DamageModifier`): an effect on the head that multiplies the damage taken by one more than its value (held to at least -0.99) while it works; enemy damage to the head is not multiplied unless the multiplier is above one.
- **Pain, tremor, tunnel vision**: a pain on the head (as any pain, hidden by painkillers, its tremor after 30 s); a tremor of its own (its 15 s delay first; the breathing's tremor); a tunnel vision (2 s in, 2 s out; the screen's vignette only). Each ends with its buff.
- **The blood loss's stop** (`RemoveAllBloodLosses`): every bleeding ended as it goes to work, and while it works damage draws no bleeding and a wound does not open again (a bleeding put on otherwise still opens).
- **The rest**: a contusion (the Golden Star balm's, 1 s on, for 5 s); and what the range cannot cause and a host's game would (toxins and the antidote, the body's temperature, the events' own buffs): read, and left out here.

**Shared effects.** The pain, the tunnel vision, the weight's and the damage's effects do not stack on a part: two stimulators' weight buffs make one effect at the stronger strength, and the first to end takes it away for both. Tremors stack, one a buff.

**In TRP** (`trp_sim::health::stimulator`): the sets are read with the health's figures (`HealthSettings.stimulator`); the stimulator, its buffs and their effects run in the health, under the HEALTH SYSTEM switch (off, no item is used). The host reads what the buffs make each frame: the skills' buffs (`skill_boosts`, which the app puts on its skills as their summary levels: with SKILLS off they do nothing, as every skill's rule), the stamina's (`stamina_buffs`, put on the movement's stamina by the health each frame), the weight's (`carry_modifier`, times Strength's lift on every overweight limit and on the GEAR tab's weight), and the blood loss's stop (the health's own). PROVISIONAL: the stamina's reference figures for a relative buff (115 and 4.5; no item has one); the HEALTH tab's two rows of a stimulator, its buffs and its side effects with the seconds left, are not built (its icon in the effects' strip is the buffs' alone). Compared with no recording: none of a stimulator or a buffed food exists; one would settle it: a Propital and an SJ6 used in course 1's way, the health, the stamina's capacities and the skills' levels recorded through their 300 s, and a second SJ6 at 100 s.

## The voice: hurt, death, agony, breathing, and a bone's crack (`BaseSpeaker`, `TagBank`, `Player.OnAudioHealthApplyDamage`, `ApplyHitDebuff`, `OnDead`, `UpdateBreathStatus`, `OnSpeakerRelease`, `SetupSoundsFromFaceShieldComponent`, `PlayerSelfInflictedDamageThresholdAudioChecker`, `OnHealthEffectAdded`; read 2026-10-25, built 0.136.0)

**The voice and its banks.** The character's voice is its profile's (`Customization.Voice`, an id of the database's customizations whose name is the voice's: `Bear_2_Eng` for the install's profile). Its phrases are a bundle of their own (`assets/content/audio/phrases/<name in small letters>_voice.bundle` for most; the game keeps a few exceptions in its code, so where the guess fails every phrase bundle is looked through for the `Voice` of that name). A voice is a list of banks, one a trigger; the health uses four: being hurt (importance 2, 13 clips here), death (3, 7), agony (3, 4) and breathing (0, 4). A clip has the tags it answers to (how aware, alone or in a group, the side, how hurt; a field with no bits answers to anything), its volume (0.3 here) and falloff, and its length. A bank's clip is drawn among those whose tags agree with the phrase's (in each of the six fields where both have bits, they share one), each once a round: the clips said are set aside until one is left, which is said and the round begins again without it, so the same clip never comes twice running. The game draws with Unity's generator, which cannot be matched draw for draw: which clips come, not their order, is the game's.

**The speaker.** One phrase at a time: a phrase keeps the speaker busy for its clip's length and a tenth of a second; while busy a phrase of no greater importance is not said, and one of greater importance cuts the one playing. When it is no longer busy the phrase is let go of: the breathing is asked again, and unless it goes on, what plays stops.

**When it is said.**
- **Damage** (every time the health takes damage, as multiplied): a fall's of more than a point grunts even on painkillers. On painkillers nothing of 4 or less is said. The character's own damage (a bleeding, thirst, hunger, a stimulator, existence) grunts only every sixteenth time for each such effect at work (the count starting again as the first starts and the last ends), and only not badly hurt or on a painkiller. Any other damage grunts.
- **A hit** (a bullet, a blow, a blast's fragment, the wire: what reaches the character's parts): it is the last part hit, and alive and not on painkillers (or more than 4), a grunt; coming after the damage's own, it is said only where that one was not.
- **Death**: unless the last part hit was the head (the head where nothing has hit yet), the death's cry for a weapon's kill, the agony's otherwise, over the grunt the killing damage began; a head's: silence (what plays is let go of).
- **Breathing**: needed while badly hurt or dying off a painkiller, while the breath is heard (not held, and the legs' stamina under 15 with the install's `StaminaExhaustionStartsBreathSound`, or the breath's under 15), or while muffled. Asked as any part's points change by a hundredth, as whether the breath is heard changes (from none), as the face shield changes, and at each phrase's end; started where it was not, with the health's tag when hurt and the breath's (aware when heard), at an importance of -1 when only muffled. The clip so chosen is the dying's, the hurt's, the out of breath's or the calm one's. A breath runs on, clip after clip, while needed; when it is no longer needed it plays out.
- **Muffled**: a face shield down (or one with no switch) over armour on the jaw in that slot's thing or what is fitted to it. The speech then goes to its reverb's group. The shield raised with nothing else breathed and nothing more important than a breath said: silence; a muffled breath (importance -1) plays out.
- **A bone's crack**: as a fracture starts, `Sounds.FractureSound` (`bone_break`) at the feet, by the character group's rolloff over 15 m.

**How it sounds.** First person: unplaced, forced stereo, the speech source's volume (the `Speech` preset's `OverallVolume`, 0.7) times the clip's, through the player's speech group (`ClientPlayerSpeech`, +2 dB, under `Main`, so a contusion's dip silences it too) or, muffled, its reverb's (`ClientPlayerSelfSpeechReverb`). ## Death

`Kill` sets the character not alive and tells the player (`DiedEvent`): the hands controller is dropped, the body goes limp, and the raid ends with the death screen naming the cause (the damage kind that killed). Nothing in health changes after it.

**How soon the raid ends** (read 2026-10-17, `BaseLocalGame.Spawn`, `InitiateGameStopping`, `Stop`, `GameEnd`; `PreloaderUI.StartBlackScreenShow`, `Co_ShowBlackImage`). The death's own handler shows the death panel and stops the raid at once (`Stop(…, Killed, …, 5)`). The stop lays a black image over everything and raises its opacity by each frame's length from nothing, so that it is whole after a second and done at 1.05; then the raid ends (`GameEnd`) and its world is let go (`CleanUp`). So what the player sees of a death is its first second, darkening to black; the fade's two seconds and the eyes closing from 1.8 s (below) are under the black. **Recorded**: the four deaths of `courses2` (recorder 0.10.0's course 0, 2026-10-17) end 1.04 s after the death (121 to 126 frames), as the four of `courses1` did. **The app** (0.42.7, `death::fade`, `Black`) lays the same black over everything, its opacity the seconds since the death (the dying clock is advanced by each frame's length from the frame of the death, as the game's opacity is), and shows its own death screen (the app's: the game ends the raid there) when it is done, at 1.05 s (`death::SCREEN_AT`). Seen in captures: half dark 0.49 s after the death, all but black at 0.85 s, the death screen at 1.18 s. Until 0.42.7 the screen came at 2.05 s, when the eyes had closed, and nothing went black before it.

**What the player does with it** (`Player.OnDead`, read 2026-10-15): the body's and the arms' animators are switched off and the character's controller with them, and a corpse is made of the player as it stands (`Corpse.CreateCorpse`): every object of it goes to the `Deadbody` layer, and a ragdoll is made on its bones (`CorpseRagdoll`). Then a push is given (`ApplyCorpseImpulse`): `HIT_FORCE` (150 here) times `0.3 + 0.7 * InverseLerp(50, 20, penetration)`, along the killing hit's direction at its point, on the body whose own collider was hit; a death with no collider hit (a fall, a bleeding, the health controller's own kill) has none. A frame later the thing in hand is let go (`DropItemDead`).

**The ragdoll** (`CorpseRagdoll.Start`, and the player prefab's `RigidbodySpawner` and `CharacterJointSpawner`, read by `trp_data::ragdoll`; `probe ragdoll` prints them). Twelve bodies: the pelvis (11 kg), the second and third spine bones (8 each), the head (5.625), each thigh (6) and calf (4), each upper arm and forearm (5.625); drag 0.05 (the head's 0.1), angular drag 0.2. Read from the code, their limits would count from how the two bodies stood when the joint was made, the pose of the moment of death; **the recordings show them counting from the skeleton's rest** (the prefab's), and that is what is built (`physics.md`, "The corpse"). The game sets each joint's mass scale to the connected body's mass over its own, without preprocessing, with projection. **Every body starts with the player's velocity, at most 2 m/s** (straight up, 1 m/s); each is pushed out of what it is in at most `CorpseMaxDepenetrationVelocity` (4 here).
