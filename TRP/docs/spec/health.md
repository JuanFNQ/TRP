# Health

The character's health: its body parts and their hit points, energy and hydration, the effects that hurt it, heal it or change what it can do (bleeding, fractures, pain, tremor, painkillers, hunger and thirst, medicine at work), death, falls, barbed wire, and what each of those does to movement, stamina and the weapon. Written from a reading of the game (0.16.9.5, SPT 4.1.6 assembly) on 2026-10-14, night, in my own words: `PlayerHealthController`, `ActiveHealthController` and `BaseHealthController` with their effects, `HealthValue`, `DamageHelper`, `EffectsSettings`, `Player`'s handlers of health, `MovementContext`, `Physical`, `ProceduralWeaponAnimation.PhysicalConditionUpdated`, `BreathEffector`, `BarbedWire`, `DamageTrigger`, `BodyPartCollider`, `Player.MedsController`, `InventoryScreenHealthPanel`. Figures marked "here" are this install's (`globals.json`, `config.Health` unless another key is named). Class and field names are the game's.

## Whose it is

Every character of a raid is a `Player`, and in this install each is made by `LocalGame` as a `LocalPlayer`, which gives it a **`PlayerHealthController`** (`LocalPlayer.Create`): the one-player kind of `ActiveHealthController`, which does the work. The other controllers are not a raid's: `OfflineHealthController` is the menus' between raids (made by `MainMenuShowOperation` and `SessionResultShowOperation`), `ClientPlayerHealthController` an online client's, `ObservedPlayerHealthController` another player seen over the network.

A `PlayerHealthController` made for the one at the keys (not a bot):

- is filled from the profile's health (each part's current and maximum, energy, hydration, temperature, poison, and the lasting effects the profile lists, each made again with the time it had left);
- gets the **existence** effect on the head at once (hunger and thirst; below);
- is given the profile settings' starting buff if it names one (`ProfileHealthSettings.DefaultStimulatorBuff`: none here).

A bot's is started at once and gets neither.

## The body

Seven parts (`EBodyPart`): head, chest, stomach, left arm, right arm, left leg, right leg; and **common**, which is no part but the sum of all seven (each part's current and maximum added; `GetBodyPartHealth(Common)`). Each part has a value with a least and a most (`HealthValue`: 0 and its maximum) and a flag, **destroyed**.

The maxima here (`ProfileHealthSettings.BodyPartsSettings`, each `Default` and `Maximum` alike): head 35, chest 85, stomach 70, each arm 60, each leg 65; **440 in all**. Each part has two more figures there: what environmental damage is multiplied by on it (`EnvironmentDamageMultiplier`: head 0.125, chest 0.25, stomach 0.17, arms and legs 0.5), and what share of over-damage it takes (`OverDamageReceivedMultiplier`: 0.7 for all).

A change of a part's value is clamped to its least and most; a fall in it is first multiplied by the part's `DownMult` (the controller's damage coefficient, 1 in a raid). A change to a destroyed part does nothing, and nothing changes once the character is dead.

## Energy, hydration, temperature, poison

Four values of the same kind (`ProfileHealthSettings.HealthFactorsSettings`): **energy** 0 to 100 and **hydration** 0 to 100, both starting at 100 here; temperature 28 to 40 (36.6); poison 0 to 100 (0). Energy at its least starts **exhaustion**, hydration at its least **dehydration** (below), unless metabolism has been turned off (`DisableMetabolism`, which nothing in a raid of this install calls for the player).

The stamina reads two of them (`stamina.md`, "Capacity and refill"): the refills by how fed the character is, the sprint's and the jump's costs and the refills by how watered, and the oxygen's capacity by the fed fraction and **the common health's fraction**. That last is kept with the capacity and worked out again only when energy changes, so the health in it is as it was at the last change of energy (at most a minute old, since hunger takes energy in steps a minute apart).

## An effect

Almost everything is an **effect** on a part (on the head when it is the whole character's). An effect goes through four states:

1. **Added**, for a delay (its `DelayTime`).
2. **Started**, for its working time (`WorkStateTime`, often without end). Its strength rises from 0 to its full strength over a build-up (`BuildUpTime`: 1 s unless the kind says otherwise, never longer than the working time), in a straight line.
3. **Residual**, for its residue time. Its strength falls toward 0 over what is left of the state, and whatever rates it had set (health, energy, hydration a second) are set to 0 as it enters.
4. **Removed**: taken off the list. If it had made another effect on its way (a **side effect**: a fracture's pain, pain's tremor), that one is ended too: cut to the end of its working time if it has started, removed if it has not.

A state whose time is 0 passes at once. Each frame every effect on the list is stepped by the frame's time (`ManualUpdate`): the time is cut short at the end of the state, and the state's own work is done (`RegularUpdate` while started, `ResidualUpdate` while residual) before the next state is entered. A **forced residue** (`ForceResidue`) moves an added or started effect straight to residual; a **forced removal** goes on to removed.

**Adding an effect** (`AddEffect`): if the part already has one of the kind (added or started) and the kind does not stack, that one is kept: its working time is made the longer of the two (or, once started, extended to *now plus the new time*), and its strength raised if the new one is stronger; nothing new is made. Otherwise any of the kind on that part that is still residual is removed, and a new one made. Contusion, tremor and medicine in use stack (`IStackable`); painkillers and stimulators stack when they come from different items, and a second one from the same item adds to the first instead (`AddStackableEffect`, `UpdateWithSameOne`: the time left plus the item's whole duration times the new strength, to that duration at most).

Each effect may say, while started, how fast it changes health, energy, hydration and temperature; those rates are summed for the screen (`HealRate`, `DamageRate`, `EnergyRate`, `HydrationRate`). They change nothing themselves: the effect does its changes in steps of its own.

## Damage (`ApplyDamage`)

Damage to a part, of a kind (`EDamageType`), while alive:

1. Multiplied by the damage multiplier (stimulators' `DamageModifier`; 1 otherwise; enemy damage to the head is not multiplied unless the multiplier is above 1), and **environmental damage** (barbed wire, fire) by the part's `EnvironmentDamageMultiplier`.
2. The part's value lowered by it. A part brought to 0 is **destroyed**. A destroyed head or chest **kills**, unless the damage is bleeding.
3. Unless the damage is self-inflicted (bleeding, poison, dehydration, exhaustion, radiation, a stimulator, existence, a lethal toxin): **over-damage**. What the damage exceeded the part's value by, times the part's over-damage factor (head and chest 1, stomach `StomachOverdamage` 1.5, arms `HandsOverdamage` 0.7, legs `LegsOverdamage` 1, all at the top of `globals.json`'s `config`), is shared among every other part not destroyed in proportion to their maxima, each share times 0.7; a part brought to 0 so is destroyed (and a head or chest so destroyed kills).
4. Unless self-inflicted, and if the damage was 1 or more and not barbed wire: **pain** on the part, starting at once, for no working time, with a residue of `15 * damage / maximum` seconds (1 to 10) and a strength of `2 * damage / maximum` (0.33 to 2).
5. Unless self-inflicted, and unless a stimulator stops blood loss: **bleeding**, by chance, with `x` the damage over the part's maximum. Heavy only from weapons (a bullet, a blow, a landmine, a sniper, a blunt hit) or a grenade's fragment, at the odds of `HeavyBleeding.Probability`; failing that, and if the part has no heavy bleeding already, light at `LightBleeding.Probability`. The odds' top is raised by the Vitality skill's buff.
6. Unless self-inflicted: a **fracture**, by chance on the same `x`: a fall's at `Fracture.FallingProbability`, anything else's at `Fracture.BulletHitProbability`, the odds' top raised by the Health skill's buff.
7. Common at 0 **kills**. Otherwise common at or under 130 (`LowEdgeHealth.StartCommonHealth`) adds **low health** on the head.

It returns how much common health was lost.

**The odds** (`ProbabilitySetting.Try(x, top, bias)`): nothing under the threshold; otherwise a draw between 0 and the top (1 untrained) is under `K * f(x) + B`, where `f` is `x`, `x²` or `√x` as the setting says. Here:

| | f | K | B | threshold |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| heavy bleeding | x | 0.45 | -0.17 | 0.5 |
| light bleeding | x | 0.5 | -0.125 | 0.35 |
| fracture, a fall | x | 1 | 0.38 | 0.2 |
| fracture, anything else | √x | 0.3 | 0.05 | 0.3 |

So a fall that takes a fifth of a leg's maximum or more breaks it 58% of the time or more, and one that takes 62% breaks it for certain.

The player around it (`Player.ApplyDamageInfo`): before the damage, any **wound** on the part is pushed by the damage (below); after it, a hurt voice (not under painkillers unless more than 4); a weapon's hit slows the character for a moment and takes stamina (`ApplyHitDebuff`), which no damage on the range is.

## The effects

Those that the range can bring about, with their figures here:

- **Light bleeding** and **heavy bleeding** (on a part). Delay 0.4 s, residue 0.4 s, working without end (33 s and 22 s with the elite Vitality buff). Every `HealthLoopTime` (light 6 s, heavy 4 s) it takes `DamageHealth` (light 0.8, heavy 0.9) **from every one of the seven parts**, as bleeding (so no over-damage, no pain; a destroyed part loses nothing), and every `EnergyLoopTime` (light 6 s, heavy 5 s) 0.5 energy, starting exhaustion if that empties it. Dehydrated, the figures are the dehydrated ones (0.4 every 6 s; light bleeding then ends after 30 s). Nothing while berserk. Only one bleeding a part: a heavy one replaces a light one, and a new bleeding takes away the part's wound. **A heavy bleeding that ends leaves a wound.**
- **Wound** (on a part, 480 s). On being made it draws a threshold between 25 and 60; each push adds to a tally (a hit's damage on that part's wound; 1 on every wound at the start of each jump; a tenth of a sprint's distance on every wound, at the sprint's end), and past the threshold the part bleeds lightly again, if it is not bleeding and nothing stops blood loss.
- **Fracture** (an arm or a leg not destroyed). No delay when made by damage; working without end; residue 5 s. Starting, it gives the part **pain**, a second later, for as long as the fracture lasts.
- **Pain** (on a part). Build-up 0.1 s, residue 15 s unless told; its working time, residue and strength are cut by the Stress Resistance skill's pain buff. Thirty seconds after it starts (`Pain.TremorDelay`) it gives the head a **tremor** for as long as the pain lasts (which itself waits its 15 s: so 45 s from the pain's start). A forced residue lasts 2 s. Hidden from the screen by a painkiller.
- **Tremor** (head; stacks). Delay 15 s (`Tremor.DefaultDelay`), working without end, residue 0.2 s.
- **Painkiller** (head; stacks by item). From a medicine's `Pain` entry: its delay, a working time of its duration times the amount used (lengthened by the Immunity skill's buff), a residue of its fade.
- **Low health** (head). Delay 0.4 s, residue 5 s; ended when common health is back to 130 or more. With the elite Stress Resistance buff it starts a berserk.
- **Existence** (head; the player's from the start): every `EnergyLoopTime` (60 s) it takes `EnergyDamage` (3.2) energy, and every `HydrationLoopTime` (60 s) `HydrationDamage` (2.6) hydration, each times one less a skill's buff (`HealthEnergy`, `HealthHydration`: 0 untrained); with the stomach destroyed the loops are five times shorter. So a character goes from full to empty in 31 minutes for energy and 38 for hydration.
- **Exhaustion** (head), when energy reaches 0: after a delay of 30 s, 1 damage to every part every 5 s (none with the elite Metabolism buff), as exhaustion; ended (residue 5 s) as soon as energy is above 0.
- **Dehydration** (stomach), when hydration reaches 0: on being added it gives the head **pain**, ten seconds later, for as long as the dehydration lasts; after 50 s, 1 damage to every part every 15 s, and every bleeding goes over to its dehydrated figures; ended (residue 5 s) at the first step with hydration above 0, the bleedings going back.
- **Chronic stamina fatigue** (chest), which the stamina adds after 15 s of spent legs (`stamina.md`): 180 s, its strength one more each time it is added again; energy taken every 10 s at `0.0001 + 0.0063 * strength` a second.
- **Encumbered** and **over-encumbered** (chest), while over the first and the second weight limits: the screen's notice of what the weight already does (`movement.md`, `stamina.md`).
- **Medicine at work** (below).

The rest come from what the range has none of, and are read only as far as their names: contusion, stun, flash, sanding screen, disorientation, tunnel vision (explosions, flashes, sand); intoxication, lethal intoxication, radiation, body temperature, frostbite (toxins, zones, cold); berserk, panic, misfire (events); health boost, full regeneration, the scavs' regeneration; zombie infection; muscle pain; the stimulators' buffs (`Stimulator`: a list of timed changes per buff name, `config.Health.Effects.Stimulator.Buffs`, 51 lists here, which some food and drink also carry).

## Falls

Every frame on the ground the movement asks the health to take a fall (`MovementContext.CheckFlying` calls `HandleFall` with the height fallen since the character last stood: 0 on every frame but the landing's). The heights are read in the frame's ground check, before the frame's move (`movement.md`, "Speed"): the landing's is where the feet were when the ground was found, up to a frame's fall above where they come to rest (recorded: 6.6 cm in a walk off a ledge of 4.3 m, which the damage shows to the fourth decimal). The part of the height over **3 m** (`Falling.SafeHeight`) is cubed and square-rooted (`h^1.5`), times **9** (`Falling.DamagePerMeter`), times the weight's fall multiplier (`Physical.FallDamageMultiplier`: from 1 unburdened toward `Stamina.FallDamageMultiplier`, 3.9 here, at full overweight), and that is done to **each leg** as fall damage, with the fall's odds of a fracture. A fall of 4 m does 9 to each leg; of 5 m, 25; of 6 m, 47; of 7 m, 72, which destroys both legs.

A leg damaged (broken or destroyed) and not under painkillers: **each sprint costs it 2 every 1 to 1.5 s** (`InflictSelfDamage`, as fall damage), and **each landing of a jump 3**.

## Barbed wire (`BarbedWire`, a `DamageTrigger`)

A box of the world the character's body passes into:

- from the moment the character's capsule enters it, the speed is held to **0.15** of the top (`ESpeedLimit.BarbedWire`, the trigger's `AddPenalty`);
- every physics step, each of the character's hit colliders inside the box ends a sprint (`ProceedDamage`: so a sprint goes on until a hit collider is in, recorded ten frames after the speed was held down) and is cut by how far that collider has moved since the step before: **2 a metre** (`BodyPartCollider.ProceedBarb`), tallied, and once the tally is 1 or more **the whole tally is done** as barbed-wire damage on that collider's part and its fraction over a whole point kept for the next (so the fraction counts twice: recorded cuts of 1.000 to 1.064). A collider that moved half a metre or more in one step (a jump through) starts its tally again instead.

As environmental damage it is multiplied by the part's figure (a leg's half point a cut), it gives no pain, it can start a light bleeding but not a heavy one, and a fracture only at the bullet's threshold (a third of the part at once, which a cut never is). A character that stands still in the wire loses nothing; one that walks through it (both legs, the hands with the weapon swinging) a little on each part that moves. The game's own wire has its box and its sound in a map's scene; the rule above needs neither.

## What health does to the character

The player keeps a set of **physical conditions** (`EPhysicalCondition`, on the movement context) from its health, set again whenever an effect of these kinds comes or goes and whenever a limb's health changes:

- **on painkillers**: a painkiller or a berserk is at work;
- **left leg damaged**, **right leg damaged**: that leg broken or destroyed;
- **left arm damaged**, **right arm damaged**: that arm broken or destroyed, **and no painkiller at work** (a painkiller clears both);
- **tremor**: a tremor (or a zombie infection) at work;
- **using meds**: medicine at work; **healing legs** with it, if what is being used cannot be used walking (a splint on a broken leg; anything that mends a destroyed part).

What each does (`MovementContext`, `Player.UpdateSpeedLimitByHealth`, `Physical`, `ProceduralWeaponAnimation.PhysicalConditionUpdated`):

| Condition | Movement | Stamina | Weapon |
|---|---|---|---|
| a leg damaged, no painkiller | no sprint (a sprint under way is ended); the speed held to **0.3** of the top, **0.2** with both (`ESpeedLimit.HealthCondition`) | the sprint's drain may not start | the walk's bob is the **lame** one (`WalkEffector.EWalkPreset.lame`) |
| a leg damaged, painkiller or not | a vault over 0.65 m or a climb over 0.65 m (`MaxWithoutHandHeight`) goes at the slow end of its speed range, as at full overweight (three quarters of the way there on painkillers: `SetVaultingSpeed`); **a vault over 0.65 m costs the left leg 3 if the left leg is damaged** (`VaultState`, `ApplyDamageByVaulting`; the game asks of the left leg alone, and only the vault calls it) | | |
| an arm damaged | the left arm slows a vault or climb over 0.65 m as a leg does, the right arm a climb over 1.2 m (`MaxOneHandHeight`) | aiming costs **1.5 times** as much with one arm, **2 times** with both (the cost kept is marked stale when an arm's condition changes) | the sway spring is the damaged one's (its frequency and damping lerped toward `SWAY_*_NORMAL_DAMAGED.y` by 0.5 for one arm, 1 for both, plus the overweight); the breathing's **hands' level is capped at 0.3** and, not aiming, its tremor is driven; the skill's faster reload and swap are not applied |
| tremor, no painkiller | | | the breathing's **tremor** is on (`BreathEffector.TremorOn`) |
| using meds | no sprint, no lying down, no jump, no crawling | the sprint's drain may not start | |
| healing legs | no walking at all | | |
| on painkillers | sprinting allowed with a damaged leg, no speed limit from it | | the walk's bob normal, no tremor |

A sprint started with a leg damaged runs the self-damage above.

## Using medicine, food and drink

**What an item can be used on** (`TryGetBodyPartToApply`, `BodyPartHealHelper`). Food and drink go to the head, if anything is left in them. A drug or a stimulator goes to the part asked for, or the head. Medicine otherwise goes to the best part it can help: a part not destroyed and not full, if the item heals hit points (a medkit with resource left); a destroyed part other than head and chest, if the item mends destroyed parts; or a part with an effect the item cures (bleeding, fracture, pain, contusion, intoxication, radiation), each cure costing the item's resource where it has one. Parts are ranked: those it can help first; then those with cures to do, the worse cures first (the order of `EDamageEffectType`) and more of them first; then the emptier part first; then the parts' own order. An item with a priority of parts (`BodyPartPriority`: the surgical kits' stomach, legs, arms) takes them in that order. An item that can do nothing is refused (`Inventory/IncompatibleItem`), as is any item while one is already at work or the hands are busy.

**The hands** (`Player.MedsController`; read further 2026-10-15, latest at night, built 0.25.0). Using an item takes it in hand by the same process as a weapon (`Player.Proceed` for medicine or food, `Process`): what is in hand is put away, then the item's own hands are made from its `UsePrefab` (a bundle of its own: `item_meds_salewa_container`, `item_ifak_container`; a weapon-like prefab with arms and an animator, `UsableHandsPrefab`, set up as a weapon's: `MedsController.Setup`). A quick-use key on medicine first asks whether it can do anything to the body (`SetQuickSlotItem`: `CanApplyItem(item, Common)`).

- **As the item's hands spawn, its use begins** (`MedsController.Spawn`, `MedsInHandsOperation.Start`): the item goes to work on the first part of its list (`DoMedEffect`: the part health chooses for "the common body" unless the game's continuous healing is on, which lists the parts by the item's priority: `Player.TargetBodyParts`), medicine whole and food or drink by the share asked, at most what is left in it; the animator takes a variant (below) and is told `Active`. The animator is told the use speed too (`UseTimeMultiplier`: one, a fifth more if the part at the list's head lacks under 10, the whole body's sum where the head is the common body, and the Surgery skill's buff taken for a percentage, `SurgerySpeed.Value / 100`: 0.0016 at level 40), which only the surgical kits' states run by among the items read (the CMS's draw and put-away; recorded at 1.2016, the fifth and the skill's sliver). The animator was made a ten-thousandth of a second on before any of this (`Setup`: `SkipTime(0.0001)`), which is where the recordings' first frame finds it.
- **The parts' list by the way of use**: a quick-use key names the common body (`TargetBodyParts`, the game's continuous healing being off, as the user's `Game.ini` has it); the inventory's "Use" names the common body too unless the item has a priority of parts of its own (the surgical kits), when it names the first of those it can help alone (`ItemUiContext.HealPriority`, `BodyPartsPriority`); "Use All" and food's dialog the common body; a drop on a part of the HEALTH tab that part (`HealthBarButton`, `ApplyItem`). The common body always leaves the list when a part is done, so an item goes on to a second part only where more were named (continuous healing's list).
- **The item's animator** (the Salewa's and the IFAK's alike, `probe states <UsePrefab>`): it rests in `off` until `Active`, then draws the item by its variant (`OUT TO USE`, 2.133 s; the syringe's `OUT TO USE S`, 2.93 to 2.97 s; the `off` state is left at 0.75 of its 0.033 s clip with a 0.25 s blend), uses it, looping, as long as `Active` holds (`USE` and `USE_1`, 0.933 s each, by turns; `USE S`), and when `Active` falls puts it away (`USE TO OUT`, 0.6 s; `USE TO OUT S`, 0.77 to 0.9 s). Its marks: `OutUse` as the draw begins (the controller then raises `Active`, lowers `NextLimb` and takes the next variant: `MedsController.OnOutUse`), `WeapIn` as the draw ends, `ThirdAction` for the body, sounds, and `WeapOut` near the put-away's end (0.968 of it), **which ends the item in the hands** (`HideWeaponComplete`, `BeginRemoveFromHands`): the last weapon or knife held is then taken in hand again (`SetControllerInsteadRemovedOne`, `TrySetLastEquippedWeapon`), else the first in the slots' order that can be, else the empty hands.
- **A part done** (health's `EffectRemoved` of a medicine): the parts the item can no longer help are dropped from the list's head (`NeedDequeue`); if parts are left and it is a medkit with resource left (`HaveToContinue`), the animator is told `NextLimb` with `Active` down (it puts the item away and, at 0.58 of that, draws it again: `USE TO OUT` to `OUT TO USE` when `NextLimb`) and the next part is begun with the next variant; else it **finishes**: `NextLimb` and `Active` down.
- **The variant** (`AnimationVariantsNumber`: 2 for the Salewa and the IFAK): the operation starts its count at a random one and takes the next before each draw (`SetRandomVariant`), so the first draw's is a random one and the next ones go round in turn.
- **Cut short** (another thing taken in hand: the item's hands' `Drop`): 0.6 s later (`DropWithDelay`) the medicine's work is cut (`RemoveMedEffect`: "Cut short" below) and `Active` lowered; a draw under way is played back (`BREAK`, the draw at speed -1) to its own `WeapOut`.
- **Stopped by the player** (0.33.0; the user: "Lets add Right click cancels healing"). While an item is in the hands, its hands' input (`MedsInputTranslator`) stops it for the shoot key's command (`ToggleShooting`), the aim key's (`ToggleAlternativeShooting`: the right button, the install's `Aim`) and the scope's (`ChangeScope`, the install's `ChangeAimScope`): `MedsController.Remove`, 0.6 s later (`RemoveWithDelay`, `Task.Delay(600)`), empties the parts' list and stops the item's work (`MedsInHandsOperation.Remove`: `CancelApplyingItem`, which on the local player is `RemoveMedEffect`, the medicine left over at once). Health then takes it off (`EffectRemoved`), the list being empty the use finishes, the animator puts the item away and the hands take back the last weapon. What it did before is kept: stopped before its delay was over, the item is as it was; partway, it has spent what it gave (`MedsHands::cancel`; a test; seen in captures: an AI-2 stopped as it was drawn kept 100 of 100, one stopped partway 90, one let run 69). Only with the inventory shut: open, the keys are the screen's. So the left button stops it too, as the game's does; the user asked for the right.
- **Let go on a part** (0.33.0; the user: "Lets add drag healing"). Medicine, food or drink the character carries, let go on a part's view of the HEALTH tab (`HealthBarButton.OnDrop`, on each part's `BodyPartView`; the engine's pointer events going up to it from whatever of the view is drawn), is used on that part (`PlayerHealthController.ApplyItem`: if it can do anything to it, `CanApplyItem`, the item is taken in hand with that part its list, `Player.SetInHands`, as by a quick-use key; else nothing happens). Food and drink go to the whole body whatever part they are let go on. Carried over a part it can help, the part's glow is lit (`OnPointerEnter`: the view's animator to `Highlighted`, its glow's alpha from 0 to 1 in a third of a second), and put out as it leaves or is let go (`Normal`). The use goes on with the screen open (the weapon put away and the item's hands drawn behind it), the part's figure rising as the screen shows. In the app's own look the drop is on the part's box, and nothing glows. Seen in captures: an AI-2 let go on a left arm at 30 of 60 took it to 59, the AI-2 to 69 of 100. Not captured: the glow (a scripted drag lets go on the frame it arrives).
- **Each item has an animator of its own**, all on these rules: the Salewa and the AFAK draw from a resting state; the stew, the water and the CMS begin in their draw; the splint and Analgin's blister in their use (`USE` to `END`); the Esmarch's draw lasts a frame and its put-away cut short has six ways back by how far it got (`BREAK_1` to `BREAK_6`); the Grizzly's second variant goes from its use to a fresh syringe (`TAKE NEW SYRINGE`) and on. `probe states <UsePrefab bundle>` prints each, with each state's hash as a recording keeps it.
- **Set beside the medicine course** (2026-10-15, built 0.25.0; below, "Compared with what"): every use of the twelve in the nine recordings, its states and `Active` on the game's frames, how far each state has played within a millionth of a cycle, and the item gone from the hands at its put-away's `WeapOut`. The first Salewa: in at 6.550 s, resting in `off`; drawn from 6.819 (`OUT TO USE S`); at work from 9.689 (`USE S`); done at 11.549 (`Active` down, 400 of its resource to 324); put away from 11.704 (`USE TO OUT S`); the empty hands at 12.296.

**Medicine at work** (`MedEffect`, on the part, stacking). Delay by kind (`MedEffect`): a medkit 2 s, a drink 2 s, food 1 s, the rest none. Working time: the item's use time on that part (`medUseTime`; IFAK 3 s, Grizzly 5, CMS 16, Surv12 20), shortened by a fifth more (divided by 1.2) if the part lacks under 10, and by the Surgery skill for a destroyed part; for food and drink its use time times the share eaten. While at work, every 0.4 s (`MedEffect.LoopTime`):

- food and drink lose their share of what is left in them;
- a medkit gives back hit points at `hpResourceRate` for the whole use (IFAK 50, Salewa 85, Grizzly 175, the car kit 70), spread over the working time, never more than the part lacks or the kit holds, and the kit loses what it gave;
- energy and hydration rise by the item's (`effects_health`: water 60 hydration a bottle, a ration 80 energy; Analgin -19 hydration), spread the same way, to their tops.

**When it is done** (residual, not cut short): each of the item's cures (`effects_damage`) the kit can still pay for is done on the part: a bleeding, a fracture, or (on the stomach) an intoxication or radiation is ended; pain is a **painkiller** made on the head (Analgin 80 s with a 15 s fade, Ibuprofen 280 s, Morphine 300 s); a destroyed part is mended: back to 1 point, its maximum now **that share of the old** drawn between the item's two figures (CMS 25 to 45%, Surv12 60 to 72%; the Surgery skill takes it toward all), the part's own effects gone; each cure paid from the kit (`cost`). An item with a stimulator's buff gives it. A kit with no hit-point rate loses one use (a bandage, a splint, Analgin's four doses). Then what is used up is gone: a kit at 0, food at 0, a single-use item.

## Death

`Kill` sets the character not alive and tells the player (`DiedEvent`): the hands controller is dropped, the body goes limp, and the raid ends with the death screen naming the cause (the damage kind that killed). Nothing in health changes after it.

**What the player does with it** (`Player.OnDead`, read 2026-10-15): the body's and the arms' animators are switched off and the character's controller with them, and a corpse is made of the player as it stands (`Corpse.CreateCorpse`): every object of it goes to the `Deadbody` layer, and a ragdoll is made on its bones (`CorpseRagdoll`). Then a push is given (`ApplyCorpseImpulse`): `HIT_FORCE` (150 here) times `0.3 + 0.7 * InverseLerp(50, 20, penetration)`, along the killing hit's direction at its point, on the body whose own collider was hit; a death with no collider hit (a fall, a bleeding, the health controller's own kill) has none. A frame later the thing in hand is let go (`DropItemDead`).

**The ragdoll** (`CorpseRagdoll.Start`, and the player prefab's `RigidbodySpawner` and `CharacterJointSpawner`, read by `trp_data::ragdoll`; `probe ragdoll` prints them). Twelve bodies: the pelvis (11 kg), the second and third spine bones (8 each), the head (5.625), each thigh (6) and calf (4), each upper arm and forearm (5.625); drag 0.05 (the head's 0.1), angular drag 0.2. Each takes as its colliders the hit colliders (`BodyPartCollider`) on its bone and on what hangs under it that has no body of its own: the head seven, the third spine bone six, the second four, the pelvis two, each limb one capsule. Eleven joints, each a `CharacterJoint` from a body to the one it hangs from, at the bone's own origin: a twist about its axis between two limits and two swings, about its swing axis and about the axis across both, each within its own limit (the knee: twist -80 to 10, no swing; the thigh: twist -40 to 70, swing 35 and 0; the head: twist -40 to 25, swing 25 and 0; the upper arms the widest, the left's twist -100 to 70 and swings 60 and 45). Read from the code, their limits would count from how the two bodies stood when the joint was made, the pose of the moment of death; **the recordings show them counting from the skeleton's rest** (the prefab's), and that is what is built (`physics.md`, "The corpse"). The game sets each joint's mass scale to the connected body's mass over its own, without preprocessing, with projection. **Every body starts with the player's velocity, at most 2 m/s** (straight up, 1 m/s); each is pushed out of what it is in at most `CorpseMaxDepenetrationVelocity` (4 here). The engine's own settings and its step (once a drawn frame, by the frames' average time) are in `physics.md`; **`Deadbody` meets `Deadbody`**, the ground and the world's colliders, so a corpse's limbs strike one another (a joint's two bodies do not).

**The weapon let go** (`DropItemDead`, `CorpseRagdoll.AttachWeapon`): its own colliders switched on, a rigid body of 1 kg (2 for a pistol, a throwable or a knife) set with its right-hand marker on the corpse's right palm and given the player's velocity (not held to 2 m/s). A pistol is held fast to the right forearm (`FixedJoint`); anything else hangs from the first body under the player, the pelvis, on a spring (33, damped by 3, its two bodies meeting) between a point by the right palm, 10 cm up and 10 cm back in the weapon's axes, and (-0.215, 0.164, -0.159) in the pelvis's.

**The camera** stays where the player's camera hangs (`Cam`, under the third spine bone), so the view rides the chest as it falls. Its effects (`EffectsController.OnHealthDied`, figures from the camera prefab `Cam2`): `DeathFade` turns on: its colour fades in over 2 s by its curve (from 0 with no slope to 1 with a slope of 2: the square of the time's share) at a third less than the curve, and from 1.8 s the eyes close over 0.25 s by the same curve at half of it, in black; `FastBlur` blurs for 1.5 s by a smooth step times the power of the last hit felt (`OnPlayerDamaged`: the damage with what armour took, times 6 for the head and 3 elsewhere, or 1 on painkillers; a death that no hit came before has no blur) and then switches itself off. What the two shaders do with their strengths is not read (`PROVISIONAL` in the app below).
