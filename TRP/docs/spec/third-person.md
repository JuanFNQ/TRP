# A character seen from outside

How the game poses and moves a character for eyes other than its own: another player's, a bot's, a camera's. Written from a reading of the game's code (0.16.9.5, SPT 4.1.6 assembly), of the body's controller and of the player prefab in the install; nothing here is copied from any of them. Class and field names are the game's. Read and built on 2026-10-13.

## One class, and a switch on it

There is no second character controller. Every character of a raid is the class `Player`, and `Player.PointOfView` says whose eyes it is made for: `FirstPerson` for the one playing, `ThirdPerson` for everyone else. In this install every other character is a bot, and `LocalGame` makes each of them as a `LocalPlayer` with `EPointOfView.ThirdPerson`. So what another player sees of a character is that character's own simulation with the switch thrown: the same movement, the same body animator, the same weapon animator and logic, the same arm solver.

Three things looked for and set aside:

- **`NextObservedPlayer`** (`ObservedPlayerView` and some two hundred classes) is how the live game shows a player whose state arrives over the network: nothing in an offline raid makes one. It is not what is described here.
- **The key `ChangePointOfView`** (`Home` in this install's `Control.ini`) is a binding with no handler in this build: `PlayerInputTranslator` has the method that flips the switch, and nothing calls it. Only a debugging owner for several players (`MultilePlayerOwner`) answers the command. The switch itself is a public property and works when set.
- **A third-person camera** for the one playing does not exist either: `ThirdPersonCameraState` is abstract and its one child is the locked camera of `EPointOfView.FreeCamera`. With the switch thrown the camera stays where the eyes were.

## What the switch does (`Player.PointOfView`, `InitVisuals`)

Thrown to third person, in this order:

1. The squeeze of a wide field of view goes: the ribcage's scale is put to 1, and the third-person strategy keeps it there (`ThirdPersonStrategy.ApplyFovAdjustments`).
2. The body's animator is told (`MovementContext.PlayerAnimatorPointOfView`): the flag `ThirdPerson` and the figure `ThirdPersonFloat` go to 1, and **layer 1 (`Weapon`) is given weight 1**. In first person all three are 0.
3. The body's renderers change over (`PlayerBody.UpdatePlayerRenders`): the first-person hands and the watch off, every other part drawn in full and casting its shadow.
4. The weapon's motion takes its third-person strategy and the turn-away its second set of figures (`ProceduralWeaponAnimation.PointOfView`).

And from then on, each frame, `Player.VisualPass` takes its other branches (below).

## The body's animator

The controller's own answer to the flag and the figure, all of it data of the install:

| Layer | For its own eyes | For another's |
|---|---|---|
| `Additive_FullBody` | rests in `INACTIVE_FP` | `Breath`: an additive clip of the chest and shoulders breathing, blended by `Level` (`idle_breathe_low_additive`, `idle_breathe_additive`), at one and a half times its speed; off lying down and in a vault |
| `Additive_BodyTurningOnMove` | rests in `Idle_No_Offset` | `Move_Offset` while moving and `Sprint_Offset` in a sprint: the body leans into its turn, by `DeltaRotation` (and by speed, stance and the way it moves) |
| `Additive_FP_Offsets` | may turn the torso in a sprint and shift the ribcage in blind fire | `INACTIVE` |
| `Sync_SprintHands` | a weapon two cells wide sprints by `sprint_slow_Hands_1st`, a launcher by `Sprint_Hands_Launcher_1st_angled` | by `sprint_slow_0` and `Sprint_Hands_Launcher_3rd` (the tree's blend by `ThirdPersonFloat`) |

Two layers are weighed by the game's code and not by the controller:

- **Layer 1, `Weapon`** (additive, the upper body's bones): the body's own part in what the hands do with the thing they hold. It is driven by one integer, `FirstAction`, which the animator of the thing in hand sets through a mark in its clips, `ThirdAction <number>` (`IEventsConsumerOnThirdAction`, `TranslateAnimatorParameter`): **2 as a weapon begins to leave the hands** (`Weapon.idle_to_out`: the body reaches to put it away, `idle_low_weapon_out` and `idle_weapon_out` by `Level`), **1 as one comes to them** (`Weapon.out_to_idle`, then back to `INACTIVE` at the clip's end); 3 to 7 and 9 are a grenade's pin and throws, 8 and 12 a knife's slash and stab, 13 and 14 an axe's (each from `INACTIVE`, at the blade's `SwingSpeed`), 8 and 15 the quick strokes (from `idle_to_out`): `grenades-and-knife.md`, "The knife's strokes seen from outside". Both states play at `SpeedDraw`, the weapon skill's figure. The stock AKM has the mark 0.008 to 0.010 of the way into `OUT TO IDLE`, `OUT TO IDLE ARM`, `IDLE TO OUT` and `FAST IDLE TO OUT`; the empty hands have it too. **The integer is set whoever is looking**; with the layer's weight at 0 its own eyes see none of it.
- **Layer 2, `UtilityOperation`** (additive): the reach for the vest. Its machine goes to `Idle_Reload_Utility` (or `Prone_Reload_Utility`) while `ReloadFloat` is above 0 and back when it is under 1, at half speed. Its weight is set every frame, **for a character seen from outside only** and within 70 m of the camera (`Player.AdjustUtilityLayerWeight`): while a reload is in hand or the inventory is open, and the character is not sprinting, `inverseLerp(0.5, 0.2, distance)` of the distance between the left hand's marker (`weapon_L_IK_marker`) and the weapon's marker for the vest (`weapon_vest_IK_marker`, a point the weapon's rig carries: on the stock AKM 32 cm to the left of the weapon's bone, 12 cm behind and 29 cm below); otherwise `lerp(weight, 0, 3 * dt)`. A rig with no such marker has the layer at 0, its weight kept for when one comes back. The player takes the marker from the rig's `TransformLinks` list, entry 18, and only from a list that long (`UpdateBonesOnWeaponChange`): an item's hands for using it have none there, so the layer is at 0 from the item's first frame however the inventory stands (`courses8`'s eating, 2026-11-01), and the empty hands after it take up the weight left from before. So the torso turns to the pouch as the weapon's own clip takes the hand there. The two distances and the 3 are fields of `Player` with those values in the code (`UtilityLayerRange`, `UtilityLayerLerpSpeed`); the player component is added in code, so the prefab has no say.

Nothing else in the controller asks who is looking. The legs, the stance, the lean, the layer that bends the body with the view (`Additive_Aiming`) and the one that leans it into the sights (`Additive_ISaim`) are the same animation for both.

## Whose arms, whose weapon (`Player.VisualPass`)

In first person every curve by which a body's clip takes an arm or the weapon from the first-person rig is multiplied by one more, `First_Person_Curve_Weight`, which the clips hold at 0 except where the first-person picture is to show the body's own motion (a sprint, a crawl, a vault). **For any other eyes that factor is 1**: the code reads `FirstPersonPointOfView ? GetCurveValue(FIRST_PERSON_CURVE_WEIGHT) : 1`. So (`hands-on-weapon.md`, "Whose arm it is", has the first-person forms):

- **The hands.** `1 - Hand_Left` and `1 - Hand_Right` are the weights of the two arms' solvers and of the left hand's finger pose. Standing, walking and aiming both curves are 0: both hands are solved onto the weapon's markers, as in first person.
- **The shoulders.** `PlayerBones.SetShoulders`, which puts the body's upper arms on the weapon rig's, is called in the first-person branch alone. **Seen from outside the upper arm is where the body's own clip has it**, and the solver works from there.
- **The elbows.** `AdjustElbows(1)`: each elbow's goal is the weapon's own goal blended toward the body's goal bone by `Elbow_Left` or `Elbow_Right` alone.
- **The weapon.** `ThirdPersonWeaponRootAuthority = Weapon_3rd` (0 while mounted), and `PlayerBones.ShiftWeaponRoot` takes its third-person branch: the bone the weapon's motion moves (`Weapon_root_anim`) goes to `lerp(where the first-person rig has it, Weapon_root_3rd_anim's place + its turn * Offset, authority)` and `lerp(its turn, Weapon_root_3rd_anim's turn * DeltaRotation, authority)`, where `Offset` and `DeltaRotation` are what the weapon's motion made of the bone in its own parent this frame. `Weapon_3rd` is 1 in the body's standing, walking and sprinting clips (a tenth less for as much of a side clip of `Additive_Aiming` as is blended in: `body-animator.md`, "An additive layer's curves add"), so **seen from outside the body's own weapon bone carries the weapon all the time**, where the first-person picture has it there only in a sprint. A stationary weapon has the authority at 0.
- **The weapon in the hand.** As in first person: while the right hand's weight is under 1 the weapon is moved to the body's right palm (`PlayerBones.Kinematics`).
- **The rig itself** hangs where it does in first person: at the ribcage's place, turned to the view's yaw and pitch (`MovementContext.DefaultRotationFunction` makes no difference between the two). **On a mount** the rig is moved onto the mount's point (`MountingRotationFunction`) and the weapon goes with it (the authority 0), but the arms are still solved from the body's own shoulders: the hands reach for the weapon with the arms' lengths kept, and stop short where it is out of reach. Built 0.144.0 (`arms::place_hands`: the body's upper arm and elbow goal taken back by the rig's move); before it they were carried with the rig and the shoulders stretched. Looked at, not recorded.
- **The ribcage is not turned to the view.** That turn is part of the first-person squeeze (`FirstPersonStrategy.ApplyFovAdjustments`, which also keeps the collarbones in place and scales the ribcage). Seen from outside the ribcage, the collarbones, the neck and the head are where the animation has them.
- **The head** (`PlayerBones.RotateHead(0, GetHeadRotation(), ...)`): the neck and the head are each turned by half of what the free look has the head turned (`NeckChain`, `HeadChain`, both 0.5 on the prefab). The first argument, which would blend in a turn of the neck toward the sights (`R_Neck`, `R_Head`), is always 0. With no free look the head is the animation's. Built the same day with the free look (`free-look.md`).
- **The full-body solver's passes** (`FBBIKUpdate`): `clamp(int(15 / distance), 0, 2)` in third person, fewer the further off the camera is. It has nothing to do in this build: the feet's solver (`GrounderFBBIK`) is never started (its `Update`, `FixedUpdate` and `LateUpdate` are empty; nothing calls its `Initiate`) and `HitReaction` is off on every `Player` (`body-hits.md`, "No flinch"), so no pass moves a bone (corrected 0.242.0: this said the feet's solver ran them).

## The weapon's motion (`ThirdPersonStrategy`)

Each physics step, in place of everything `weapon-handling.md` describes:

1. The turn-away's depth is put to 0 if a first-person pose is held or the mask has no turn-away, then the turn-away runs.
2. If the mask has aiming, the aim pose runs (below).
3. The position spring, the rotation spring and the recoil step.

**Not run:** the reaction to the view's turning and to movement, the force reaction, the breath, the walk's bob, the hand shake, the sway spring, and both of the camera's springs. A shot is felt only if the mask has shooting, and the breath is not told of it (`Shoot`: `Breath.OnShot` is first person's).

Each frame the bone is then placed as in first person (the springs' rests from the lean, the held-closer shift and the poses; `ApplyPosition`, which adds the recoil's kick back) but **turned by `ApplySimpleRotation`**: by the rotation spring alone, about the point the recoil turns the weapon about (`RecoilPivot`) and not the point it swings about, with no sway and **no turn from the recoil at all**. The levelling of the iron sights' line and the turn-away follow as in first person. The camera is not touched (`ApplyCameraTransformations` is empty): it stays where it last stood. A first-person pose of a body state is not assigned (`MovementContext.AssignAnimatorPose` asks for first person).

**The mask** (`EProceduralAnimationMask`). The prefab's is 125: breathing, the two reactions, shooting, the turn-away and aiming; walking is switched at run time. **A bot's is the turn-away alone** (`BotOwner.Create` sets it): a bot's weapon neither kicks nor takes the aim pose. A player's own, seen from outside, keeps the prefab's.

**The aim pose** (`CustomEffector`, on the prefab; its preset is `AimPose`). A figure goes from 0 to 1 at 4 a second while the sights are up and no first-person pose is held, and back when they are down. While it is not 0, each physics step reads two curves at it and adds each curve's figures to a spring as accelerations, divided by the spring's own input scale (so that the scale, which aiming lowers, does not thin them):

- a bump (0 at 0, 1 at 0.35, 0 from 0.685 on): the position spring's y by -0.0066 and the rotation spring's x by -0.33, that second one times its own size again, with the sign of the way the sights are going (`_initAimRotation`): +0.109 as they come up, -0.109 as they go down;
- a rise to 1 at 0.76, held: the position spring's x by -0.002 and its z by +0.002.

So the weapon is held up and to the left of where the body's clip has it for as long as the sights are up, and tips for a moment as they move. Measured in the simulation with the stock AKM: up to 27 mm and 0.8 degrees as the sights come up, and held 28 mm off with them up.

**The turn-away** reads the second pair of figures the prefab has for every component (`AltIntensity`, `AltIntensity1`), has no squeeze in it (the scale is taken as 1) and moves no camera. A rifle is drawn back 0.27 m at most where its own eyes see 0.40; its turn about the barrel's axis is -43 degrees where they see -25.

## What is drawn

- The parts: above. The legs are drawn for both.
- No squeeze, anywhere.
- **By distance the game does three different things** (read 2026-11-09, `local\distance_brief_s266.md`; rewritten 0.242.0):
  1. **Levels of detail** (Unity's `LODGroup`): every body, legs and head part, most worn things and every weapon's part draw a coarser mesh from a few metres and nothing past their last level's reach (below, "Levels of detail"). Built 0.242.0.
  2. **Posing by distance** (`Player.VisualPass`, read from code, confirmed called), with `d` the camera's distance to the character (`CameraManager.Distance`; 0 in first person). The whole pass (the weapon motion's effectors, the body-height lag, the full-body solver, every arm's solving, `AdjustElbows`, `ShiftWeaponRoot`, `RotateHead`) runs only while the character is on screen (`PlayerBody.IsVisible`: any level of its body or legs visible) and `d <= CULL_GROUNDER` (**90 m in this install**, `probe hardsettings CULL`; the code's default 40); otherwise the lag is put to 0 (`ResetHeightInterpolation`). Within it: the hands' goals blend from the weapon's markers to its grips only while `d < 40` (`IkProcess`; beyond, the markers, `HandPoser.GripWeight` left as it was); the left arm is solved to 300 m, the right arm and both hand posers to 70 m, the forearm's twist (`TwistRelax`) to 40 m, nothing past 300 m, which the 90 m cut never lets it reach (`IkApply`); the reach-for-vest layer's weight is updated only to 70 m (`AdjustUtilityLayerWeight`). **The fingers are posed at any distance**: `HandPoser.ManualUpdate(skip)` never passes `skip` on (it calls `SimpleGrip()` bare); only the forearm's twist stops at 40 m (corrected 0.242.0: this said the solver skipped the fingers). **Measured** (recorder 0.18.0, takes of 2026-11-09; `local\lod_compare_s271.md`, part 5): the weights never drop (the hands' `[1, 0, 1, 0]`, the limbs' `[1, 1]` on all 20,174 passes); the work stops, seen in the bones, walking back and in: the forearm's twist at 40.015 m (39.987 m in; the left's from 16.85 to 9.58 degrees in a frame), the right arm and palm at 70.027 m (69.989; the palm down 9.8 cm and turned 28.6 degrees), no posing pass at 90.024 m (89.994; the left palm off the handguard by 6.4 cm), and none on a frame the character is unseen at any distance. **Not built**: plan step 14c is replaced by 14e, TRP's own posing economy, whose results are what full posing gives (the game's cuts move hits).
  3. **Off-screen economies**, which change no picture: past `AnimatorCullDistance` (10 m, code and install) the body's animator, and the weapon's unless the hands are empty or hold a knife or a usable thing, are `CullUpdateTransforms` (Unity's mode keeps the state machine running and stops writing bones while no renderer of the animator, its shadows included, is seen by any camera), so **an unseen character's bones, and the hit colliders on them, hold their last pose**; a bot's renderers are switched off (`OfflinePlayerCulling`, `ObservedCullingManager`) while it is past `CULLING_PLAYER_DISTANCE` (40 m install, 30 code) and its sphere (`CULLING_PLAYER_SPHERE_RADIUS`, 4 m) is out of the view, shown again at once and hidden 5 s after first found hidden, kept while any live shot passes within 40 m of it under 45 degrees, off for good at death. `CULL_RECOIL` (35) and `CULL_GROUNDER_QUALITY` (10) have no reader. **Measured** (the same takes): the animators' culling on (`[1, 1]`) from 10.028 m walking back (10.026 m turned away), off again at 9.977 m walking in; turned away at 5 m the bones moved on every frame, at 21 and 60 m every bone held on all 1,040 and 714 unseen frames (moving only on the 4 and 5 frames before), past 176.9 m held too; `OfflinePlayerCulling` at 60 m 0.10 s after the bot left the view (not 5 s), back three frames before it re-entered; at 21 m never. **Not built**: 14e replaces the hold (it moves hits); the renderers' switch is economy, recorded here as read.

## Levels of detail (Unity's `LODGroup`, 0.242.0; TRP's own rule)

**Unity's pick, read in the engine** (Ghidra on the install's `UnityPlayer.dll` with Unity's symbols, 2026-11-09: `LODGroupManager::CalculateLODMasks`, `CalculateLODMask`, `UpdateLODGroupParameters`, `LODGroup::GetWorldSpaceSize`, `Camera::CalculateCullingParameters`, `QualitySettings::ApplySettings`). Each camera, as it culls, takes its own place, its vertical field of view and `QualitySettings.lodBias` as it then stands, and for each group reckons `d × 2 tan(fov / 2) / bias`, `d` the straight distance from the camera to the group's reference point in the world (`m_LocalReferencePoint` through the group's object's transform). Each level's reach is the group's world size (`m_Size` times its object's largest absolute scale) over the level's `screenRelativeHeight`; the first level (from `maximumLODLevel`, 0 in every quality preset) whose reach the reckoned distance is still under is drawn alone, and **nothing is drawn at or past the last's**. That is the documented rule: a level is drawn while `size × scale × bias / (2 d tan(fov / 2))`, the share of the view's height the group fills, is above its height. With the fade mode 0 (every character part's) the switch is made at once. A cross-fading group (fade mode 1: 18 equipment groups, 4 weapons') draws both levels across a band before each switch, and only where the quality's own cross-fade is allowed; the game's not built, TRP drawing by its own rule (below), which cross-fades every switch. The camera's levels are its shadow casters' too. Shadows: third person casts them On, the first person ShadowsOnly (`Dress.AdjustShadowMode`); their reach is the quality's (`settings.md`, "The shadows").

**Compared with the game** (2026-11-09; recorder 0.18.0, three takes on Shoreline at 2560 by 1440, FOV 74, bias 2: `0-lod-walk` walking back from a held Scav to 201 m, `0-lod-scope` through the TAC30 at 201 to 6 m, `0-lod-away` turned away at 5, 21 and 60 m; `local\traces\lod_s271\`, the comparison `local\lod_compare_s271.md`, the test `the_games_pick_as_the_three_recordings_drew_it`): the rule above, with the straight distance from the camera's place to the group's recorded world point, the camera's vertical field of view and the bias in force, gives the level the main camera drew on **all 105,862 group-frames** it had in view and switched on, and the optic camera's on all 14,700 (two lie within the record's rounding of a reach and are set aside); the depth along the view in place of the distance disagrees on 181. **TRP's own rule (a departure from the game on purpose, an exception to rule 8: a pass of TRP's own for the levels of detail, as the game's is slow, made with many characters in mind).** A level is drawn where the change it makes cannot be seen. **The measure** (`trp_data::lod::deviations`, once a group as it is read for a picker, kept for the run): each coarser level's *deviation*, the largest distance between its surface and the fullest level's, both ways (each corner and each triangle's middle of either to the nearest point of the other), a skin's in its bind pose, a rigid model's in its group's object's axes; each level's openings closed first (a loop of open edges near a plane, as a body's waist, neck and wrists, closed by a fan to its middle), because a part's join is hidden by the part beside it and one level caps it where another leaves it open (the bear body's caps at the waist came to 7.3 cm alone). **The loops and the caps, since 0.246.0**: a loop is walked as the surface's own triangles bound it, from an open side in its triangle's winding to the next by turning about its end through that triangle's fan, until the first side comes round again, so that two rims touching at a corner are two loops (the surface's rims as though each corner where separate fans meet were split), the same loops whatever order the sides are met in; and a cap, hidden as the join is, is surface the other level's points are measured to but not itself measured. Before, the loops were walked in the order a map gave the open edges, fresh each run: the bear body's coarser right wrist, two rims of about 3 cm about one middle that meet at a corner, was closed as two loops, as one, or not at all, and the measure came to 24.7, 23.7 or 13.6 mm from run to run, the larger two from a cap's own middle, which nothing draws.
