# Optics: scopes, collimators and the choice of sight

How a sight fitted to a weapon is chosen, aimed through, zoomed and drawn. Written 2026-10-23 (0.106.1) from a reading of the game's code (SPT 4.1.6's assembly: `OpticCameraManager`, `OpticSight`, `OpticComponentUpdater`, `ScopePrefabCache`, `ScopeZoomHandler`, `OpticRetrice`, `CollimatorSight`, `ScopeMaskRenderer`, `AutoFoldableSight`, `ProceduralWeaponAnimation`, the firearm controller and its input translator, `Player`), of the install (`items.json`'s sights, 100 scope bundles, the shaders bundle, `resources.assets`' `BaseOpticCamera`), and of one recording of the game (`courses2`, `4-sights-optic`: an AKM with a Burris 1-4x on a side mount, aimed, zoomed by the key and by the wheel). Step 1 of "In the app" is built (0.107.0: the sights as the game picks them, in `trp-sim`, no picture); the rest is not. The scratch of the reading: `local\optics_*_s118.*`; of step 1, `local\*_s119.*`.

**The user's two decisions** (2026-10-23, `decisions.md`): a switch **`PIP`** on the SIMPLE list, on, the game's own optics, a second picture in the lens; off, **the world zooms and the scope stays**, as Anomaly's and GAMMA's 3D scopes do: the view behind narrows by the scope's magnification while the weapon and the scope keep their size, the lens clear glass with its reticle. `PIP` off departs from Tarkov by the user's choice; everything else here is the game's, either way.

## The sights a weapon has

**One sight a bone.** Each `mod_aim_camera` bone of the assembled weapon is a sight: the name with a suffix too (`mod_aim_camera_001`, `_002`), which is how a part with two scopes names its two (the Specter DR's, the G36's carry handle with its 3x below and a rail above; a red dot mounted on that rail names its own bone `_002` as well). A part with one scope and several modes has one bone, shared by every mode. Where no sight part is fitted, the weapon's own `aim_camera` is the sight. The weapon keeps which is current (`AimIndex`, saved with the item; a new weapon 0).

**How the list is made** (`FindAimTransforms`): the sight parts by their items' names (an item's `Name` is its template's id with " Name", so by template id), an iron sight left out unless it hangs on a sight; from each one's slot bone every bone whose name has `mod_aim_camera`, breadth first, on only, not into a child named `mod_scope` (a sight hung on it lists its own); a bone already listed goes to the nearer part; one turned 55 degrees or more from straight back is left out. The part whose prefab's modes a bone goes by is the slot bone's first child's (`ScopePrefabCache`); the bone is an optic's where one of those modes' `OpticSight` names it. Where no listed bone looks within 1 degree of straight ahead, the first iron sight (by name) with a `mod_aim_camera` below its slot is added, and the line through the iron sights is measured (`weapon-handling.md`, "The sights"); else the iron sights are not used at all. Then, if the list is empty, the first `mod_aim_camera` anywhere, then the weapon's `aim_camera`. The iron sights and those two are listed with no turn and with their line taken; the parts' with neither. The stock builds give: the SPEAR its Tango's one bone; the G36 three (the carry handle's 3x, its backup above, the red dot on top); the M60E6 two (the Specter's optic and its backup on top), its rear sight left out.

**`ChangeAimScope`** (the install binds the right mouse button with the left Ctrl) moves to the next bone, wrapping, with no animation: refused in blind fire and while the left hand is busy, and, mounted, a bone turned more than 1 degree from the current is passed over. The eye goes from the old sight's place to the new one's at the aiming rate. **The scope that goes with the new index** (`CycleScopes`) is found by counting every sight item's scopes in the game's walk of the items (an item's slots in its template's order, then each of those items' the same way: `AllSlots`), and set on the sight listed at that count, not on the item counted: on the G36 the carry handle's backup is its scope 1; on the M60E6 the rear sight's one scope is counted first, so the backup on top leaves the Specter on its scope 0, whose sensitivity stays (kept). Where the count runs past the list the game's code stops there (an index out of range), the eye's depth and the field of view as they were. The field of view then starts again toward the new sight's, unless the mouse has the head.

**Folding sights** (`AutoFoldableSight`): each is shown exactly when its own setting is on or a sight part's aim bone faces within 1 degree of straight ahead, but not both: a backup iron sight folds down under a scope that looks along the bore.

**What counts as an optic** (`SightNBone.IsOptic`): the current sight's current mode has an `OpticSight`, the bone is that optic's own, and the item's `CustomAimPlane` is empty (the T12 thermal names one, and aims as an iron sight would while its lens still shows its camera's picture).

## A scope's parts (the install)

**The item** (`items.json`, the 222 children of `Sights` and its six kinds: `OpticScope`, `AssaultScope`, `SpecialScope`, `Collimator`, `CompactCollimator`, `IronSight`): `Zooms` a list a scope of a list a mode (the PSO-1 [[4, 4]], the Specter DR [[4, 1], [1]], the HHS-1 magnifier [[3, 1]]; the VUDU's [[6, 1]] reversed as kept); `ModesCount` a scope; `AimSensitivity` a scope a mode; `CalibrationDistances` a scope a mode; `ScopesCount`; `SightingRange`; `IsAdjustableOptic` with `MinMaxFov` (the widest field of view, the narrowest, and the speed), `ZoomSensitivity`, `AdjustableOpticSensitivity` and its `Max`; `CustomAimPlane`; `sightModType` (iron 124, optic 42, reflex 33, hybrid 10, holo 6).

**The prefab** (each scope's bundle, read with its type trees): its root holds `ScopePrefabCache` (one entry a mode: the mode's object, its `CollimatorSight` or `OpticSight`, and `IgnoreOpticsForCameraPlane`), the aim bones, and an object a mode (`mode_000`, `mode_001`). A mode's object carries the `OpticSight` (its `LensRenderer`, its `ScopeTransform`, which is the aim bone, and `DistanceToCamera`: the PSO's 0.0864 m, the Tango's 0.1252, the Specter's 0.0907) and under it the lens (`linza_mode_00N`), the back lens, the switch, and an inactive `optic_camera` whose transform is the optic camera's place and which carries `ScopeCameraData` (a fixed scope's field of view: the PSO's and the Specter's 4x 5.75, its 1x 23, the ACOG's 5.25, the PU's 4.714; near 0.05, far 1000) and `ScopeEffectsData` (chromatic aberration). A variable scope has a `ScopeZoomHandler` (the zoom ring's two pivots) and, on 13 of them, a `ScopeReticle` (a mesh in the optic camera's view). Across the 100 scope bundles: 68 `OpticSight`, 63 `CollimatorSight`, 50 `ScopeCameraData`, 16 `ScopeZoomHandler`, 13 `ScopeReticle`.

**The prefab's own zoom range is not used.** The zoom handler takes its range and speed from the item's `MinMaxFov` (`SightComponent.AdjustableOpticData` returns the template's): the Burris 1-4x's 29.5 to 7.375, as recorded; the VUDU's 26.5 to 4.4167 where its prefab says 24 to 3.2. The item's `Zooms` feeds only the HUD's magnification panel. In the five adjustable scopes checked, the two ends' ratio is the zooms' ratio: a field of view times the magnification is a constant of the scope (21.9 to 29.5 degrees), and so is a fixed scope's (the PSO's 5.75 × 4 = 23, the Specter's 23 × 1 and 5.75 × 4): the lens shows, at each magnification, what that many degrees of the aimed view would.

## The optic camera

**One camera for every optic**, made once with the main camera from the build's `BaseOpticCamera` (`resources.assets`) and left off: on exactly while an `OpticSight` component is, and only one at a time. Aiming in first person, the current sight's current mode's `OpticSight` is switched on (and the others off); with none on, a mode flagged `IgnoreOpticsForCameraPlane` (only the T12's) is switched on even when not aiming. (Seen from outside, the current optic still comes on while aiming once its list was made in first person: an edge of `F5`.) Read 2026-10-23 (`local\optics_camera_s119.md`).

- **Its picture**: one square image of 1024 pixels whatever the settings, half floats (HDR), a 24-bit depth buffer, no mip levels, no multisampling, bilinear; the camera's target, bound once as the global texture `_CamTex`, which keeps the last picture while the camera is off.
- **What it sees**: every layer but 3, 5 (UI), 6, 7, 19 (the weapon preview) and 25 (the menu's) (its mask `0xFDF7FF17`; the main camera leaves out only 5 and 19); the player's layer is drawn. It renders before the main camera (depth -1 against 0), deferred, cleared to the sky. **The world's effects are in it**: the flash's jets (their mesh made on layer 0, `MuzzleJet`), the fumes, the sparks and every hit's and blast's particle system (the effects' bundle's 1,006 objects are on layer 0 but four smoke volumes' colliders on 13: `probe bundle-layers`), each drawn by the engine for this camera as for any other. **Not in it**: the gun smoke's trail, which draws itself only when the camera drawing is the main one (`MuzzleSmoke.OnRenderObject`), and the heat haze, whose renderers draw nothing of their own (`Hidden/ClipAll`) and are drawn only by the main camera's `DistortCameraFX`, which this camera has not; nor the screen's own effects (blood, a contusion's).
- **When it is set up** (a different optic switched on; the same one again skips it): its field of view and near plane from the scope's camera data (near 0.05 on most; 0.01, 0.1, 0.23 or 0.47 on some), its far plane the main camera's at that moment (a thermal scope's own), and the scope's own effects (`ScopeEffectsData`): chromatic aberration on every one of the 68 optics, a bloom on the 14 thermals, night and thermal vision as set.
- **Each frame** it takes from the main camera the occlusion culling, the volumetric light, the scattering (its density power times 1 + 0.25 × (clamp(fov, 1, 11) − 1) / 10, with the field of view it was set up with) and the anti-aliasing's mode and figures; its auto-exposure is kept off. Its post-processing volume's profile is not read.
- **Where it is**: each frame, after the player's own late update (no frame behind), at the world place and turn of the current mode's `optic_camera` object (its scale not taken: the Tango's is exactly 0, so nothing under it is ever drawn). That object is a child of the scope, so the camera moves rigidly with the weapon; on the ACOG it stands 6 cm ahead of the eyepiece, the eye's bone `DistanceToCamera` behind it.
- **Where it looks** (`ProceduralWeaponAnimation.Calibrate`, in the player's late update: on aiming, a change of sight, of mode or of zeroing, and on every aimed frame where the field of view's squeeze is below 1, as at the install's 74): its object is turned to look at the bullet's path at the zeroing distance, its up toward the fireport's forward: "Zeroing", below.
- **Its field of view** (vertical, equal to the horizontal on the square picture): a fixed scope's `ScopeCameraData.FieldOfView` for the mode, set at set-up; a variable scope's zoom handler's value, set every frame after its update.

**The main camera while aiming through an optic** narrows to 35 degrees, eased as any aiming change (`weapon-handling.md`, "Camera"): recorded from 74 to 35 in about 60 frames. **The main camera's post-processing draws the live lens again** after its anti-aliasing, without the jitter and before the bloom and the grading (`SSAAPropagator`, in `Unity.Postprocessing.Runtime`), having filled the depth with the scope's body, the weapon and the hands so that they hide it: the lens stays sharp. The current collimator, while no optic is live, is drawn there alone, not in the main pass.

## The lens

**A scope's lens** (shader `CW FX/OpticSight`, read by disassembly 2026-10-23: `local\optics_shaders_s119.md`; the game's ordinary variant, no keywords) is drawn by the main camera in the queue Transparent+100, in four passes over a stencil: the lens mesh itself marks the window (depth tested, no depth written, back faces culled), and three passes draw inside it at a pinned depth, so that no clip plane cuts them. **No value is fed per frame for the eye**: the picture and the shadow move with the eye because they are geometry placed off the lens's plane. The lens's frame is its object's, with its local y (the axis, toward the eye) rescaled to the length of its local x (a lens squeezed along the view, as the weapon is at a wide field of view, still places its planes in x's units).

- **The lens mesh** (all those read: the Tango, the Specter's two modes, the G36's 3x, the Burris) is one template: a flat disc in local xz, its normal +y toward the eye; a centre, an inner ring of 24 at `r_in` (white vertex colour, uv radius 0.4725) and an outer ring of 24 at `r_out = r_in / 0.647` (black, uv radius 0.5).
- **The picture** is the disc scaled by `_Scales.x` (40; 43 on the G36) on the plane `_Shifts.x` along `_ShiftDirection` ((0, 1, 0) on all: −0.15, 15 cm ahead of the lens; the G36's −0.015), its uv `10 × uv − 4.5`: the camera's square spans `_Scales.x × r_in / 9.45` either way of the axis in that plane. It shows `_CamTex` there times `_MaskTex2`'s alpha (a soft circle: `MaskScope`, 1 to uv radius 0.42 and 0 at 0.5; the Specter's `mask1`, 1 to 0.27), times 1 − `_SwitchToSight`, replacing the frame. Seen through the window it shifts against the rim as the eye moves: parallax by perspective alone. On the axis, with the eye on the aim bone, it shows the world at 0.94 to 0.96 of the scope's magnification (the Tango at both ends, the Specter's 4x, the Burris; the G36's 2.94 of 3).
- **The shadow** multiplies the frame by `_MaskTex`'s alpha (`MaskWindow`: 1 in the middle, 0.62 at uv radius 0.35, 0 at 0.5) over a funnel: the inner disc shrunk by 0.99 × `_Scales.y` and moved `_Shifts.y` toward the eye (3 to 7 cm), the outer ring left where it is. A vignette whose middle slides against the rim as the eye moves off the axis. Where the funnel folds (its inner disc seen larger than the rim: the Specter, the G36, the Burris) the annulus faces away and is culled, and the inner disc covers the window.
- **The reticle** (`_MarkTex`, a 1024-pixel texture; none on the Tango, whose reticle is its own mesh, below) lies on the picture's plane with the picture's uv, so it moves with the picture and keeps its size through a variable scope's zoom (the Burris's red reticle). Laid over after the shadow: colour `(_MarkTex.rgb + _MarkLightness) × fade × s`, alpha `_MarkTex.a × s`, `s = 1 − _SwitchToSight`, where `fade = (saturate(2 × the axis · the way from the lens to the eye − 1)) ^ _NormalHideness`: 1 on the axis, gone within a few degrees off it (`_NormalHideness` 5 to 32). `_NormalHideness` is that fade's exponent, not the masks' sharpness; the lens has no `_Color` (the materials keep one the shader never reads).
- **In all**, at a pixel of the window: `lerp(picture × mask2 × s × shadow, reticle colour, reticle alpha)`; a thermal scope's lens is black while its thermal picture is not on (`_ThermalVisionOn`).

**A lens not in use** is dimmed: `_SwitchToSight` 0.97 on the lens's material, 0 while its optic is on; once a lens has been live it has its own copy of the material. Nothing writes it to the front glass (`Custom/OpticGlass`) or as a global: the glass is not dimmed with the lens (an earlier line here said it was).

**The back lens** (`CW FX/BackLens`) is an opaque black surface. **The front glass** (`Custom/OpticGlass`, queue Overlay) only adds light: a Fresnel term, a cubemap's reflection and a highlight, scaled by its `_SwitchToSightMultiplier` (0.1), since its `_SwitchToSight` is never set.

**A variable scope's reticle** is a mesh drawn into the optic camera's own picture before its image effects (`OpticRetrice`; 13 components, in the 11 variable scopes' bundles; the Tango has one, the Burris none: its reticle is its lens's `_MarkTex`): placed relative to the optic camera by its `ScopeReticle` (half a metre ahead, turned half round about y; 10 m on the Leupold Mark 5HD), its first part only, with its own material (`Custom/Crossfair`, read by disassembly 2026-10-23: `local\optics_reticle_s119.md`): the camera's projection, then the clip-space x and y times the global `_Scale` (the stored scale times 0.1: the Tango's 0.0013), so that the mesh is fixed in angle (a first focal plane: it grows with the magnification); its colour the vertex's times `_Color`, written over the picture whatever the depth, triangle after triangle in their order (the Tango's red ring under its black ticks). Its first blend shape is weighted by clamp((1 − `ReticleBlendCurve` of the zoom) × 100, 0, 100) of its full move, the zoom 0 at the widest of the item's `MinMaxFov` (the curve a straight line on the Tango and the Burris): the lines thicker at 1x.

**The picture's chromatic aberration** (`Hidden/ChromaticAberration`, pass 1, from the scope's `ScopeEffectsData`: `ChromaticAberrationShift` s, 0.00325 on the Tango and the Burris, 0.007 on the Specter and the G36's 3x): with d the way from the picture's middle, red from 0.666 of the tap at 1 − s and 0.333 of that at 1 − s/2, blue from 0.666 at 1 + s and 0.333 at 1 + s/2, green a third of the taps at 1 and 1 ± s/2, then the plain tap mixed back by (1 − 4 |d|²)²: none at the middle, whole at the sides' middles. After the reticle mesh, so it acts on that too.

**A collimator** (red dot, holographic: `CW FX/Collimator`, queue Overlay+11, one pass, depth tested, nothing written to depth, no culling) has no camera: it adds light `_MarkTex(uv).a × _Color × _NoiseTex.r / 1.3 × _FadeTex.r × _HDR` (the T1's colour (1, 0.071, 0.071), `_HDR` 3) where the mark's uv comes from the eye's place in the lens's own frame: with `e` the eye there and `d = e − _MarkShift − v / _MarkScale` from the vertex `v`, `k = _MarkShift.y² / d.y`, uv `(0.5 + 0.5 k d.x, 0.5 − 0.5 k d.z)`. With the stock `_MarkShift` (0, −150, 0) and `_MarkScale` 1 the dot sits at infinity along the lens's local y, which `CollimatorSight.LookAt` turns to the zeroing point; its angular size shrinks as the eye moves off.

**What else**: `ScopeMaskRenderer` writes a mask of the live lens and the collimators near the eye into `_ScopeMask` for the main camera (only the depth of field and the bloom's bright pass read it: they spare the sights); `OpticCullingMask` draws a full-screen quad before the optic camera's G-buffer on every optic render. Nothing darkens or blurs the main view around a scope.

## Zoom and modes

**`ChangeAimScopeMagnification`** (the right mouse button with the left Alt) moves the current scope to its next mode, wrapping (the hands animator's `ModSwitch`, then the modes' objects switched), and snaps every variable scope on the weapon to the end of its range farther from where it is: on a one-mode variable scope the key goes from the widest to the narrowest and back (recorded: 29.5 to 7.375 in one frame). The scope moved on is found by counting the sights sorted by their items' names, not by the current sight: kept. Only items with a bone in the list count; with none the key does nothing. A prefab shows the mode of the first of its item's scopes that has as many modes as it has (a prefab of one mode stays). The field of view starts again toward its figure and the eye's depth is worked out again (`OnScopesModeUpdated`).

**The wheel** (with Alt, as the install binds it) zooms a variable scope, read by its zoom handler each frame with input: a velocity grows by the input times the item's `ZoomSensitivity`, the target moves by it, clamped and put through the scope's `FieldOfViewCurve`, and the field of view moves toward the target at `MinMaxFov.z` times the velocity's size, degrees a second; wheel up narrows. The input is the wheel's turn times `OpticSensitivity` (the options' 1 to 5; 1 in the install). It works the live scope while aiming, and every variable scope on the weapon when not. The value is kept with the item (`ScopeZoomValue`: the field of view itself, 0 until first set, which is the widest). Recorded: steps of 0.06 growing to 0.3 degrees a frame, 7.375 to 20.875 in 52 frames: five notches on five frames, each adding 0.9 to the velocity, and the target moved by the velocity each time (0.9 × (1 + 2 + 3 + 4 + 5) = 13.5).

**The handler's details** (`ScopeZoomHandler.method_4` to `method_9`): the wheel turned the other way while the zoom is on its way between the ends stops it where it is (no velocity, the target there); arriving within 0.0001 of the target drops the velocity; at either end (exact equality) the run is forgotten (velocity and last input nought). The key's snap sets the field of view and the target to the end. **The input is read off the keys and the wheel directly**, not through the bindings' commands (`GetZoomInput`): a variant of `ScopeZoomIn` or `ScopeZoomOut` that ends in the wheel, with its keys held, gives the wheel's turn times `OpticSensitivity` whichever way the wheel went (the game's test of a variant's way against the turn lets either variant take either way), turned about where the options' inverted up and down is on; a variant of keys alone gives the sensitivity itself.

**The ring** (`ScopeZoomHandler.method_11`; built 0.114.0). A variable scope's prefab may name a ring (`ScopeSwitcher`) and two pivots (`StartPivot`, `EndPivot`), empty objects beside it: the ring's turn is the pivots' blended by how far the zoom is toward its narrowest (`InverseLerp` of the item's `MinMaxFov` x and y at the field of view: 0 at the widest, 1 at the narrowest), through the handler's own slerp, the sine formula without normalising. On the short way (`InverseRotation` off) the start is turned over first where the two stand more than half a turn apart; with it on the arc goes as they stand, past half a turn where they do. The game sets the world turns; the three hang from one object on every scope, so their own turns blend the same. It runs from `Init` (only an adjustable optic's), on the key's snap and on every frame the zoom moves, aiming or not: the ring always stands at the zoom. No clip moves a ring. Fifteen of the install's sixteen handlers have one, each turning about the tube, by 108.8 to 186.2 degrees: the long way on the Tango6T (185.4 degrees), the Razor Gen 2, the ATACR 7-35 and the two KMZ 1P59 and 1P69; the short way on the VUDU (its start turned over: 163.0), the Burris 1-4, Mark 4 LR, March 3-24, the S&B 1-8, 3-12 and 5-25, the Hensoldt 4-16, Mark 5HD and NcSTAR. The March 3-24 and the S&B 3-12 rest at their end pivot in the prefab, which `Init` puts right; the Nightforce NXS's handler names none and its item is not adjustable. The inspect window's model: whether the game's `Init` runs there is not read (the ring stays as the prefab has it). **The Specter's lever** is no animation: each of its two modes' objects carries its own lever mesh, the 1x's turned a quarter turn, and the key's mode change swaps the objects at once, as every prefab's modes are swapped (`ScopePrefabCache.SetMode`); the `ModSwitch` clip that plays with it moves no part of the scope.

**Zeroing** (Page Up and Page Down, while aiming) steps through the scope's `CalibrationDistances`, and the optic camera, or the collimator's mark, looks at the bullet's path at that distance: "Zeroing", below.

## Zeroing

Read 2026-10-24 (0.112.0) from the game's code: `ProceduralWeaponAnimation` (`OpticCalibrationSwitch`, `method_2`, `PreCalibrate`, `CalculateLocalSightTarget`, `Calibrate`, `GetWorldCalibrationPoint`, `OpticLookAt`, `CollimatorLookAt`, `method_12`, `method_13`, `method_14`, `RotateCurrentScopeToCorrectDistanceAngle`, `ApplyAimingAlignment`, `UpdateCurrentScopeRotation`), `FirstPersonStrategy` and `MountingStrategy` (the calibration's place in the late update; the third person's and the stationary weapon's strategies have none), `SightComponent`, `Weapon` (`RecalculateOpticCalibrationPoints`, `CreateOpticCalibrationData`), `ScopePrefabCache` (`LookAt`, `LookAtCollimatorOnly`, `GetLensTransformForward`, `RotateToAngleByDistance`), `OpticSight.LookAt`, `CollimatorSight.LookAt`, `FirearmHandsInputTranslator`, `Player.FirearmController` (`ChangeAimingMode`, `SetScopeMode`, `AdjustShotVectors`), `BattleUIScreen`, `AmmoCountPanel`, `AnimatedTextPanel`, `BattleUIComponentAnimation`, `ScopeZoomHandler.method_9`; and from the install: the sights of the range's builds (`probe sights`, `local\sights_*_s122.txt`) and the battle screen (`probe scenepanel level44 "/EFTBattleUIScreen Variant/AmmoPanel"`, `OpticCratePanel`: `local\*_panel_s122.txt`).

**The keys** (`OpticCalibrationSwitchUp` and `Down`, the install's Page Up and Page Down, on the press). With an under-barrel launcher in use they set its range instead (none of the app's has one). Else, refused unless aiming, the current sight's item (`CurrentAimingMod`: the sight part of the current way to aim; none for the weapon's own aim bone) moves its current scope's zeroing index (`ScopesCurrentCalibPointIndexes`) one place up or down that scope's list of points, held at either end, no wrap. **The index is a scope's, not a mode's**: the Specter's 4x and 1x share one; the G36's carry handle keeps one for its 3x and one for its backup. It is kept with the item, 0 (the list's first distance as the install writes it) for a new one. A step that moved asks for the calibration (`method_2`). Iron sights step too, their items having distances (the AKM's rear sight from 100 m; the G28's flip-up, 100 alone): the HUD names it, nothing turns, and the shot keeps the weapon's `IronSightRange` (ballistics spec, "A shot leaves the weapon").

**The points** (`RecalculateOpticCalibrationPoints`, as the weapon is set up and when its parts change): for every sight item on the weapon and each of its scopes, its distances sorted, repeats dropped; the weapon's default round (`defAmmo`) flown from nought along z at its `InitialSpeed` times the weapon's `SpeedFactor` by the game's own trajectory with gravity (ballistics spec, "The flight": the table of 10 ms steps, read between them), sampled from 0 s every millisecond and carried on from one distance to the next; a distance's point is the first sample whose distance from the start, the drop counted, is at least it. Each distance of the list as written is then given its point. A weapon with no default round has none (the game logs an error), nor has a scope with no distances: neither names a distance nor turns.

**The calibration** runs in the player's late update, after the procedural animation (`StartFovCoroutine`: `PreCalibrate`, the squeeze put on, `Calibrate`), in first person, mounted or not. It is asked for (`method_2`) as the sights come up, at a change of sight (`CycleScopes`), of mode (`OnScopesModeUpdated`) and of zeroing. **Where asked**, and the current item has points for its current scope: the weapon motion's local point (`_localCalibratePoint`, the weapon motion's own: a sight without points leaves the one before, nought at first) becomes that scope's current point, the HUD names the distance, a launcher's line of sight is measured, and the part a prefab turns by distance is turned (below). **Then, where asked, or aiming while the field of view squeezes the weapon** (the squeeze below 1: at the install's 74, every aimed frame): the optics are turned (not on a launcher), then the collimators, and the asking is over. Seen from outside, or on a stationary weapon, nothing runs and the asking waits.

**Where the point is** (`GetWorldCalibrationPoint`): the fireport (`HandsContainer.Fireport`, the first `fireport` below the moved bone, depth first) plus, in the fireport's own axes, the local point turned by Euler (90, −c, 0): its forward to the fireport's down (the barrel), its up to the fireport's forward, then about the barrel by −c, where c is the current sight's cant (`UpdateCurrentScopeRotation`: its turn from straight ahead where that is at least 1 degree, else 0).

**The optics' turn** (`OpticLookAt`, where the current sight's prefab has an optic in any mode): every mode of the prefab, in order, its collimator (if it has one) as below, then its optic's camera object (the object of its `ScopeData`, where the optic camera stands) turned to look at the point (`Transform.LookAt`: its forward to the point, its up as near the fireport's forward as that allows); the T12's (`IsThermalSightAvailableAt45Degrees`) then turned about its forward by minus its local roll, where that roll's Euler figure less 1 is above nought. It switches the shot's squeeze off (below). **The collimators' turn** (`CollimatorLookAt`, where the prefab has a collimator in any mode): every mode's collimator object turned to look at the point with its up the first collimator's forward as it then stands, then by −90 degrees about its own x (`CollimatorSight.LookAt`): its local y, the lens's axis toward the eye, then points straight away from the point, so that the dot at infinity along it ("The lens", a collimator) lies on the bullet's path at the distance. The G36's HKV red dot names no collimator in its prefab's modes, and does not turn. A turn is the object's own and stays as it was left until the next.

**The game turns them in the squeezed world**: positions along the hands' root's forward taken in by the squeeze, turns not (Unity's rotations do not take a parent's scale). The turn is the same in the weapon's own frame however the weapon stands, the fireport, the scope and the point moving with it; the squeeze changes it only by taking in the fireport's distance ahead of the object: on the G28's T1 at 25 m and the install's 74 degrees (a squeeze of 0.664), 4e-5 radians (1 mm at 25 m), less the farther (`tests/sim/zeroing.rs`).

**A launcher** (`AdjustCollimatorsToTrajectory`, the 13 launchers; of the app's, the M32A1): no optic is turned, and its line of sight (`method_13`, where the current sight is not an optic and its prefab has a collimator: as the sights come up and at a change of sight, with the local point as it stands, and at each calibration that found a point) is the line from the first collimator's object to the point, in the weapon bone's own axes, measured as the iron sights' line is (weapon-handling spec, "The sights": its pitch, and in first person the eye's shift to it). The weapon is turned to level it while aiming (`ApplyAimingAlignment`, which runs for a launcher as for a weapon with the iron sights' pair): the M32A1 lifts its barrel to lob its grenade the distance set.

**The part a prefab turns by distance** (`ScopePrefabCache.CanChangeAngleByDistance`, `WeaponScopeAxis`, `AngleByRange`; of the app's builds only the M32A1's M2A1 reflex, its `weapon_scope_axis` −87 degrees at 50 m to −68.5 at 400 m in steps of 50): at a calibration that found a point, where the table has the distance exactly, its local turn is set to Euler (angle, y, z) with y and z its local turn's quaternion's own y and z (the game takes a quaternion's parts for degrees: kept). This comes after the launcher's line is measured, which so takes the part where the last distance left it.

**The shot's squeeze** (`ShotNeedsFovAdjustments`, on as a weapon is made): while on, and the field of view squeezes the weapon, a shot's start and way are squeezed (ballistics spec, "Moved back along it") and the recoil's climb is taken back by the squeeze (weapon-handling spec, `ApplyComplexRotation`). The optics' turn switches it off; on again with the sights going down and at a change of mode (`method_12`), as the sights are listed (`FindAimTransforms`), as a weapon is put away, and as the iron sights' line is measured (`method_14`: the sights coming up, a change of sight or of mode, on a sight that lines up with the iron sights). So it is off while aiming through any sight of a prefab with an optic (the Specter's backup on top among them), from the calibration of the frame the sights come up: that frame's procedural animation still has it on.

**The HUD** (`BattleUIScreen`): **the zeroing** is named in the ammunition's panel (`ShowAmmoCountZeroingPanel`, the distance as a whole number) at each calibration that found a point: the sights coming up, a change of sight, of mode or of zeroing; not while the azimuth's panel is up (none of the app's). ## Aiming through it

**The sensitivity** while aiming is the mouse's aiming sensitivity times a factor: the weapon's `AimSensitivity` with no sight part fitted; else the current sight's `AimSensitivity` for its scope and mode (the ACOG's and the Specter's 4x 0.1138, the Specter's 1x 0.455, iron sights 0.65); and through a variable scope's optic, from `AdjustableOpticSensitivity` to its `Max` by the zoom's share of its range, straight with the field of view (the VUDU 0.455 to 0.0758). The recorder's `sensitivity` is the stored first figure (0.65 on the Burris), not the factor applied.

**The eye's place** through an optic goes by the far plane: its depth is the smaller of the optic bone's depth less an eyecup's shift (`ShiftsAimCamera`) and the weapon's `FarPlane`, and the forward shift is (`DistanceToCamera` + the depth's excess over `FarPlane`) × (s − 1), where an iron sight's is (1 − s) × 0.075 (`weapon-handling.md`, "Camera", the part not read until now).

**When the eye's depth is worked out** (`UpdateFarPlane`, `FindCameraDepth`): as the sights are found, at a change of sight that runs to its end, at a change of mode, and when the field of view's squeeze changes; the far plane counts every optic in the list, not only the current one (on the M60E6 the Specter's bone sets it for the backup too). The sensitivity is taken again as the sights come up, at a change of sight or mode, and each frame the variable scope's optic camera is on.

**A canted sight** (a red dot on a 45 degree offset mount) turns the weapon about its barrel while aimed through: `weapon-handling.md`, "The sights", "A canted sight" (built 0.110.0, frame for frame against `courses4`'s M4A1).

**Nothing else** is the optic's: no sway, breath, recoil or aiming time of its own.

**The game has no option** for a scope's picture: neither its size nor a switch. The only one is `OpticSensitivity`.

## With `PIP` off (the user's)

While aiming through an optic, the main camera draws the world narrowed by the scope's magnification from the aimed 35 degrees: a field of view of 2 × atan(tan(17.5°) / m), where m is the current mode's `Zooms` figure on a fixed scope and, on a variable one, its lowest zoom times its widest field of view over the field of view the zoom handler holds (the same constant as above: the world through the whole screen is as large as it would be through the lens with `PIP` on). The weapon, its scope with it, keeps the size the aimed field of view gives it; the lens is clear glass with the reticle (a fixed scope's `_MarkTex` on it; a variable scope's reticle mesh on the lens's axis). The zoom, the modes, the sensitivity, the choice of sight and zeroing work as with it on. No optic camera is made. A collimator is the same with the switch either way. **Zeroing shows as the reticle moved** (the user's, 2026-10-24, asked): the reticle on the clear glass (the mark and a variable scope's mesh alike) is drawn where the optic camera's turned axis would fall in the picture of the camera as its prefab stands, the same shift the world gets in the picture with `PIP` on, so that its middle lies on the bullet's path at the distance.

**As built** (0.111.0, step 3). The narrowing is put on as the simulation's field of view goes from the setting to the aimed 35 degrees: 1 + (m − 1) × the share of that way gone, so that it comes and goes with the aim's ease; the simulation's own field of view is left as the game's. The weapon, its arms and all drawn with them are drawn squeezed across the view about the camera by the narrowing, after the frame's transforms are worked out: the same picture as a camera of its own at the aimed field of view, but in the world's depth, so that it does not show through a wall (`PROVISIONAL`: their shadows are squeezed with them); the bullets and the simulation read the rig's places before that. The lens's shader undoes the squeeze where it measures its own frame. A variable scope's reticle mesh is drawn by a camera of its own, the reticle's layer alone, at the scope's field of view into a transparent square, which the lens reads where the picture would be, under the `_MarkTex`, so that it grows with the zoom as with `PIP` on. The back lens and the front glass are not drawn. **The glass's vignette** (0.117.0; the user's choice, 2026-10-24, "Shadow + soft circle"): the world behind the clear glass is darkened as the picture would be, times the soft circle (`_MaskTex2`) and the funnel's shadow (`_MaskTex`) worked out as in "The lens" from the eye's true place, in the game's numbers, the reticle laid over after (`PROVISIONAL`: the game's masks on clear glass are the app's own, as `PIP` off is). So, while a lens is live, every triangle of the rig's rigid meshes (not the arms') with a corner more than a centimetre ahead of the eye, on a line from the eye that passes through the glass's disc to its rim, is left out (beyond the lens, or between it and the eye, where the lens is at the objective). This is what a 3D scope's modeller does by hand in other games (Arma 3: a tunnel through the model from the aiming point); here it is worked out from each scope's own lens. The cut is made from the camera's place in the rig's frame once the eye has settled (moved less than 0.2 mm in a frame), and made again should it move 2 mm from where it was made (about a millisecond on the M60E6: 86,157 corners, 8 to 10 meshes cut); the lens is black until then. A triangle goes whole when any corner of it is seen through the glass, so the tube's long inner walls go with it, and what lay behind them shows round the glass's edge: the black rim covers it.

The main camera's field of view within 0.000008 degrees on every frame; the optic camera's within 0.000002 on the 5,686 frames it was on; the zoom value within 0.000002; the index, count, bone, optic, scope, mode, magnification, the item's sensitivity and the optic camera's on and off the same on every frame. The keys and the wheel are not recorded, so they are read off it: each frame the first of no input, a whole number of notches (-3 to 3) or the magnification key that brings the zoom to the recorded one. Every frame fitted, with 11 presses and 36 single notches; of the 399 frames the zoom moved on, 352 had no input read off them. The scope's mount is not recorded (the comparison's is a 30 mm mount on a railed handguard), so the eye's place through the optic was not compared; nor was the applied sensitivity (the recorder logs the item's first figure). The SPEAR, the G36 and the M60E6 are on no recording: their stock builds are tested (`tests/sim/sights.rs`) and were looked at in the app.
