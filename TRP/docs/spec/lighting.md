# The range's light: Factory by day, and the camera's exposure

How the game lights a level and how its camera brings that light to the screen, read for Factory by day, and how the range takes both. Written 2026-10-19, after the user's decisions "The range I guess give it factory's daytime" (2026-10-18) and, of three choices put, "Port exposure + tonemap" (2026-10-19): the range and the effects drawn in the game's scene values, then the camera's eye adaptation, filmic tonemap and vignette (PRISM), in the game's order, under a switch; the camera's other effects later, one by one (`decisions.md`).

**Where the knowledge comes from.** The figures are read from the install: the scene of Factory's scripts (below), its scripts' fields by the order the game's code declares them, its light by the engine's own layout of a `Light` (AssetRipper's published layouts for 2022.3.43f1, `local\unity_ref`). The rules are the game's code (`TODSkySimple`, `AmbientLight`, `LevelSettings`, `EnvironmentManager`, `PrismEffects`), the engine's (`SphericalHarmonicsL2`, `RenderSettings::SetupAmbient`, read with Ghidra and Unity's public symbols, and the managed side with ILSpy) and the compiled shaders in the install (`Hidden/PrismEffects`, `Hidden/PrismEffectsSecondary`, disassembled with the Windows SDK's `fxc /dumpbin`). **Compared with** recorder 0.11.0's scene of Factory by day (course 0's first step, `local\traces\courses3\0-light-factory`): the figures below marked so are the game's to the sixth place.

## Where a level keeps its light

Factory by day as the game loads it is the reworked Factory: its scripts' scene `Factory_Rework_Day_Scripts.unity` (here `level525`; the build's list of scenes names each, `probe scenes factory`). The older `Factory_Day` (`level69`) is not what the game loads (its sky is not the recorded one). In it:

- `Weather_Factory`, with `TodLightSetter`, `TOD_Time` and `TODSkySimple` (the simple sky); under it `AmbientManager` (`AmbientLight`), `Light` (the sun, with `ShadowMaskExtractor`) and `Atmosphere` (the dome the sky is drawn on);
- `---FactoryDay_levelsettings---`, with `LevelSettings`;
- `Factory_EnvironmentManager` (`EnvironmentManager`), with the indoor triggers under it.

`probe level-light` prints what is read of them.

## The sun

`Weather_Factory/Light`, a directional light: colour (1, 0.796, 0.6), intensity 1.2, soft shadows of strength 0.97; its turn in the world (0.2879, 0.8909, −0.1545, 0.3156), its light going (0.4733, −0.4569, −0.7531) in the engine's axes, 27.2° up from the horizon. It lights every layer but 3, 5 (`UI`), 6, 7, 19 (`Weapon Preview`), 21 (`CullingMask`) and 25 (`Menu Environment`). Its script hands the shaders its way and intensity (`_GlobalLightDir`, `_GlobalLightIntensity`); the simple sky hands them the way to it (`TOD_LightDirection`). In Factory by day it never moves: the game's time is held (`TODSkySimple.OnGameTimeUpdated`, `LockCurrentTime`). **Compared**: turn, colour, intensity, shadows, layers and way as recorded.

In the game's gamma space a light's colour times its intensity is what a shader is given (`_LightColor0`, `unity_LightColor`): (1.2, 0.955, 0.72).

## The level's settings

`LevelSettings.ApplySettings` (on waking and when the level has loaded) sets the engine's settings from its own: the ambient mode flat, its sky colour 0.03 (also the engine's flat ambient, `RenderSettings.ambientLight`), equator and ground colours and an intensity of 0.6 (unused by a flat ambient); the fog on, exponential, density 0.02, colour (0.21, 0.203, 0.201); the skybox material (`skybox_morning_factory_rework`, which the camera never draws: below, "The sky"); and the shaders' `_DirectionLightShadow` (0.832: how much of the sun the shaders that take no shadow leave out), `_MinAmbientColor` (0.49, the smoke grenades' least ambient), `_WaterLevel` and `_SSRFactor`. Its `HeightFalloff` (0.029) and `ZeroLevel` (−78.64) are the camera's scattering's (not built). **Compared**: as recorded.

**The engine's flat ambient** (`RenderSettings::SetupAmbient`, decompiled): its sky, equator and ground colours are kept as they are in a gamma project (converted to linear only in a linear one), and `glstate_lightmodel_ambient`, which the old vertex-lit shaders take, is **half the sky colour** (all four channels); those shaders double it back (`UNITY_LIGHTMODEL_AMBIENT`), so they are lit by 0.03. The engine's own ambient harmonics (`unity_SHAr` and the rest, `RenderSettings.ambientProbe`) hold the flat colour made linear, 0.03 / 12.92 = 0.002322 in the constant alone (recorded so), which a shader turns back to gamma (about 0.03).

## The ambient

The game's own surfaces and some of its effects take an ambient of their own, as spherical harmonics the simple sky works out on every frame (`TODSkySimple.Update`, `CalculateSphericalHarmonics`) and the ambient script hands to every shader (`AmbientLight.SetSH`). **The rule**, with `t` the height of the way to the sun (minus the light's way, its y; 0.457 in Factory) and `t01` the same from 0 to 1 (`t / 2 + 1/2`):

- the sun's colour for it, the gradient `LightColorGradient` at `t01`; the curves `AmbientContrast`, `AmbientSaturation`, `AmbientBrightness` at `t`; a grey `light`, from 0.003 to 0.25 as `t` goes from 0.07 to 1;
- every colour below is then **graded**: taken from 0.5 by the contrast (`LerpUnclamped(0.5, c, contrast)`), toward `light` by 1 (no change), from its own grey (its three channels' mean) by the saturation, times the brightness, and held at nothing below;
- a light from **halfway between the sun's way and straight down** (the sun's way plus (0, −1, 0), made of length one), of the graded sun's colour and the intensity `BounceHarmonicIntensity` at `t`;
- an even light of `AddAmbient` at `t01` plus `CloudnessLightColor` times a third of `t` (between 0 and 1), graded;
- (those two are the harmonics "without the top") and a light **from straight above** of `AddTopAmbient` at `t01`, graded, intensity 1;
- the whole taken toward an even 0.013 by `HarmonicsDaylight` (0 in Factory: not at all).

**The engine's operations** (`SphericalHarmonicsL2`; decompiled): an even light adds its colour to each channel's constant coefficient as it is (`AddAmbientLight`); a light from one way (`AddDirectionalLight`) is its colour times twice its intensity (the managed side), times 16π/17, times each of the nine coefficients' basis at its direction (0.2820948; 0.4886025 for −y, z and −x; 1.0925485 for xy, −yz and −xz; 0.9461747 for z² − 1/3; 0.5462742 for x² − y²) and its normalisation (0.2820948, −0.325735, 0.325735, −0.325735, 0.2731371, −0.2731371, 0.0788479, −0.2731371, 0.1365686: each band's basis constant signed by the engine's axes, times the band's share of a cosine lobe, 1, 2/3 and 1/4). The normalisation is the engine's data, not its code: it was taken from the bands' rule and **is confirmed by the comparison below**.

**Handed to the shaders** (`SetSH`): for each channel `_SHA` = (c3, c1, c2, c0 − c6) and `_SHB` = (c4, c5, 3·c6, c7), and `_SHC` = (the three channels' c8, 1); the sum facing n is `_SHA · (n, 1) + _SHB · (xy, yz, z², zx) + _SHC · (x² − y²)`. **`_EFT_Ambient`** is that facing straight up (`GetColorTop`: c1 + (c0 − c6) − c8), as it is in a gamma project. In Factory by day: from above (0.0691, 0.1009, 0.1246), sideways about (0.012, 0.014, 0.015), from below (0.022, 0.022, 0.020).

**How a surface takes them** (`AmbientLight.InitAmbientBuffer`: a full-screen pass after the lighting, added (one, one) in HDR; its shader `Hidden/WriteScreenAmbient`, the `UNITY_HDR_ON` variant, disassembled): the sum at the surface's normal (from the G-buffer), held at nought and **taken from linear light to gamma** by Unity's `LinearToGammaSpace` (`1.055 x^0.416667 − 0.055`, held at nought), times the surface's colour. The harmonics are linear light's: a surface facing up in Factory takes (0.29, 0.35, 0.39), one facing sideways about (0.11, 0.12, 0.13), not the sums as they are. The same pass adds a reflection (the ambient script's own cube, `_MyGlobalReflectionProbe`, rendered from 0.1 to 0.2 m: nearly nothing; times the surface's specular colour, a rim term, its occlusion and `_ReflectionIntensity`), and the colour's brightness squared times 0.05 (more with the sky's fogginess or night vision) times one less the sun's height (`_SunDirection.y`); indoors (`_StencilShadow`) the sum is taken toward its value from above. The effects' shaders take `_EFT_Ambient` as it is, not so taken.

**Compared**: the harmonics worked out by this rule from the install's figures are the recorded `_SHAr` to `_SHC` and `_EFT_Ambient` within two millionths (`trp-sim/tests/ambient.rs`).

The ambient script also renders a cubemap of its surroundings a face at a time for the shaders' reflections (`_MyGlobalReflectionProbe`), and the simple sky hands them its atmosphere's cubemap (`_MySimpleGlobalReflectionProbe`, `Factory_sky_47`, with the keyword `USE_SIMPLE_REFLECTION_PROBE`): neither is taken here (below, "Not built").

## The sky

The game's camera clears to black (`CameraClearFlags.SolidColor`); the level's skybox material is set but never drawn. Factory's sky is **its dome**, `Weather_Factory/Atmosphere`, a mesh drawn by the engine's `Skybox/Cubemap` shader with the material `Factory_skybox_47_A`: the cubemap `Factory_sky_47` (1,024 square, DXT1) read by the dome's own direction turned 190° about up (`_Rotation`), times `_Tint` (0.8) times 2 (`unity_ColorSpaceDouble` in a gamma project) times `_Exposure` (0.3): the cubemap's colour times 0.48. `PROVISIONAL`: the dome read by the way the camera looks, as a sky at an endless distance (the dome's own place and size are not read).

## What each effect's shader takes

| Shader (ported) | Its sun | Its ambient |
|---|---|---|
| `Particles/Smoke Distorted` (the fumes, the explosion's smokes) | the vertex lights (`unity_LightColor`): the sun's colour times its intensity, times 1 − `_DirectionLightShadow` | twice `glstate_lightmodel_ambient` (the level's flat 0.03), at least the material's `_Ambient` |
| `Particles/Smoke Bumped` | `_LightColor0` by the bump's normal toward `_WorldSpaceLightPos0`, wrapped by `_Scaterring`, times 1 − `_DirectionLightShadow` | the engine's harmonics' constant (0.002322) made gamma, times `_GIIndirect` |
| `Global Fog/Alpha Blended Lighted` (the explosion's lit dust) | the vertex lights, whole | twice `glstate_lightmodel_ambient`, at least `_LocalMinimalAmbientLight` (unset in Factory: 0), the sum at most 0.7; the picture times `1 + _EFT_Ambient` |
| the additive ones, the fire, the sparks, the haze | none | none |

So in Factory by day the fumes are lit by 0.03 and a sixth of the sun, (0.20, 0.16, 0.12); the lit dust by 0.7 times (1.069, 1.101, 1.125). Factory's own lamps (325 point and 271 spot lights on in the recorded scene) light its effects too, as vertex lights; the range has none of them.

## The camera's effects, in the game's order

The camera (`FPS Camera`, deferred, HDR, gamma) runs its image effects in the order of its components: `SSAA`'s propagation, `HBAO` (ambient occlusion, after the lighting), `TOD_Scattering` (the sky's scattering fog), `Undithering`, night and thermal vision (on only with their devices), `UltimateBloom`, **`PrismEffects`**, `BloomAndFlares`, `CC_Vintage` (a colour lookup at 0.498), `ChromaticAberration`, `BloodOnScreen`, `PostprocessGrayscale`, `DistortCameraFX`, `CC_Sharpen`, then the post-processing layer (screen-space reflections) and `SSAA`. The effects already built in this work (the hit's, health's and the flash's: `health.md`, "The camera's effects in gamma space") come after PRISM. Particles are drawn before any of them, in the scene.

## PRISM: the exposure, the vignette and the tonemap

**Whose camera.** The player's camera is made from the level's own camera prefab where its `LevelSettings` names one (`CameraManager.SetCameraFromSettings`, `SetCameraFromPrefab`), else from the Resources prefab `Cam2`. Factory by day names its own (`Cam2_obshaga_zalupa4_DistantShadows_OFF`, `sharedassets525.assets`, the part 15), and its PRISM differs from `Cam2`'s: `Cam2` has another filmic curve (6.2, 0.05, 0.8 and 0.004, 0.06, 0) and no exposure. The range lit as Factory takes Factory's camera's (`trp_data::prism::PrismSettings::of_camera`). The camera's other effects built so far (the hit's and the inventory's blur, blood, the distortion, the eye's burn: `health.md`) are read from `Cam2`; whether Factory's copies of them differ is not read.

**Its settings** as Factory's camera has them (read from the prefab; **compared**: as the recorded camera ran them, `trp-sim/tests/ambient.rs`): exposure on, its least and most multipliers −10 and 0.9 (`exposureLowerLimit`, `exposureUpperLimit`); the filmic tonemap (`toneValues` (7, 0.3, 0.3), `secondaryToneValues` (−0.05, 0.25, 0)); the vignette on, from 0.9 to 0.4 at 0.887, black; bloom, lookups, sharpening, fog, noise, chromatic aberration, depth of field and ambient obscurance off. **The middle grey and the adaptation's speed are not its own** (it stores 0.12 and 2): on every frame it takes `EnvironmentManager`'s `PrismExposureOffset` as its middle grey and `PrismExposureSpeed` as its speed (`OnRenderImageInternal`).

**`EnvironmentManager`** (Factory's: `OutdoorFadeTime` 0.25, `OutdoorExposureSpeed` 2, `OutdoorExposureOffset` 0.23): outside every indoor trigger the targets are its outdoor figures; inside one, the trigger's own `ExposureSpeed` and `ExposureOffset` (Factory's main hall gave the recorded 4 and 0.14). Each frame the two follow their targets by `Mathf.SmoothDamp` over the fade time (0.25 s outdoors). Both begin at 0 when the manager is made. **On the range** the outdoor figures hold outside the hall, and inside it Customs' indoor ones (since 0.76.0: "The range hall", below).

**The keywords** this camera's variant is drawn with: `PRISM_FILMIC_TONEMAP`, `PRISM_USE_EXPOSURE`, and `PRISM_SIMPLE_BLOOM` (set for every shader whenever bloom is off).

**The adaptation** (`HandleExposure`, the secondary shader's passes 3, 2 and 4): the frame is read once into a square of half its width on a side (`source.width / 2` both ways: a quirk), each texel one bilinear sample of the frame at its place, its colour held between 0 and 65,472 and weighted to a brightness (0.22, 0.707, 0.071); that square is halved again and again (each texel the mean of four bilinear samples one source texel off along the diagonals) until it is one texel; the first frame copies it into the kept texel (`_adaptationTexture`), every later frame blends it in by an alpha of `saturate(speed × 0.0125)` (source alpha, one minus source alpha): **a share of the way each frame, not each second**, so the adaptation runs faster at a higher frame rate, as the game's does. **The kept texel is eight bits a channel** (`RenderTextureFormat.ARGB32`, made in `OnEnable`): the brightness is kept between nought and one in 255ths, and a frame's blend that would move it less than half a step leaves it where it is (at 0.025 a frame it stops within about 0.08 of the frame's brightness).

**The main pass** (`Hidden/PrismEffects`, its one pass; this variant disassembled): on the frame,

1. **exposure**: times `clamp(middle grey / kept brightness)` held to at most the upper limit (0.9) and then at least the lower (−10, so no floor);
2. **the vignette**: `t = saturate((d × intensity − start) / (end − start))` with `d` the distance from the screen's middle in its own shares (0.5 at an edge's middle), `s` its smoothstep, the colour times `lerp(vignette colour, 1, s)`: whole within about 0.45 of the middle's distance, the corners at 0.57;
3. **the filmic curve**: `x = max(c − secondary.x, 0)`, then `(x (x·tone.x + tone.y) / (x (x·tone.x + tone.z) + secondary.y))²` per channel: 0 becomes 0.013, 0.5 becomes 0.81, 1 becomes 0.94;
4. a gamma (`_Gamma`, 0: none), the lens dirt and the grain (off).

The frame is LDR after it (`[ImageEffectTransformsToLDR]`).

## The camera's colour filter (built 2026-10-19, 0.72.0)

**Whose.** `CC_Vintage` (Colorful's lookup filter) is on in every level's camera as the game ships it: Customs' (`sharedassets15`), Factory by day's and `Cam2` all have it switched on with the filter "Jason" at 0.498 (`trp_data::prism::ColorFilter`), and nothing in the game's code switches it or changes it (only the player's own filter below names its kinds); the recorded Customs camera ran it so. It is **not** the colour filter of the game's Post-FX settings: that one (`PostFxSettingsGroup.ColorFilterType`, `None` unless chosen, under `EnablePostFx`, off unless switched on) goes through `GradingPostFX`, off in the shipped cameras and in the recording, and is not built. (The user's own `PostFx.ini` names "Jason" at 50 with Post-FX off: it plays no part.) The user, 2026-10-19: "I dont want to force the user onto an optional setting"; the camera's own filter is not one.

**The rule** (`CC_Vintage.OnRenderImage`, `CC_LookupFilter`, `Hidden/CC_LookupFilter`'s gamma pass, disassembled): on its first frame the camera loads the table of its filter's name (`Resources/Instagram/Jason`, 512 by 512, eight bits, repeated, bilinear); each frame, after PRISM and `BloomAndFlares`, every pixel's colour held between nought and one; 63 times its blue picks the tile above and the tile below (8 by 8 tiles of 64 by 64), its red and green a place within each (63/512 of a tile's width and half a texel in), the tile's row counted from the top (the second coordinate turned round, which the table's repeating makes one less it); the two blended by the blue's fraction; the colour taken toward that by the amount. **What it does to colours** (Jason at 0.498, worked out from the table by that rule): black to 0.016; greys 0.1 to (0.081, 0.083, 0.074), 0.25 to (0.217, 0.214, 0.196), 0.5 to (0.537, 0.520, 0.525), 0.75 to (0.81, 0.81, 0.81), white stays; a sky blue (0.45, 0.6, 0.85) to (0.49, 0.67, 0.86): the middle tones and the sky lighter, the darkest a little darker and warmer.

**In the app** (`color_filter_view`, `color_filter.wgsl`): the switch `CAMERA COLOR FILTER` (on; with `CUSTOMS LIGHT`, whose camera it is), the table read at the start and put on the card once (its rows as stored, its texture's own repeating and filtering), a pass after PRISM's on the frame in the game's numbers. **Compared**: the camera's filter, its amount and its being on with the recording (`trp-sim/tests/time_of_day.rs`); the picture not (no capture of the game's screen). **Seen** (`local\shots\filter_on_s95.png`, `filter_off_s95.png`): the sky lighter and a touch more cyan toward the horizon, the ground a little lighter, the darkest faces a little deeper.

## The sky's scattering over the scene (built 2026-10-19, 0.73.0)

**Whose.** `TOD_Scattering`, on in Customs' camera as shipped and as it ran (`trp_data::prism::CameraScattering`): its density 0.0041 and its sunrise glow 0.9755 its own, its height falloff and zero level the level settings' (`FromLevelSettings`: 0.025 and −1.6, where its own are 0.029 and 15), its dithering an 8 by 8 alpha texture (`bayer_matrix`, values up to 46/255). The weather sets its density only in its debug mode. **MBOIT**: the weather turns its `MBOIT` on as the camera is made where the level has fog remap data (Customs has), which hands the fog to `MBOIT_Scattering` (compute shaders) only while the windows' manager is active; in the raid recorded it was not, and this one ran (`MBOIT_Scattering` off): what decides it in other raids is not known.

**The rule** (`TOD_Scattering.OnRenderImageNormalMode`, `Hidden/Time of Day/Scattering`'s pass 0, its HDR gamma variant without `LIGHTEN`, disassembled with its parameters' names): an opaque image effect (after the opaque scene, before the transparent: particles are drawn after it). The way to the scene at each pixel from the frustum's corners at the far plane times the depth's linear share. **The share**: one less e to the minus (at most 10) the distance times the density over one plus (at least nought) the falloff times the height over the zero level (the place's height, the camera's height less the zero level plus the way's rise); the game's `_StencilShadow` (cleared to nought each frame, drawn into by its indoor volumes) splits the depth into a part that only darkens and the rest: nought, as on the range, it does nothing. Where the scene is the sky (the far plane), the share is a smoothstep of 200 times 0.005 less the way's height: only below the horizon. Nought: the pixel is left. **The colour**: the sky's scattering as the atmosphere's shader works it out (four samples; "Customs by its time of day"), along the way shortened by the sunrise glow, every sample's weight times the share, the phases, the night sky, the halo, the fog's grey and the ground below the horizon at that way, times the scattering brightness, to the contrast's power, plus the dithering's alpha over 65, square-rooted (no cap). **The pixel**: taken toward that colour by the share.

**In the app** (`scattering_view`, `scattering.wgsl`): the switch `SKY SCATTERING` (on; with `CUSTOMS LIGHT`), right after the scene's depth is copied (`muzzle_view`, which now copies it for this view too) and before the explosion's and the muzzle's effects, on the frame in the game's numbers; the way and the distance from the depth by the camera's own matrices; the camera's height over the zero level each frame. `PROVISIONAL`: the range's heights taken as Customs' own (its ground at nought). **Compared**: the scattering's figures with the recording (`trp-sim/tests/time_of_day.rs`); the picture not. **Seen** (`local\shots\scatter_on_s95.png`, `scatter_far_s95.png`, `scatter_far_off_s95.png`, the share alone `scatter_debug_s95.png`): nothing near, the far ground and the walls a hundred metres off and more taken toward the horizon's pale colour, the horizon whiter; looking out over the far ground the frame comes out darker than without it (PRISM's exposure answers the brighter frame).

## On the range (built 2026-10-19: Factory by day 0.70.0, Customs' morning from 0.71.0)

**The switch**: the options' feature `CUSTOMS LIGHT` (`FACTORY LIGHT` until 0.71.0; its key in the options' file `customs_light`), on unless switched (restart required, as every switch). Off, the range is lit as before (the app's sun of 12,000 lux, its ambient, Bevy's tonemapper, the effects' lights `ESTIMATE`), and the effects' shaders take the same figures as before.

**Read at the start** (`trp_app::lighting::read`): Customs' time of day, worked out for the recorded morning by the game's rules (below, "Customs by its time of day": `trp_data::tod`, `trp_sim::tod`, the moment `trp_sim::tod::CUSTOMS_RECORDED_MORNING`), its level settings, its camera's PRISM and its environment manager's outdoor figures (`trp_data::prism`). The run's first lines say so (`the range lit as Customs at 09:30 (cloudiness …): …`), or why not (the range then keeps its own). **At 09:30** since 0.122.0 (`lighting::RANGE_MOMENT`: the recorded morning's weather, its hour 09:30 by the user's word, "make 09:30 the default"; until then the recorded 09:37); the figures below and the tests are the recorded 09:37's. **Another moment** (0.72.0; since 0.121.0 also the options' `TIME OF DAY`, below, "The range's own light by the hour"): `--hour <0 to 24>` and `--cloudiness <−1 to 1>` light the range at another hour or weather by the same rules (compared only at the recorded one). By the game's own curves a higher sun is not a whiter or stronger one: the light's colour times its intensity is (0.73, 0.63, 0.40) at 09:37, (0.62, 0.55, 0.36) at 11:00, (0.55, 0.49, 0.32) at 12:30, (0.53, 0.48, 0.32) at 14:00, (0.59, 0.52, 0.34) at 16:00, the same warmth throughout, the ambient from above near the same; a cloudiness of 0.2 halves the sun and greys it (0.34, 0.31, 0.25), the sky greyer; −1 deepens the sky's blue a little. Factory by day's reading (`trp_data::level_light`, `trp_sim::ambient`) stays, compared, for the probe and the tests; the app no longer draws it.

**In the game's numbers.** The game shades in its gamma space; the app draws the scene in Bevy's linear light, in HDR with no tonemapper of Bevy's, and turns the frame into the game's numbers after the scene by a **plain 2.2 power** (`gamma_ui`'s `encode_power`). Every figure of the game's is given to Bevy made linear by the same power (`lighting::linear`), so that a product of two of them, as the game's shading multiplies a surface's colour by a light, comes back as the game's product. (The sRGB curve the camera's effects are otherwise encoded by has a straight part near black: by it a 0.32 surface under a 0.1 ambient came to 0.011 where the game's is 0.032, and the shadows went black. The way back for the screen stays the sRGB curve, which is the screen's own.) Bevy's exposure is at EV100 0 (1/1.2) and its diffuse light has a 1/π the game's has not, so:

- **the sun**: a directional light of colour the linear of the game's colour times intensity, per channel, at an illuminance of π × 1.2, with the range's shadows (`RangeSun`, the light the effects take their way to the sun from);
- **the ambient**: an environment map (`EnvironmentMapLight`, intensity 1.2) whose every direction holds the linear of what the game's surface pass gives a surface facing that way (the harmonics taken to gamma, above; 32 square, half floats; Bevy reads an environment map's cube by the world's way with z turned round, which is the game's own way); its reflections the same cube at six levels; Bevy's own flat ambient none. (Until 0.70.1 the harmonics' sums were taken as gamma figures, three to ten times too dim: the faces in shade came out black.) Not built: the pass's reflection (nearly nothing) and its brightness-squared term (under 0.003 for a mid-grey);
- **the sky** (`tod_sky_view`, `tod_sky.wgsl`; 0.71.0): Customs' own atmosphere mesh (`Icosphere` of `sharedassets15`: 2,562 vertices, radius one, read by the engine's order of a mesh's fields, `trp_data::screen::build_mesh`) drawn as the game's atmosphere shader draws it: **each vertex turned by the view alone and put at the far plane** (the game's vertex shader takes no matrix of the object's, only the view's turn and the projection with its depth at the far end: the dome's own place, the world's middle on Customs, does not matter, and the scene covers the sky), the scattering worked out at the vertices with the four samples and the rest at each pixel, as the game's; the sun's disc in the same draw at each pixel, where the way meets the sun's quad (the game's `Quad`, −1 to 1, scaled by 2 tan(2° × `MeshSize`), square to the sun at one), added under the atmosphere as the game adds the atmosphere over it; the result made linear by the 2.2 power. The camera clears to black, as the game's. No depth written, no shadow. Until 0.70.1, with Factory, the dome's cubemap as Bevy's skybox, at a brightness of 1.2 times the linear of 0.48, turned about up by 190°.

## Tarkov's surface shading (built 2026-10-20, 0.84.0)

The user, of the next step put after the light was checked against the game's ("double check our light system with tarkovs. If everything checks out, lets continue with whatever is next"): "Port Tarkov's shading (Recommended)", whose words were "Tarkov's deferred lighting, done its way in gamma for every surface (sun, sky ambient, lamps, the explosion's lights), with Bevy's as a fallback switch. **Whose.** The game renders deferred, by its own copy of the engine's light shader, `Hidden/Internal-DeferredShadingEFT` (`globalgamemanagers.assets` 18, which the build's graphics settings name for the deferred mode). Each surface's shader writes the G-buffer: its colour (its occlusion in the alpha), its specular colour and smoothness, its normal, and into the light buffer its emission and any ambient of its own. Each light then adds itself to the light buffer; then the ambient pass (`Hidden/WriteScreenAmbient`, "The ambient" above) and the highlight ("The ambient highlight"). The project is in gamma space: every figure is the number stored or set, none is made linear, and the sums are of those numbers.

**One light** (disassembled: the sun's variant with its cookie and the screen's shadows, `b99`; a point light's, `b91`; a spot light's, `b93`; in `local\shaders_deferred_s102`). With c the G-buffer's colour (held from nought to one), s its specular colour, g its smoothness, p = 1 − g and a = max(p², 0.002); n the normal, v the way to the eye, l the way to the light, h the way halfway (v + l over its length, its length's square held at 0.001 at least); n·l, n·h and l·h held from nought to one, and n·v taken whole, without its sign:

- the diffuse: c × the light × n·l × (1 + (f − 1)(1 − n·l)⁵)(1 + (f − 1)(1 − n·v)⁵), with f = ½ + 2(l·h)²p (Disney's, without its 1/π);
- the specular: the light × √max(π·D·V, 10⁻⁴) × n·l × (s + (1 − s)(1 − l·h)⁵), with D = a²/(π(((n·h)²(a² − 1) + 1)² + 10⁻⁷)) (GGX's) and V = ½ / (n·l(n·v(1 − a) + a) + n·v(n·l(1 − a) + a) + 10⁻⁵) (the joint Smith visibility as the engine approximates it); none where s is black. The square root is the engine's gamma-space form of its standard light;
- the light: its colour times intensity (`_LightColor`), times, for the sun, its cookie's alpha (`_LightTexture0`, by `unity_WorldToLight`: the clouds' shadows, "Customs' clouds") and the screen's shadow (`_ShadowMapTexture`); for a point light, the engine's attenuation (below) at the distance squared over the range squared (`_LightPos.w`); for a spot, that times its cookie's alpha (by its cone), and nought behind it;
- a light of pure red (neither green nor blue): its specular left out and the colour held to 0.4, and with the x of a global under one, nothing. No light of Customs' or the app's is red.

**The engine's attenuation** (its texture `UnityAttenuation`, made at the start: `BuildAttenuationTexture` and `LightAttenuation`, read with Ghidra, `local\ghidra_atten_dec_s103.txt`): 1,024 texels; the i-th holds 1/(1 + 25u) at u = i/1,024, the distance squared over the range squared, times 1 − (u − 0.64)/0.36 from u = 0.64 on (past four fifths of the range), and nought at the last. So a light is halved a fifth of its range out, a seventeenth at four fifths, and fades to nought at the range.

**The ambient pass** adds, after the lights: the colour times the harmonics at the normal taken to gamma (an indoor volume's alpha taking them away: "The range hall" in "On the range"); a reflection (not built: nearly nothing, "The ambient"); and, indoors and out, the colour's brightness squared (by the weights 0.2126, 0.7152, 0.0722) times 0.05 (1 + max(1.5 × the sky's fogginess, night vision)) times one less the sun's height (`_SunDirection`'s, either way: the sun's way turned round, which `RippleController` hands the shaders; recorded on Customs as (0.845, −0.525, 0.098)). **In the app** (`shading.rs`, `game_shading.wgsl`, `trp_sim::shading`; the switch `TARKOV SHADING`, on, with `CUSTOMS LIGHT`, restart required). ## Customs' clouds (built 2026-10-19, 0.82.0)

The user, of Customs' light: "clouds and their shadows after", then, of the next three pieces of work: "1, 2, then 3 easy peasy. Goa head" (the clouds first, the moon with them).

**Whose.** Customs' weather object carries `EFT.Rendering.Clouds.CloudController`: the game's port of HDRP's cloud layer to Unity's built-in pipeline, one layer of it. Not `Time of Day`'s own cloud shaders, which the level does not use. Its settings are an asset of the level's shared assets (`CloudLayerSettings_main`), the weather's remap of its cloudiness another (`CloudRemapData_main`, the weather controller's `CloudsRemap`), the map `CloudMapWoHorizonBorder2` (2048 by 1024, RGBA at eight bits; across repeated, up clamped, blended). The renderer's compute shaders `BakeCloudTexture` and `BakeCloudShadows` and its draw `Hidden/HDRP/Sky/CloudLayer` are in `resources.assets`, disassembled; HDRP's published shaders of the same names were read beside them, and the game's differ where said below.

**The figures** (`trp_data::clouds`, `trp_sim::clouds`):

- **The settings**: the layer's opacity 1; the shadows' multiplier 109 (the bake takes an eighth of it), their tint a grey of 0.2264, the cookie 500 m across; the planet's radius 6,704,000 m (`PlanetCenterRadius.w`, which the game's shaders take where HDRP's have a fixed 6,378,100); the layer's bottom 6,000 m up and 800 m thick (the shader's own), turned 191°; its tint white, at an exposure of 2.1 EV taken off (`ConvertEv100ToExposure`, with the lens's scale of 1.2: times 3.5726); the wind's way 180°, its speeds 20 to 100 km/h; lit through; its density 1 (an extinction figure of 1 × 0.095 + 0.005); the ambient's dimmer 0.675.
- **The quality** (the graphics setting `CLOUDS QUALITY`, `CloudQualityPreset`): the cloud texture 2048 across at each choice, the cookie 128, 256 or 512 square and the light's steps through the layer 5, 7 or 12 at LOW, MEDIUM and HIGH; each casts shadows. The user's game: LOW.
- **The map's channels** (`WeatherController.ControlClouds`, each frame): each channel's opacity from the remap's curve for it at the weather's cloudiness taken from −1 to 1 onto 0 to 1. At the recorded morning's −0.1949: 0.883, 0.722, 0.438, 0.016.
- **The wind's speed**: the weather's wind's magnitude (0.378 recorded) times the wind controller's multiplier for the clouds (2, `Weather/WindZone`'s), halved, between the two speeds: 50.3 km/h. The weather's wind's way is not taken (the layer keeps its own). The scroll grows by the speed times the frame's time times 0.277778, metres a second, from nought.
- **The sun's colour for the clouds**: the tint times that exposure times the light's colour made linear (Unity's sRGB curve) times its intensity: (2.31, 1.70, 0.61) at 09:37.
- **The ambient for the clouds** (`CloudController.UpdateAmbient`): the time-of-day controller's harmonics (`ToDController.SH`, the ambient's own) with the controller's gradient's colour added evenly (by the sun's height: half its way's height plus a half); the draw takes them at straight down (the buffer is packed as the shaders' usual harmonics), to gamma by the sRGB curve (the project is in gamma), at least nought, times the dimmer: (0.44, 0.48, 0.50) at 09:37.

**The cloud texture** (`BakeCloudTexture`): the sky above the horizon in lat-long, as wide as the quality says and half as high, two figures a texel (half floats). Baked again only when the settings change or the light turns by three degrees or more (`GetBakingHashCode`). A texel's coordinates are its own across less the layer's turn and its own up doubled; the map's opacity there is its four channels weighed by their opacities, each over their sum where that is above one. Where the layer is lit through and that opacity is not nought:

- the texel's way (the inverse of the lat-long), the way to the sun turned about up by the layer's turn, and two phases of Henyey and Greenstein's for it (forward 0.7 and back 0.3, and three quarters of each for a second octave);
- where the way meets the layer, from a ground at the planet's radius, its bottom (the planet's radius plus the altitude) and its top (800 m more), and the place half way into the part of that range the opacity fills, counted from the top;
- the light lost through the layer there: e to the minus a tenth of the opacity squared times the extinction figure times the range;
- toward the sun from the place to the layer's top, at most 500 m, in the quality's steps, the layer's density at each (the map's opacity at that place's own way; a tenth of it inside the part of the layer that opacity fills, else nought), summed;
- the light that comes through toward the sun at both octaves (the second at three quarters of the extinction, weighed by 0.75), each times its phase;
- the texel holds that light times one less the loss, and one less the loss. Where the layer is not lit through it holds the opacity twice; where the opacity is nought, nought.

The ways' coordinates are worked out with HDRP's quick arc tangent and arc cosine (their polynomials, as the game's shaders have them), turned into coordinates by 0.1591 and 0.3183, not by 1/2π and 1/π.

**The shadows** (`BakeCloudShadows`, each frame, before the G-buffer): a square of the quality's; each texel a way near the way to the sun, its middle the sun's and its edges 0.2 of the light's right across and 0.2 of its up down from it; the layer's opacity along that way: none below the horizon, else, where the way meets the layer's bottom, two samples of the cloud texture's opacity there scrolled by the wind as the draw's (below), times the layer's opacity. The square's last quarter across and up is blended by a smoothstep toward the samples a whole square back, so that it tiles. The texel's light is one plus the tint's luminance less the opacity times the multiplier's eighth, each held between nought and one: one under a clear sky, 0.2264 under a cloud. It is the sun's cookie (`Cloud Shadows`, the light's `cookie` and `cookieSize` 500 set each frame), laid on the ground by the light's own axes from the light's place: the engine's `CalculateLightMatrix` for a directional light with a cookie (read with Ghidra) is a half plus the place's distance from the light's along its right and its up, over the cookie's size, repeated. The deferred light takes the cookie's alpha (the same figure) times its shadow.

**The draw** (`CloudLayer`'s second pass, in the controller's command buffer after the opaque scene, `AfterForwardOpaque`, before the opaque image effects): a triangle over the screen at the far plane, drawn where the scene's depth is the far plane (the sky: the dome writes no depth), each pixel's way from the camera; above the horizon only. ## The range's own light by the hour (built 2026-10-25, 0.121.0)

The user, 2026-10-25, of a weapon's light that barely showed by day: "lets do the hour option, can we put a slider in-game so we can change the time at any time? place it inside features." **The slider**: `TIME OF DAY`, on the options' `FEATURES` page under the panel (`options_view`, `Slide::Hour`), 00:00 to 24:00 by five minutes, with a `DEFAULT` button beside it. **Not kept** (since 0.122.0; the user, 2026-10-25: "can we make a "default" button next to the time slider, and also make 09:30 the default so the app always starts at that time"): every run starts at `lighting::RANGE_MOMENT`'s hour, 09:30 (the recorded morning's weather at 09:30: 7 minutes before the recorded 09:37, at which Customs' light is compared), or at `--hour`'s; `DEFAULT` puts the bar back at 09:30. With `CUSTOMS LIGHT` on there is no bar: Customs' light is worked out once as the run begins (above, "On the range"), at that hour, and the page says which. With it off, the bar is taken at once (`range_day`):

- **The rule**: the range's own light (the app's, not the game's: a sun of 12,000 lux, an ambient of 400, a clear colour of (0.55, 0.63, 0.70); `lighting::RANGE_OWN_*`) is what it was at 09:30 (09:37 in 0.121.0) times what Customs' changes by between 09:30 and the hour, by the game's own rules (`trp_sim::tod`, Customs' sky as read, at `RANGE_MOMENT`'s cloudiness): the sun's colour and strength by Customs' light (its colour times its intensity, linear) channel by channel, its strength by the most changed; the ambient by Customs' ambient colour the same way; the clear colour by Customs' sky at the horizon. The sun turns as Customs' light turns between the two hours (the moon's by night, as the game's light is), from the range's own sun's way. So at 09:30 the range is as before to the figure.
- **The night**: Customs' figures alone leave the night far too bright for the range's camera (at midnight a 4,000 lux moon), since the game's camera adapts its exposure to the dark and the range's own does not. Each of the three is faded by the day's share (`LerpValue`: 0 with the sun 20° under the horizon, 1 from 10° over it) to `NIGHT_SHARE` of itself at full night (`ESTIMATE`, 5%: chosen so that a weapon's light shows), as a share of the fade at 09:37.
- **What is drawn alone**: nothing of the simulation goes by it.

**The devices' lights at the range's own exposure**: with `CUSTOMS LIGHT` off the camera keeps Bevy's own exposure (EV100 9.7, `Exposure::EV100_BLENDER`) while a weapon's light (and an effect's) is worked out for the game's light's (EV100 0), so it came 2^9.7 (about 830) times too dim to see. A device's light is multiplied by that difference with the range's own light (`tactical_view::exposure_gain`; `tactical-devices.md`): what a lit surface takes is then what it would be by the game's light. The effects' own lights (the explosion's, the muzzle's) are not yet (`findings.md`, 0.121.0).

**Seen** (`local\shots\hour_light_day_s128.png`, `hour_light_22_s128.png`, `hour_light_0_s128.png`, a light on): 09:37 as before (the sun 12,000 lux, the ambient 400); 22:00 a dusk, the sky a dark red (Customs' horizon after sunset), the light's pool on the floor and the table plain; midnight dark, the moon 204 lux and the ambient 3.2, the sky black, the light's cone and its picture plain.
