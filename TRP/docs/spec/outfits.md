# Outfits from another game: the Sunrise and the SEVA suits

A body of S.T.A.L.K.E.R.'s, worn as an item in the armour's slot, drawn in place of the character's own with its own build and moved by Tarkov's animation (the user, 2026-10-13: "add an outfit in the chest that goes into armor slot that replaces the player model when equipped, with one from stalker gamma. See if you can find the Sunrise suit"). It is the first thing of the other game's in the runtime, and it is the direction settled on 2026-10-11 the other way about: there, Tarkov's motion on that game's own arms and weapons; here, that game's body on Tarkov's motion.

**Three lie in the crate** (the user, 2026-10-13, late: "Add another suit of your choosing", and "Can we import a model from tarkov that has bare hands and we can compare?"): the Sunrise suit, the SEVA suit, and **the Telnik**, which is not the other game's at all: one of Tarkov's own tops with Tarkov's own bare hands, worn the same way, to set the other two beside ("An outfit of the install's own", below).

**Whatever an outfit has not got to draw is the character's own** (the user: "The default tarkov body and gloves should always be the fall-back"): a body that is not named, not found among the other game's files (loose, or packed since 2026-10-14), not read, or with none of its pictures leaves Tarkov's default body; arms for first person so missing leave Tarkov's default arms. Each is said once when the program starts.

**An outfit's arms for first person go by a switch of the options** (the user, 2026-10-13, night: "add an option for 'Custom First Person Hands' which can be enabled or disabled to not change with body-changing outfits. The fall back should always be the defualt tarkov outfit"): `CUSTOM FIRST PERSON HANDS`, on the options tab's `FEATURES` part, on unless switched (`settings.md`, "Features"). Off, the hands do not change with what is worn: they are Tarkov's default arms, the suit's body is still the body from outside and the legs under the eyes, and the Telnik's bare hands are not shown either.

**Nothing of the other game is in the repository.** The model and its picture are read where they lie in the user's own install of GAMMA and Anomaly, every time the program starts (`local\gamma_dir.txt`, `local\anomaly_dir.txt`, or `GAMMA_DIR` and `ANOMALY_DIR`); those folders are read and never written. Without them the item is still in the crate and nothing is drawn for it.

**Compared with nothing: neither game shows this.** What is checked is the reader against the file itself and the fit against the two skeletons (at the end).

## The model's format

The engine's source is published (OpenXRay, `xray-16`), and the reader is written from it: `xrCore/FMesh.hpp` (the chunks and the vertex formats), `Layers/xrRender/SkeletonX.cpp` (skinned vertices), `Layers/xrRender/SkeletonCustom.cpp` and `xrCore/Animation/Bone.cpp` (bones), `utils/xrMiscMath/matrix.cpp` (a bone's turn).

- **A file is chunks**: a number, a length, a body; a chunk may hold chunks. Of a model with a skeleton: 1 the header (its first byte the version, 4), 9 the meshes (one chunk each), 13 the bones' names (each with its parent's name and a box of 60 bytes), 16 the bones at rest.
- **A mesh** has 2 its picture's name (and its shader's), 3 its vertices, 4 its triangles (a count and that many 16-bit numbers), and 6, where it is kept at several levels of detail, which run of the triangles is the fullest.
- **A vertex** goes with one to four bones; the format is named by that count, or by 1, 2, 4 or 5 times `0x12071980`. With one: place, normal, tangent, binormal, picture's two coordinates, the bone. With more: the bones (16-bit each) first, then place, normal, tangent, binormal, the shares of all bones but the last (which has the rest; with two bones the one share kept is the second's), the coordinates. 76 bytes with four bones, 70 with three: the Sunrise's body is 3857 vertices of the first, its rucksack 277 of the second.
- **A bone at rest** is three angles and a place in its parent's axes, after a version, a material's name, a shape of 112 bytes and a joint of 72 or 76. The angles make its axes by the engine's `setXYZi`: the heading, pitch and bank are minus the angles about y, x and z. The engine goes down from the root (`CalculateM2B`). **Checked on the file**: the 47 bones so placed lie inside the mesh, each at the near end of the vertices that go with it, the left side's the mirror of the right's.
- **The space** is Unity's own kind: left-handed, x to the right, y up, z ahead, metres. The model faces along z, stands on y = 0 and is 1.85 m tall, its arms out to the sides.
- **A picture** is a `.dds` file (Microsoft's): 128 bytes of header (148 where it says `DX10`), then every level. The block packings are given to the graphics card as they are.

## The fit: the model keeps its own build

The model's skeleton is not Tarkov's: 47 bones for Tarkov's 60 or so, its spine's joints at other heights, three fingers to a hand, no bones for the turn along a forearm or a thigh, and it stands with its arms out where Tarkov's clothing is made with its arms forward. And it is another build: as tall, but a quarter broader at the hips and the shoulders, with a shorter thigh and its ankle higher over the sole (`findings.md` has the two side by side).

**The first fit moved the model onto Tarkov's own joints**, stretching each bone to Tarkov's length and narrowing the trunk to Tarkov's hips and shoulders. It held together and looked wrong: a slim figure, and fingers pulled half as long again (the user, the same night: "I'd rather keep body proportions. Also, the fingers are mangled", and asked whether the animation could be shrunk to the model instead). **It is done that way now: nothing of the model is stretched or narrowed.** The model is only brought into the pose Tarkov's clothing is made in, each piece turned at its own joint, and it is drawn on a skeleton that has Tarkov's bones at the model's own joints (the next part).

**Tarkov's skeleton at rest** is read off the character's own clothing: each mesh keeps, for each bone it hangs on, the matrix that takes a point of the mesh into the bone's axes, and the bone's joint is the point that matrix takes to nothing. Tarkov's meshes are z up and face along minus y. Each skeleton's right, up and ahead are the axes of its own space nearest to hip-to-hip and hips-to-head.

**At rest the model stands as it is**: on Tarkov's ground at its own height, the middle of its hips over the middle of Tarkov's. Its trunk, its head, its collarbones and the joints of its shoulders and hips are where that puts them. Then, for each limb's bone, a turn at its own joint (never a stretch), with a second direction to settle the turn about the bone:

| The model's | Turned to lie as Tarkov's | The second direction |
|---|---|---|
| upper arm, forearm | from its own shoulder to Tarkov's wrist, bent at the elbow its own two lengths give, to the side Tarkov's arm is bent | the elbow's axis (at right angles to both bones) |
| hand | `Palm`: **knuckle on knuckle**, its largest finger's on `Digit31`, lying as Tarkov's hand lies; its wrist wherever that leaves it | from the largest finger's knuckle to the first finger's |
| thumb, first finger, the finger for the other three | `Digit1x`, `Digit2x`, `Digit3x`: each joint where the piece before carries it, along Tarkov's joint | the hand's; a last joint as the one before it |
| thigh, calf | from its own hip to its own ankle over Tarkov's, bent at the knee its own two lengths give | the knee's axis |

- **A forearm is twisted towards the hand.** Tarkov's hand at rest is turned about the forearm from where the model's is (the palm half inward, as it holds a rifle), so what lies along the forearm is turned about it by a share of that angle, none at the elbow and all at the wrist.
- **The foot stands as it is**, its sole on Tarkov's ground and its ankle over Tarkov's ankle (the two skeletons have that joint 13 cm and 6 cm over the sole). The toes go with the foot.
- **What goes with the trunk** is shared among Tarkov's `Pelvis`, `Spine1`, `Spine2`, `Spine3` and `Neck` by its height: wholly a bone's at the middle of that bone, evenly between two middles. Along a forearm and a thigh the share goes the same way to Tarkov's three and two bones.

**Three lists of triangles**: what lies under the waist, which the character's own eyes see as they see Tarkov's legs; the arms (by the bones a triangle mostly goes with), and the rest. The last two are in first person a shadow and no more, as Tarkov's upper body is.

**The waist is Tarkov's** (the user: "The first person body of the stalker suit should be clipped from the waist down just like tarkov's (can be seen when sprinting and looking straight down)"). The game draws the character's own body, for its own eyes, as the mesh of the legs and no more, and that mesh ends at the waist: on the default legs, 1.082 m up at rest, 12.0 cm over the two thighs' joints. The suit is cut as far over its own thighs' joints (1.107 m on the Sunrise, whose hips stand 2.5 cm higher): a triangle with all three corners at or under that is seen, and any other is not, whatever bones it goes with. **By the bones it had been otherwise**: everything that went mostly with the model's hip bone was seen, the lower front of the jacket with it, which reaches well over the waist. (The hips are the thighs' joints because the two skeletons have the bone they call the pelvis at other heights in the body.)

## The rig: Tarkov's animation on the model's own lengths

The outfit is drawn on **a rig of its own** (`trp_data::outfit::Rig`): Tarkov's bones, each with the joint the model has for it. Every frame the rig is posed from Tarkov's skeleton as it then stands (`Rig::pose`), and the meshes go by the rig. Nothing of this is the simulation's: it is how its result is drawn.

For every bone, what is on it is turned exactly as Tarkov's bone turns what is on its own. Where the joint is, goes by one of five rules:

| A bone of the rig | follows |
|---|---|
| the hips; a hand; a foot | **Tarkov's own bone**: the hips carry the trunk, a hand is on the weapon where Tarkov's is, a foot on the ground where Tarkov's is |
| the spine, the neck, the head, a collarbone, a finger's joint, the toes | **hangs** at its own joint from the bone before it, turned as Tarkov's is turned |
| an upper arm and a forearm; a thigh and a calf | **a limb of two**: it hangs where the model's own shoulder or hip then is and reaches for Tarkov's hand or foot with the model's own two lengths, bent to the side Tarkov's own limb is bent. Each of the two is Tarkov's turn and then the least further turn that brings it there |
| the bones along a forearm and a thigh | hang on it, with Tarkov's turn and then the limb's |

So the trunk keeps its breadth and its height, the shoulders are the model's (broader, and higher under the head), the hand is still exactly on the weapon and the foot on the ground, and an elbow or a knee is a little elsewhere than Tarkov's, as it has to be for other lengths. **At rest the rig moves nothing**: the fit bends each limb by the same rule. Out of reach (a hand further off than the model's arm is long), the limb is straight and ends short.

**Why knuckle on knuckle.** A hand is on a weapon by its knuckles and its fingers, and the two skeletons do not have the wrist's joint at the same place in the hand (wrist to the middle knuckle: 10.9 cm on Tarkov's, 10.1 on the body's model, 7.0 on the arms' model for first person, whose joint lies two centimetres inside the palm). Laid wrist on wrist, the smaller hand's knuckles are short of the grip by that difference; laid knuckle on knuckle, they are on it. (The arms' model for first person is laid both ways at once since 2026-10-13, night: by its knuckles, at the size that has them as far apart as Tarkov's, and with its wrist taken where Tarkov's is and not where its own joint is: "The arms for first person".)

**The height.** The hips are Tarkov's bone, with the model standing 2.5 cm higher on them than Tarkov's own figure (its legs are that much longer to the sole); its head's joint is then at 1.656 m for Tarkov's 1.658, and the eye, which is the simulation's, is where it was.

**The squeeze.** In first person the game squeezes the arms along the view with the weapon. What Tarkov squeezes (the arms' bones, the collarbones) is taken out of the squeeze before the rig is posed, so that its lengths are true lengths, and put back into it after.

## The arms for first person

The other game shows the player a model of arms alone, finer than the body's (9,000 vertices for two arms, a thumb and four fingers to a hand, the bare fingers a mesh of their own), and so does this, **in first person and nowhere else**: from outside, and for the shadow, the arms are the body's own. **Without that model** (not named in the outfit's file, not found, or with a picture missing) **first person shows Tarkov's own default arms**, and the suit's body is seen from outside all the same.

- **Its skeleton** is two arms from a root: a collarbone, an upper arm, a forearm with a bone more near the wrist for its turn, a hand, and five fingers of three joints (the thumb's bones are `l_finger0`, `01`, `02`; the others `bip01_l_finger1` to `4` with `1` and `2` after). It stands with its upper arms down, its forearms ahead and its thumbs up.
- **Its fingers are Tarkov's fingers.** Every joint of every finger, the thumb's too, is put on Tarkov's own joint at rest, and each piece of a finger is made as long as Tarkov's (and no broader) so that the next joint falls on the next. Every hold of Tarkov's weapons is a pose of those joints, and a finger whose joints are elsewhere, turned by the same angles, ends elsewhere, the further the more it is bent. **The thumb showed it** (the user: "the thumb on the stalker hand usually isn't sitting right"): the model's thumb begins 2.3 cm from where Tarkov's does and its first bone is 5.5 cm for Tarkov's 4.0, so about a grip it lay up to two centimetres off. The first finger's first joint is made 1.23 times as long and its second 0.86; the middle finger's first 1.09; the thumb's first 0.73. **This is the one place a piece of the model is stretched**, and only along its bone. The body's own hands, seen from outside, are left as they are.
- **It is not drawn at its file's size.** The other game draws these arms in a space of their own with a field of view of their own, so the file's size is no size in the world. **The whole model is made the size at which its hand's skeleton is Tarkov's hand's**: the knuckles' joints as far apart, the first finger's from the little finger's (5.9 cm in the file, 7.5 on Tarkov's skeleton: **1.27 times the file's size**, for the Sunrise's arms and the SEVA's alike, which have one skeleton). Its fingers are as long as Tarkov's at any size (the bullet before), so this is the size at which the palm, the wrist and the arm are in the model's own proportion to those fingers. `arms_size` in the outfit's file says another size outright.
- **Three sizes were tried before, and two of them were too small.** At the file's own size (1.0) the fingers ended 1.4 to 2.6 cm short of Tarkov's (the user: "the stalker hands are too small ... Their positions dont match at all"). At 1.27 by the joints nothing was said of the size; the thumb was (the bullet before). **Then the size was taken down to 1.12, and that was this work's own doing, not the user's word**: with Tarkov's bare hands beside it the glove looked bulky, and a measure was found to say so, the hand's reach on the little finger's side alone. That measure was wrong: this model's palm lies most of a centimetre to that side of Tarkov's (0.4 cm short of Tarkov's glove on the first finger's side and 1.2 over on the little finger's, at 1.27), so one edge said "an eighth too broad" of a hand that from edge to edge is a twelfth broader. And at 1.12, with fingers made as long as Tarkov's, the palm and the wrist were a ninth too small for their own fingers, which is what was seen (the user, 2026-10-13, night, with six captures side by side: "All the stalker models seem to be incorrectly proportioned. Their wrists are too small and their palms aren't wide enough").
- **Its hand is laid by the whole row of its knuckles** (`lay_arm`): the middle of the line from the first finger's knuckle to the little finger's on the middle of Tarkov's, that line along Tarkov's, and the hand turned about it towards Tarkov's wrist. At the joints' size both those knuckles are then on Tarkov's own. (Laid by the middle knuckle with the first finger's beside it, as the body's three-fingered hand still is, whatever the two rows of knuckles differ in was all at the little finger.)
- **It bends at Tarkov's wrist.** The model has its wrist's joint 2.0 cm nearer the knuckles than Tarkov's skeleton has its own (8.9 cm from the middle knuckle at its size, for 10.9), inside the palm, and its skin bends behind that joint, about where Tarkov's wrist is. The wrist is therefore taken where the laying has Tarkov's own: the forearm comes away from Tarkov's wrist along Tarkov's forearm and is turned about that point. **Turned about the model's own joint, as it was until 2026-10-13, night, the forearm left Tarkov's by as much as the wrist was bent**: 1.5 cm at thirty degrees, 3 cm at sixty, which is the thin wrist the user saw in a hand holding a magazine out.
- **Its arms are longer than Tarkov's** (the forearm 31.6 cm for 25.1, the upper arm 34.4 for 29.7, at its size): the elbow is 6.5 cm from where Tarkov's is and the shoulder 10.7, both out of the picture.
- **Its rig goes back from the hand** (`Follows::Held`). The hand is Tarkov's own bone; the forearm is held at Tarkov's wrist and turned as Tarkov's forearm is turned, and the upper arm is held at the elbow and turned as Tarkov's. So in the picture the arm comes in exactly as Tarkov's own arm comes in, at its own length, and its shoulder is wherever that leaves it, which is out of the picture. (Hung from Tarkov's shoulder instead, as the body's arm hangs from its own, an arm of another length is too short to reach the hand or bent double.)
- **What is left, measured and not changed** (`palm_box`; "Checked"): at that size the Sunrise's palm is 11.1 cm from edge to edge behind the knuckles for Tarkov's glove's 10.2 and Tarkov's bare hand's 10.1 (1.09 times), and the SEVA's, a thicker glove on the same skeleton, 12.0 (1.18 times); both lie towards the little finger's edge and the back of the hand from Tarkov's, by most of a centimetre; the Sunrise's bare fingers are an eighth thicker than Tarkov's bare ones and a tenth thinner than its gloved ones (the skin's mean distance from a finger's bone, about the middle of the bone: a session's scratch measure, `local\hands_explore_s45.txt`). **A move of the palm's skin onto the box Tarkov's palm fills was built and taken out the same night**: it put the palm right from behind and a centimetre and a half too far out in front, and left a step where the thumb meets the palm. The two hands are not one shape, and joints on joints is what holds the weapon where Tarkov's hand holds it.
- **In first person** these arms are drawn and the body's own arms are a shadow and no more, like the rest of the upper body. They cast no shadow themselves.

## An outfit of the install's own: the Telnik

To judge another game's hands on Tarkov's weapons one needs Tarkov's own hands beside them, and bare ones, where the fingers show (the default arms are gloved). `custom\outfits\telnik.json` names two bundles of the install's own: `their_top`, the BEAR's striped top (`tshirt_bear_telnik`), and `their_hands`, its arms for first person (`hands_bear_telnik`: bare hands, a watch). Worn, the top is drawn in place of the character's own top, legs and head staying, and the hands in place of the default arms in first person. **Nothing is fitted**: they are Tarkov's models on Tarkov's skeleton, as the game draws them. Which of the install's 150 or so hands are bare was told by their pictures (`probe texstats <bundle> <word> <folder>` writes them out to look at).

## The item

`custom\outfits\*.json`: an id, a name, the armour of the install's whose item it is (`donor`), and what it has to draw: the body's model by its path under the other game's `meshes` (`visual`, the other game's `actor_visual`), its arms' for first person (`arms`, with `arms_size` to say their size outright) and its model of the item as it lies in the world, which its picture in the inventory is drawn from (`world_visual`, the other game's `visual`: `icons.md`), or the install's own bundles (`their_top`, `their_hands`). It is the donor's template under its own name and with none of its slots (an armour's are for plates and soft inserts, and it may not be put on without its inserts): it goes where armour goes, takes the donor's cells and weighs what the donor's shell weighs. Every file in the folder is an item in the crate. **Its figures are the donor's, not the other game's**: the PACA's 3.5 kg and nine cells (`PROVISIONAL`; the one file of the other game's read for the Sunrise has 6.2 kg, and its protection is a system not built).
