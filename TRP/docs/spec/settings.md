# Settings: the keys, the display, and the options tab

How the game hears its keys and lets them be changed, which of its display settings the app takes, and the tab the app keeps them on. Read from the game's code on 2026-10-12 (`InputSystem`, `Settings.Control`, `Settings.Graphics`, `UI.Settings`, `MoveInputTranslator`, `FirearmHandsInputTranslator`, `PlayerInputTranslator`, `Player`), from the install's own settings (`SPT_Runtime\user\sptSettings\Control.ini`, `Graphics.ini`, `Game.ini`, each a page of JSON), from the build's input settings (`EscapeFromTarkov_Data\globalgamemanagers`) and from the scene that holds the game's settings screen (`level44`). **The keys' timing has been set beside seventeen recordings made by hand** (below, "Compared with what"): the five bindings whose doing shows in a recording all come on the game's own frame. The settings screen itself, and everything of the mouse, is from the code and the install alone. What is built, and what was run, is at the end.

## Where the game keeps them, and where the app does

The game's settings are a screen of its menu (`SettingsScreen`) with five tabs: Game, Graphics, PostFX, Sound, Controls. **TRP has them inside the inventory screen, as a tab at its far right.** That is the app's arrangement, by choice; what the tab holds is the game's wherever the game has it. It has three parts:

- **Controls**: the game's bindings, changed by the game's rules (below).
- **Graphics**: four of the game's settings: VSync, the field of view, the resolution, and how the window is shown.
- **Features**: a list of the systems a host might switch on and off. **A list only: nothing of it is switched**. **Over the list, what is switched: three things**, each by choice and each taking effect when the app is next started ("Features", below).

The install is never written to. What is changed is kept in the app's own file, `local\options.json`; without it the app starts from the install's bindings, as before.

## A binding

A binding (`KeyGroup`) is:

- the game's name for what it does (`EGameKey`: `Shoot`, `ReloadWeapon`, `LeanLockLeft`);
- up to two **variants** (`InputSource`), each a set of keys held together, which may end in a notch of the mouse wheel one way (`Mouse Scroll`, the only axis a binding may take);
- **how it is pressed** (`EPressType`, in this order): `Release`, `Continuous`, `Press`, `DoubleClick`.

Seven **axes** are bound beside them (`AxisGroup`: `MoveX`, `MoveY`, `TurnX`, `TurnY`, `LookX`, `LookY`, `LeanX`), each with two pairs of a positive and a negative variant. The mouse's four are not shown to be changed; the keys push the other three.

## How a key becomes a command (`InputManager`, `InputPreset`, `InputKeyCombination`)

The manager makes one **combination** for every variant that has keys, and runs them all once a frame.

**A key's own state** on a frame is none, going down, held or coming up, from whether it is down and what it was. A notch of the wheel is a key that is down for the frame it turns in. Several keys together are none if any is none, else coming up if any is, else going down if any is, else held.

**The order.** Combinations with more keys run first; among those with as many, the double clicks first. A combination that acts **takes** its keys, for as long as they are held or for the one frame. A combination finding one of its keys taken gives way if the taker:

- is marked as never giving way itself (the aim's while it is bound `Continuous`; the tactical device's release);
- else, has the very same keys and is a double click (so a plain press waits for the double click to fail);
- else, where both have a rank of their own, has the lower rank (`CheckFireMode` 10, `FoldStock` 11, `CheckAmmo` 12, `CheckChamber` 13, `ChamberUnload` 14, `UnloadMagazine` 15; blind fire's two 99);
- else, has as many keys or more.

The sprint's combination never gives way (rank -2). An axis keeps its keys from blind fire's combinations.

This is what makes `T` with `Left Alt` the look at the magazine and not also the tactical device's `T`, and `E` with `Left Alt` the step aside and not the lean.

**How each way of pressing is heard:**

- `Press`: the command comes on the frame the keys go down (all held, the last one going down).
- `Release`: the keys going down are noted, and the command comes on the frame they come up, if the combination still has them. If a combination of *other* keys took one of them meanwhile, the release is not heard at all.
- `Continuous`, for a binding that **holds** (`ToggleInputKeyCombination`: the trigger, the aim, the sprint, the breath, the walk, the vault, the crouch, the two leans, the two steps aside, blind fire, interacting and a few of the voice's): the command comes as the keys go down, and its second command, the taking back, as they come up.
- `Continuous`, for any other binding: the command comes once the keys have been held for the double click's time.
- `DoubleClick`: down, up, down and up again, all within the double click's time of the first press (`DoubleClickTimeout`, 0.3 s in the install, 0.3 to 1). If it does not come, the keys are given back *as just pressed*, so that a plain press on the same key is then heard.

**Consequences, all the game's:**

- A key that also has a double click bound makes its plain binding **wait out the double click's time**: `R` let go reloads 0.3 s after it went down, `B` changes the fire mode 0.3 s late, `1` draws the pistol 0.3 s late. A player rids a key of the wait by moving the double click elsewhere.
- **The crouch key used with the wheel does not crouch.** The crouch is on the key's release, and the stance is the same key with the wheel. The manager tells the two apart by all their keys while the crouch is bound `Release`, so the key coming up after a notch is not heard.
- Both ways of an axis held at once push it the positive way (D and A together walk right, W and S forward): the way with more keys is looked at first, the positive one where they have as many.
- With Left Alt held, D and A are the slow lean's and do not walk.

**The wheel** (`InputAxis.Update`; read from the build's input settings on 2026-10-12, night). A binding's notch is the engine axis `Mouse Scroll`, and the game takes it for pressed on a frame whose axis reads **exactly** one that way. The build has that axis as the mouse's third (the wheel) at one a count, so one notch in a frame reads one and is a press, and **two notches in the same frame read two and are none**: a wheel spun fast loses notches, in the game and here (`keys::notch`). It was `PROVISIONAL` before (any turn of the wheel in a frame counted as one notch).

## The mouse (`InputAxis.GetValue`, `MovementState.ClampRotation`, `MoveInputTranslator.TranslateAxes`; read 2026-10-12, night)

The view's turn for a count of the mouse is a product of five figures:

| Figure | Where it is | In the install read |
|---|---|---|
| The engine's own halving of a raw count on Windows | not in the install or the game's code: the engine's (a post by one who read its code, discussions.unity.com/t/812499) | 0.5 |
| The engine axis's own factor (`Mouse X`, `Mouse Y`) | the build's input settings (`probe keys`) | 0.05 |
| The binding's factor (`TurnX`, `TurnY`: `InputSource.sensitivity`) | `Control.ini` | 5 |
| The player's sensitivity (`MouseSensitivity`; with the sights up `MouseAimingSensitivity` times the weapon's own factor) | `Control.ini`, 0.1 to 5 | 0.7, 0.5 |
| The movement state's own factor and limit | the body's animator (`movement.md`, "Looking") | 1 standing |

So **a count is 0.125 degrees before the sensitivity**, which is the figure the community's converters list for the game (csdb.gg). The app had 0.1 as an `ESTIMATE` (the engine's default for an axis, with the binding's 5 left out): it turned a fifth slower than the game. `keys::degrees_a_count` reads the two figures the install has; the engine's halving is a constant, and is the one figure here that is neither read nor in the game's code.

`InvertedXAxis` and `InvertedYAxis` turn the sign of what the mouse gives, across and up and down, for the turn and for the head's free look alike. `OpticSensitivity` (1 to 5) is what a scope's zoom is adjusted by with the wheel (`ScopeZoomHandler`): the wheel's turn times it, since 0.107.0 (`optics.md`, "Zoom and modes"); the inverted up and down turns the zoom about too.

## What the bindings do (`MoveInputTranslator`, `FirearmHandsInputTranslator`, `Player`)

Each binding gives a command, and a binding that holds a second one when it is taken back:

| Binding | Given | Taken back |
|---|---|---|
| `Sprint` | the sprint is switched to the other of on and off (`ToggleSprint`; standing still it does nothing) | the sprint is ended (`EnableSprint(false)`) |
| `LeanLockLeft`, `Right` | leans that way, or straightens if leaning that way already (`ToggleLean`) | straightens, if still leaning that way |
| `StepLeft`, `Right` | steps that way, or steps back if stepped that way | steps back, if stepped that way |
| `Duck` | between crouched and standing, or back to the stance last chosen (`TogglePose`) | a character lower than half way is put back to the stance last chosen, or upright if that was as low (`RestorePose`) |
| `Breath` | the breath is held, or a held one let go | a held breath is let go |
| `Vaulting` | the vault's try is switched over | the same: both do the one thing (`Player.Vaulting`) |
| `Shoot` | the trigger is turned over | the trigger is let go |
| `Aim` | the sights the other way (`ToggleAim`) | the sights down (`SetAim(false)`) |
| `Walk` | the speed put to none and what it was put aside, or taken up again (`ToggleMinSpeed`; `movement.md`, "The walk key") | taken up again (`RestoreSpeed`) |

A binding that holds, bound as a `Press`, gives only its first command: **that is what makes the install's sprint and aim one press on, one press off.** Bound `Continuous`, the same binding is held.

The rest give one command: the jump, lying down, the speed's and the stance's notches (one `DELTA_SPEED` or `DELTA_LEVEL` each, from the game's settings object), the reload, the fire mode, the fullest fire mode, the looks at the weapon (its magazine, its chamber, its selector, all over: `firing.md`, "Three more keys"), the stock's fold, the backpack's drop (`interactions.md`, "A backpack dropped").

**The axes are handed out after the frame's commands** (`InputNode.TranslateInput`): the head's look, the walk, the turn, and last the slow lean, which leans a tenth of a degree a frame (`Player.SlowLean`; `movement.md`, "The slow lean").

## Changing a key (`CommandKeyPair.ListenForKey`, `ControlSettingsTab.KeyChosen`)

**What is shown.** Each axis's two ways, then every binding but the function keys, Escape, Enter and the bindings the game makes out of others (`PressSlot4` and its like, `HighThrow`, `LowThrow`, `PressThrowGrenade`, `ReleaseTactical`, `ChangePointOfView`). A row is the game's words for the binding (the install's texts have them by the binding's own name: `Shoot` is "Fire", `Duck` "Crouch"), its two variants, and how it is pressed.

**Choosing.** A click on a variant listens: "Press any key...". Every key that goes down is added, one a frame, kept in the order of the engine's numbers for them. The choice is made when one of them comes up, or a third goes down, or the wheel turns a notch (not for the trigger). Escape among the keys drops it, and what stood there stays. There is no way to leave a variant empty but to give its keys to something else.

**The same keys on two bindings.** They may stay on both only if both bindings' way of being pressed is the player's to choose and the two are pressed differently (`R` let go and `R` double clicked). Otherwise:

- if either's way is not the player's to choose, or it has a notch of the wheel, **the other loses the keys at once**;
- if both may be chosen and they are pressed alike, **the game asks how the new one is to be pressed** (its list opens, if the binding's other variant is empty); answered the same way, the other loses the keys; answered differently, both keep them. It asks likewise when a binding that had no keys is given some, and sets it to `Release` meanwhile.

The same keys on an axis are dropped there, and keys chosen for an axis are dropped wherever else they stand. The same keys in the binding's own other variant are dropped there.

**How it is pressed** is chosen from the four, where the game allows it. It does not for: the trigger, the head's free look, push to talk, interact, blind fire's two, the screenshot, discard, the voice's switch, the left stance, the tactical device, the grenade. Three have one way only: the vault `Continuous`, the tactical device and the grenade `Press`. A binding with a notch of the wheel is a `Press`. Changing it drops the same keys from any binding now pressed alike.

A quirk kept: two sets of the same keys count as the same only in the same order. The install's file has some of its own in another order than a chosen set is kept in (`Mouse1` before `LeftControl`), so those two would not be seen to clash.

## The display (`GraphicsSettingsGroup`, the display settings' class, `GameSettingsGroup`)

- **VSync** (`VSync`, off in the install): frames wait for the screen. (The game also holds the frame rate to a limit of its own, 120 in the install; not built.)
- **The field of view** (`FieldOfView`, on the game's Game tab): whole vertical degrees, 50 to 75, 50 unless set (74 in the install). With the sights up it is 15 less (`weapon-handling.md`).
- **How the window is shown** (`FullScreenMode`, kept as a figure): 0 the screen taken over, 1 a borderless window over all of it, 2 a window. The install has 1.
- **The resolution** (1920 by 1080 in the install).

- **The levels of detail's bias** (`LodBias`, the menu's `OBJECT LOD QUALITY`, `Settings/Graphics/ObjectLodQuality`): 2 unless set, clamped to 2..4, offered as 2, 2.5, 3, 3.5 and 4, written `0.#` (`GraphicsSettingsTab._lodBiasVariants`); the install has 2.0. Put on as Unity's `QualitySettings.lodBias` (third-person spec, "Levels of detail"). **In the app**: `OBJECT LOD QUALITY` on the graphics page's QUALITY section, a stepper of the game's five, the install's until chosen (`RESET TO DEFAULT` puts the install's back, as the old `AS THE INSTALL HAS THEM`), kept as `"lod_bias"` in `local\options.json` and read back clamped as the game's; taken at once; dim while both `distance_detail` and `world_distance_detail` are off for the run. **Since 0.244.0 it sets TRP's own rule's tolerance** (third-person spec, "TRP's own rule"; a departure on purpose): `2 / bias` pixels of a coarser level's deviation from the fullest allowed, one pixel at the game's own 2 (the install's and the default: a switch that cannot be seen) and half a pixel at 4, so every switch's distance scales with it as the game's bias scales the game's; the game's bias is no longer put on any view's pick. Its line says so ("2, until a coarser one is a pixel off; 4, half a pixel"). `--lod-pixels <n>` sets the pixels allowed for a run over it (a capture's aid).

The game puts a changed setting on when its screen is saved. The app puts each on as it is chosen. `PROVISIONAL`: a field of view changed in play eases to its new figure as a change of aim does; how the game's camera gets there was not read.

## The shadows (`QualityLevelPreset`, `GraphicsSettingsGroup.GetMaxShadowVisibility`, `EnvironmentManager.OutdoorShadowDistance`; read 2026-10-15)

The game's `ShadowsQuality` (0 low to 3 ultra, 0 unless set; 1 in the install read) picks one of four presets, which set Unity's shadows:

| Quality | Cascades | Where they end (share of the reach) | Reach | Shadow map |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0, low | 1 | 1 | 30 m | low |
| 1, medium | 2 | 0.33, 1 | 40 m | medium |
| 2, high | 4 | 0.05, 0.14, 0.33, 1 | 60 m | low |
| 3, ultra | 4 | 0.05, 0.14, 0.33, 1 | 80 m | medium |

The reach is the larger of the quality's and a map's own least (`ShadowMinDistance`); the range is no map of the game's. Unity's built-in renderer cuts the sun's shadow map into its cascades without blending one into the next.

**In the app** (`shadows`): the options' `SHADOW QUALITY` (LOW, MEDIUM, HIGH, ULTRA; restart required; HIGH until changed; `AS THE INSTALL HAS THEM` takes the install's), `--shadow-quality <0-3>` for a run. The sun's cascades end where the game's do, from the camera's near plane, without blending (Bevy's `overlap_proportion` 0). Before 0.15.0 the app had Bevy's own: four cascades to 150 m, the first to 10 m, each blended a fifth into the next. `PROVISIONAL`: the shadow map's size (Bevy's 2,048 texels a side for every cascade, where Unity's "low" and "medium" sizes go by the screen), and Unity's fit of a cascade to the view (`StableFit`) set beside Bevy's. Measured (2026-10-15, uncapped): at medium, against Bevy's own, 2.42 against 2.69 ms a frame walking at the range and 4.00 against 4.64 ms among 43 rifles; the view at the range within 0.88 of 765 on average. Compared with the game: nothing.

## The renderer (the app's own)

The game draws through Direct3D 11 (its `Player.log`); the app through Vulkan or DirectX 12, which draw the same picture (a still view at the range within 0.17 of 765 on average, the inventory's pictures within 0.9 of 1,020, measured 2026-10-15). The options' `RENDERER` (VULKAN, DIRECTX 12; restart required; Vulkan until changed) and `--backend` choose it; before the engine starts the app asks the graphics library whether the one chosen finds a graphics card that is not the processor drawing in its stead, and takes the other where it does not (`backend`; tried by hiding Vulkan's driver: the run drew through DirectX 12 and said why). **Why Vulkan by default**: Bevy's materials share one table of pictures there (bindless) and cannot on DirectX 12, so many weapons cost less: 2.10 ms a frame uncapped walking at the range against 2.69, 2.93 against 4.64 among 43 rifles; what is drawn is ready 0.5 s into a run against 0.7. With vsync the motion on screen is as even on either; Vulkan's own clock is a little less even (a frame now and then a millisecond late, the next as much early).

**`SSAO`**: the game's ambient occlusion's six choices in the game's own words, the install's own until changed (`Graphics.ini`'s `Ssao`), restart required; `docs/spec/ambient-occlusion.md`. **`CLOUDS QUALITY`**: the game's three choices in its own words (`Settings/Graphics/CloudsQuality`, `CloudsMode/Low` and the like: LOW, MEDIUM, HIGH; the game's default LOW, `GraphicsSettingsGroup`), the install's own until changed (`Graphics.ini`'s `CloudsQuality`), restart required (the game's takes it at once: its bakes are made again). Each its preset of the clouds' cookie and steps (`docs/spec/lighting.md`, "Customs' clouds"); with `CUSTOMS CLOUDS` off it does nothing. **`RENDER THREAD`** (ON, OFF; restart required): whether a frame is rendered on a thread of its own while the next is worked out (Bevy's pipelined rendering), as the game's engine renders with its graphics jobs (this install's `boot.config`: `gfx-enable-gfx-jobs=1`, which `AS THE INSTALL HAS THEM` takes). On for a run at the keys until changed; a capture run, which reads no options file, renders without it unless `--render-thread on` (so a capture's frame shows what it always has). With it the light run's frame costs 38% less, and each frame is drawn one update later; how evenly the updates come is the same either way on Bevy 0.18. Off until 0.80.0, by a measurement on Bevy 0.16 that no longer holds.

**The frame rate limit: `GAME FPS LIMIT` and `DISABLE GAME FPS LIMIT`**. The game's limit in a raid (`GameFramerate`, 30 to 144 by the game's own limits, `GraphicsSettingsController.MinFramerateLimit` and `MaxFramerateGameLimit`, which this install's server settings leave the same; 120 by default) and its switch (`DisableGameFramerateLimit`), in the game's words, the install's own until changed (120, on, as it comes), taken at once. As the game's `ChangeFramerate` in a raid: **with VSync off the frames are held to it** (the engine's `Application.targetFrameRate`); **with VSync on the screen alone paces them** (`targetFrameRate` -1). NVIDIA Reflex, which lifts it too in the game, is not built; the lobby's limit (`LobbyFramerate`, at most 60) has no place, the app having no lobby. The app's renderer waits for each frame's time just before it draws and presents it (`frame_limit`: a sleep to within a millisecond, then a spin, each frame's time from the last's), where the engine stamps its frame clock: measured with `--log-pacing`, 11.111 ms a frame at 90 (sd 0.17 ms without the render thread, 0.16 with it), 6.944 at 144, 16.667 at 60. A wait at the end of the main loop held the rate but, with the render thread, let frames come 2 and 20 ms apart now and then (sd 1.3 ms). A run nobody is at the keys of holds none unless told (`--fps-limit <30 to 144|off>`), so that the timing runs stay uncapped.

**`MSAA`**: OFF, 2X or 4X, the app's own multisampling of the scene (4X until changed, as the app has drawn since its start), taken at once (`--msaa off|2|4`). The game's deferred camera multisamples nothing (its anti-aliasing is FXAA or TAA, not built; the recorded game has `TAA_Low`): this is the app's choice, not the game's, and `AS THE INSTALL HAS THEM` leaves it. The ambient occlusion reads the normals' first sample either way.

## Features: what is switched

The game has no such switches: these are the app's own, kept in `local\options.json` beside the rest (`"switches"`, by their keys).

**Two of them on the HUD page since 0.191.1**: `TARKOV UI` (was under PRESENTATION) and `COMPATIBLE ITEMS HIGHLIGHT` (was under EXPERIMENTAL) are the first section of HUD, `SCREENS` (`HUD_SWITCHES`), drawn, thrown, kept and counted as the others, taken at the next start; the highlight still needs `INVENTORY` on FEATURES. **The highlight is taken at once** since 0.191.2: thrown by a click, by `INVENTORY` turned off or by a page's `RESET TO DEFAULT`, it is on the run that frame (where the run has the inventory), shows no `RESTART` and waits for no start; the only switch so. Each page's `RESET TO DEFAULT` puts back its own: FEATURES' leaves the two as they stand, HUD's puts them as they come (TARKOV UI on, the highlight off).

**The ambient occlusion has no switch**: its field is always on, and the GRAPHICS page's `SSAO` has its own `OFF`, as the game's setting has (`--feature ambient_occlusion=off` still turns it off for a run).

**The needs** (`Switch::requires`), by what the code goes by: the weight's slowing is the movement's; the condition and the stances act on the weapon the handling holds; the searches open containers through the world's interactions and show in the inventory, as the examining does; the raid limits, the clothing and the highlight are the inventory's; the dummies are made only with the bullets that hit them (`main.rs`: `with_dummy`), and the poppers fall to them; the benches are worked in the world and show the edit build screen, whose parts are the attachments'. **A switch whose need is off cannot be turned on** (its row dim, its box empty, `NEEDS ...` at its end); **one turned off takes off with it everything that needs it**, and turned on again brings none of them back (`Switches::set`). A file kept by hand with a need off is settled as it is read (`Switches::settle`); so is the command line's switch. **A switch that is not built** (`Switch::built`: `LEDGE CLIMBING`, whose move was taken out in 0.259.0 until it reads right) keeps its row and its key and sets no field: its row is dim with `NOT BUILT` at its end, it cannot be turned on from the page or the command line, and a file that has it on is settled to off. Every need is a switch with no needs of its own, so a chain is one link long.

**As it comes** (`Switch::recommended`, the agent's rule): what is Tarkov's own, on; what departs from how Tarkov plays or looks, off (`COMPATIBLE ITEMS HIGHLIGHT`, S.T.A.L.K.E.R.'s way; `GROUNDED FEET`, which draws the feet where Tarkov lets them float; the raid limits and Tarkov's lighting by an earlier choice); what the range adds without changing Tarkov (`BENCHES`, `CLOTHING`) and what is drawn between frames alone (`SMOOTH ANIMATIONS`, rule 8's one liberty), on. Against the lists before it, two came off: the highlight and the grounded feet.

**The command line**: `--feature <switch key>=on|off` throws a switch as the page does (refused, with a line saying what it needs, where a need is off), and then `--feature <field key>=on|off` sets one field alone for the run, by the keys of the lists before 0.190.0 (`vaulting_and_climbing`, `stamina_drain`, `recoil`, `movement_sounds`, `particles`, `customs_light`, `camera_sharpening`, `advanced_attachment_system`, `custom_first_person_hands`, `smooth_first_person_animations`, and the rest in the table below and in the paragraphs after it). A key that is both (`movement`, `stamina`, `inventory`, `tarkov_ui`, `sounds`, `visual_effects` and the like) is the switch's. `--custom-hands`, `--smooth-animations` and `--icons` set their fields as before.

**The options file**: `"switches"`, a key a switch. The lists' `"features"` of before 0.190.0 is not read: the switches begin as recommended.

**Before 0.190.0**: from 0.101.0 three lists, `SIMPLE` (fifteen, three of them groups), `EXPERIMENTAL` (seven, and the unbuilt `LITE INVENTORY`, which the page no longer shows: `backlog.md` keeps it) and `ADVANCED` (thirty-one, one a field), a panel at the right saying what the row under the mouse did; before that, from 0.31.0, two columns, `GAMEPLAY` and `VISUALS`. **Each switch is "restart required"**. Thrown away from what the run began with, a switch's row shows `RESTART`, and the footer counts what waits for a start (with the GRAPHICS page's settings taken only at a start) beside **`APPLY`, which starts TRP again** as this run was started (the same program, its words and its folder) and ends this one, so that they are taken now (dim where nothing waits, and in a run nobody is at the keys of, which keeps nothing).
