# The interface's sounds and the things' sounds

What the game plays while its screens are worked (opening the inventory, a tab under the mouse, a thing taken up and put down, a window opened and shut), where it keeps those sounds, and how loud it plays them. Read from the game's code (`local\decomp\src`) and from the install, and set beside a recording of both games, made out of a raid ("Compared with", at the end).

## Where the sounds are kept

**The interface's sounds** come from one object the game loads from its `Resources` (`audio/uisoundswrapper`, in `resources.assets`): its script (`UISoundsWrapper`) holds a list of pairs, a kind of sound (`EUISoundType`, a number) and the clip for it. After the script's own header and its name (a string, padded to four bytes) comes the count, then the pairs, sixteen bytes each: the kind as a 32-bit number, then a pointer to the clip (a file index and a 64-bit object number). `probe uisounds` prints them.

**The things' sounds** are a bundle of their own, `assets/content/audio/itemsounds/itemsounds.bundle` (`ItemSounds`). Each clip in it is named for a sound group and a way of handling a thing: `<group>_<way>`, for example `weap_rifle_drop`. A thing's group is its template's `ItemSound` property in the database (`templates/items.json`); the ways are the game's `EInventorySoundType`: `drop`, `pickup`, `use`, `open`, `other`, `offline_use`. A group without a clip for a way is silent for it (`GUISounds.GetClip` finds nothing and nothing plays). `probe sounds <folder> [group]` writes the clips out as WAVE files.

**A clip** (`AudioClip`, class 83) keeps its samples outside itself: its `m_Resource` names a file beside the build's (`.resource`, or the bundle's own stream), an offset and a size. That stretch is one FMOD sound bank (FSB5) holding one sample. The bank begins with `FSB5`, its version, the number of samples, then the sizes of the samples' headers, of the names and of the data, then the codec; the data starts after a fixed header (0x3C bytes from version 1 on, 0x40 for version 0), the samples' headers and the names. The clip's own `m_CompressionFormat` says how the data is kept:

- **0, PCM**: 16-bit samples as they are (FMOD's codec 2), channels interleaved.
- **2, ADPCM**: IMA ADPCM in FMOD's Xbox layout (codec 7). Each block is 36 bytes a channel: first each channel's four-byte header (the predictor as a 16-bit number, the step index as a byte, a byte unused), then the channels' four-bit codes in turns of four bytes a channel, the low four bits of each byte first. A block gives 64 samples a channel; the header's predictor is where the first code starts from, not a sample drawn. The last block is cut to the clip's own length.
- **1, Vorbis** (FMOD's codec 15; read 2026-10-16): the bank keeps only the audio's packets, each after its size as 16 bits, and in its sample's chunks (kind 11, `VORBISDATA`) the CRC32 of the setup header it was encoded with; the three headers a Vorbis stream begins with are left out. **FMOD keeps its setup headers in a table of its own, which in this build lies in the engine (`UnityPlayer.dll`, FMOD built into it)**: 33 of them, each beginning with a setup packet's mark (the byte 5, then `vorbis`). A clip's is the run of bytes from a mark whose CRC32 is the one its bank names (the thirteen clips name two: 3,908 and 3,771 bytes). The identification header is made again from the bank's channels and frequency with FMOD's block sizes, 256 and 2048 (`PROVISIONAL`: vgmstream's notes on the format; every clip made so decodes to exactly its own count of frames); the comment header is not needed. The packets are then decoded in order by `lewton` (a Vorbis decoder in Rust) and cut to the clip's frames. **Nothing of FMOD's is kept in the repository**: the headers are read from the install each time (`trp_data::sound::VorbisSetups`; `probe vorbis [folder]`). Thirteen of the things' clips are kept so: ten looting sounds (played while a container is searched item by item, `Player.InventoryControllerOnSearchEvent`: not built), and `med_medkit_use`, `med_bandage_use`, `food_snack_use` (a thing's `use`: played when it is put in a slot of the equipment, and as `offline_use` out of a raid).

The first reading of a stereo ADPCM clip took the channels' codes as alternating bytes and came out as noise; the layout above was found by comparing how rough each reading was against the clips kept as PCM (`probe sounds` prints that roughness).

**The things' own sounds** (a door's, a container's, a trunk's opening, squeak and shutting, a door's breach and hit: `interactions.md`, "A thing's sounds"; read 2026-10-23) are no bundle's. Each thing's prefab lies in a map's own scene, one of the build's `level<N>` files, and its script names its clips (`OpenSound`, `SqueakSound`, `ShutSound`, `BreachSound`, `HitClip`) in the `sharedassets<N>.assets` beside it, with their samples in that file's `.resource`. The build's files keep no type trees: the script is read by the `LootableContainer` tree of a bundle that has one with its clips (`assets/content/location_objects/lootable/prefab/rand_loot_container_twitch.bundle`), a door's own fields after the base's by their declared order, and a clip by the `AudioClip` tree of the same bundle. The app reads Customs' design scene (`level8`, `custom_DesignStuff`: its containers, corpses, safes and cases) and its dorms (`level10`, `custom_Obshezhitie`: its metal doors); `probe thingsounds` prints every prefab there with its sets.

## How loud

The game's sound settings (`Sound.ini` in SPT's `user\sptSettings`) keep each volume as a notch, 0 to 10, and the game sets the mixer by a table of decibels for the notches (`SoundSettingsGroup._realSoundValues`): −80, −40, −30, −25, −20, −17, −11, −8, −5, −2, 0. `OverallVolume` sets the master group (`MasterVolume`), `InterfaceVolume` the interface's (`UIVolume`, `SoundSettingsController.CreateBindings`). Both default to 10 where a file says nothing.

- **An interface sound** (`GUISounds.PlayUISound`) plays whole on the interface's group: both volumes apply to it.
- **A thing's sound** (`GUISounds.PlayItemSound`) plays on the inventory's group at 0.15 in a raid (`GetCommonSoundsVolume`); the overall volume applies. What the inventory's own group adds on the mixer is not read (`PROVISIONAL`: taken as nothing). **Out of a raid** (the main menu's CHARACTER screen) the same sound plays at 1 on the interface's group (`GetCommonSoundsMixerGroup`): the recording of 2026-10-16 was made there, so its things' sounds are 13.5 dB louder than the app's, which is a raid's.
- **A container's own opening** (its group's `open` clip, `GUISounds.PlaySound`) plays as an interface sound.
- **A mono clip plays at 1/√2 on each channel**, a stereo one as it is: measured in the recording (both volumes at their top, out of a raid), ten of the things' clips (all mono) came out 3.0 to 3.4 dB under the clip's own level and the interface's (stereo) at it, as a mono source in the middle at equal power does. The app plays them so (`ui_sound::frames_of`).

The recorded install has both volumes at notch 1, −40 dB each: an interface sound comes at −80 dB, which cannot be heard. The app takes the install's notches as they are, and the options' **SOUND** page changes them for the app alone (`settings.md`).

**Where a thing went decides its sound.** The game looks at the thing that now holds it, if it lies in a slot (or in a magazine's stack of rounds); in a grid's cell it holds nothing for this. A part (`Mod`) gone into a weapon's slot plays the interface's installing: a magazine `MenuInstallMag`; otherwise by its class (`ItemViewFactory.GetModClass`, in this order: functional, gear, vital, auxiliary, plate), a vital part `MenuInstallModVital`, a gear part `MenuInstallModGear`, any other `MenuInstallModFunc` (`GUISounds.PlayItemSound(EModClass)`). Anything else plays its group's `use` where the holder is the equipment itself or a magazine, and its `drop` anywhere else. A stack merged into another goes by where the other lies.

The kinds by number (`EUISoundType`): `BackpackClose` 0, `BackpackOpen` 1, `ButtonClick` 3, `ButtonOver` 4, `MenuEscape` 13, `MenuInspectorWindowClose` 14, `MenuInspectorWindowOpen` 15, `MenuInstallMag` 16, `MenuInstallModFunc` 17, `MenuInstallModGear` 18, `MenuInstallModVital` 19, `MenuOpenContainer` 23.

## In the app

`trp_data::sound` reads the clips (`ui_sounds`, `item_sounds`); `ui_sound` reads them on a thread of its own as the run begins and plays what the screen asks for at the volumes above. A sound asked for before its clip has come is not played. The inventory's systems ask for them where the table says (`inventory_view`: `screen_sounds`, `tab_sounds`, `click`, `drag`, `ask`, `windows`).

## Played

**One source plays them all**: a sound of the app's own for the whole run (`ui_sound::Mixed`), two channels at the clips' 44.1 kHz, into which each sound asked for is mixed from its next block of 64 frames (1.5 ms), at its gain.

**A sound whose source moves as it plays** is told its two channels' gains anew from the frame's side (`ui_sound::Gains`, `set_gains`; both sides in one number, so the mix never has one side of one frame with the other of the next; no lock is taken for it). The mix does not jump to them: each block it goes an eighth of what is left (`GLIDE`), by a straight line from frame to frame within the block, so three quarters of a change is made in a sixtieth of a second and no frame's gain steps. The eighth is the app's own, an `ESTIMATE`: how the engine glides a moving source's volume is not read. A dummy's speech is the one sound told so (the health spec, "Any character's voice"); the app's other placed sounds keep the gains they began with (`PROVISIONAL`: short ones, a step, a hit, a case). The equal-power pan is `ui_sound::pan`.

Until 0.35.0 each sound was a sound of its own (Bevy's `AudioPlayer`, a sink of rodio 0.20.1's for each), and **the first 6 to 12 ms of every sound came out wrong**. The recording of both games showed it (2026-10-16). From 6 ms on the app's hover sound was the game's sample for sample (correlation 0.94 to 1.00, the same level), but its start was a third quieter, its two channels one; a mono sound's first 12 ms ran fast (its best shift from the clip sliding from 36 samples to none), then was right. Made again away from the app, through rodio's own sink and mixer, a sound appended to a sink just made came out the same way. The cause is rodio's queue: an empty sink's queue stands as one channel at 48 kHz, its mixer reads a source's format again only at a stretch's end (up to 512 samples), and a sound appended in the middle of a stretch is read in the stretch's format until it ends. The one source never changes its format and never ends, so the queue reads it right from the first; a test plays a sound into it through rodio's sink and mixer and finds it whole from its first frame, each channel its own (`ui_sound::tests`).

## A weapon landing (`LootItem`, `ItemDropSounds`; read and built 2026-10-23)

**What sounds.** Every thing the asset pool makes (a weapon, a magazine, a round, an item, a part) is given the loot part `ObservedLootItem` (`AssetsPoolStaticConfig.ComponentsToAdd`, offline too), whose collision handler plays a landing for a thing whose template has a landing sound: `DropSoundType` in the database is `Pistol` (33 templates), `SubMachineGun` (22) or `Rifle` (103), and `None` for everything else. So a weapon thrown from the inventory sounds (`ThrowItem`), and so does the weapon in hand a death lets go (`DropItemDead` hands its body to the same part, `SetItemAndRigidbody`); nothing else does.

**When.** As the body begins to touch another collider (`OnCollisionEnter`). A surface is looked at no more than once each half second (`CheckSurface`, `delayToNextCheck` 0.5): a touch within half a second of the last look is not heard, and the look counts even when what was met has no surface (no `BaseBallistic`), which is then silent. The surface is the one the collider says at the first point of contact (`GetSurfaceSound`); a body's hit colliders say theirs (`BodyPartCollider.SurfaceSound`: concrete on all 27 of the player prefab's ragdoll).

**How loud.** The energy: the body's mass times gravity times the height between where the thing set off (or last sounded) and where it is now, plus the mass times the square of its speed after the touch (`CalculateEnergy`, as written: not half of it). A thrown thing sets off where it is thrown (`CacheParameters`); a weapon a death lets go keeps no place, so it counts from the world's nought. A thrown thing's mass is its whole weight; the weapon a death lets go weighs 1 kg (2 for a pistol, a throwable or a knife). The volume is the install's curve of that energy (`EnergyToVolumeCurve`: 0 at 0 J, 0.49 at 20.7 J, 0.73 at 101 J, 1 at 250 J), rounded to two places.

**Which bank, and where.** `ItemDropSounds` (`assets/content/audio/prefabs/items/itemdropsounds.bundle`, `probe dropsounds`): for each of 17 surfaces a pistol's, a submachine gun's and a rifle's bank (thin and thick wood share wood's: 45 banks), five clips each, base volumes 0.5 to 0.9 less up to 0.1, rolloff 30 m; a surface without a set takes the default surface's (concrete). Played at the thing's place as any bank at a point (`BetterAudio.PlayAtPoint`: nothing past the bank's rolloff, clips by the distance), from the banks' source kind, `Collisions` (`Main/Environment/CommonSounds`, 0 dB), falling by that group's rolloff curve (below, "The groups' presets").

**In the app** (`trp_sim::landing`, `landing_sound`; `WORLD SOUNDS`): PhysX tells a body that asks of each pair of shapes it begins to touch (`World::report_touches`, the pair's `eNOTIFY_TOUCH_FOUND`); a thing thrown from the inventory and the weapon a death lets go (the player's and a dummy's) ask. A still thing's surface is the range's where the touch is. A thing the range sets down at the start is not this work's fall to hear (the game's loot lies where the map puts it). **Run**: a thrown AKM 67.9 J, 0.65; a death's AKM first on its own body at once (15.8 J, 0.43), then on the ground. Not compared with a recording.

## A body's fall (`Corpse.PlayCorpseDropSound`, `LootItem.CheckSurfaceRayCast`, `BetterAudio.PlayDropItem`; read and built 2026-11-09)

**When.** At any character's death, the player's and a bot's alike, once, on the frame its corpse is made, just after the corpse's ragdoll: `Player.OnDead` makes the corpse (`CreateCorpse`), whose set-up (`Corpse.Init`) makes the ragdoll (`TryToCreateRagdoll`) and then plays the fall. No player class makes its corpse otherwise (`LocalPlayer.OnDead` only writes a bot's dog tag first). A corpse loaded still with a level (`CreateStillCorpse`) has no ragdoll and no fall.

**On what.** The corpse is a part added to the character's own object, so its place is the character's root: the feet as it died. From there a line goes straight down 10 m on the steps' layers (`CheckSurfaceImmediately`, `AudioControllerStepLayerMask`): nothing met, no sound; a thing with a surface (`BaseBallistic.GetSurfaceSound` at the point met), that surface; a thing without one, concrete. The 0.5 its caller hands it is not a reach but the least time before the next look (`delayToNextCheck`), which a corpse made anew never waits on.

**Which bank.** The sounds of moving (`assets/content/audio/prefabs/movement/sounds.bundle`, `probe stepsounds`): the surface's set's prone drop (`ProneDropSoundBank`, the bank of going prone from a run), or concrete's set's where the surface has no set; a set without that bank plays nothing. All twenty sets have one: one to six clips, base volume 0.8 less up to 0.1 (metal's less up to 0.05; grass's and gravel's 1.0, soil's and snow's 0.6, snow's with no draw), pitch 1 within 0.15 either way, rolloff 66.5 m, from a source of the `Character` kind (3). The clips are not all named alike: concrete's `bodydrop_concrete1` and `2`, soil's `soil_bodydrop_01`, metal's `body_drop_metal_01` to `05`, thin wood's `body_drop_wood_thin_01` to `04` (its bank `FallWoodThin`).

**How loud, and where heard.** As a thing let fall with a bank of its own (`PlayDropItem(bank, place, 50)`): the 50 is an energy, read on the drop sounds' curve (`ItemDropSounds.EnergyToVolumeCurve`, as a weapon landing's, above) and rounded to hundredths: 0.59. Then as any bank at a point (`PlayAtPoint`): nothing past the bank's rolloff from the camera; the clip chosen by the distance; the volume handed to the source 0.59 times the bank's draw (its base less up to its delta), the pitch drawn; the source's farthest the bank's rolloff; in its source kind's group of the mixer (`Character`'s, `CommonSounds`), with the one-shot occlusion test (`OneShotPropagation`).

**In the app** (`trp_sim::landing`, "A corpse's fall"; `step_sound::play_falls`). The player's corpse (`death::die`) and a dummy's (`dummy::fall_dummies`) each tell, on the frame they are made, where the root is (the character's feet) and what the range's line down from there meets (the ground or a box: every thing of the range has a surface); the surface's bank plays through the app's bank at a point (`impact_sound::play_at_point`: the `Character` preset's curve over 66.5 m, panned at equal power as the app's other placed sounds, `PROVISIONAL`), at the overall volume, in `CommonSounds`. ## A thing taken up (`LootItem.RemoveLootItem`; read and built 2026-10-23)

When a thing on the ground leaves it whole for the inventory, the game plays its group's `pickup` clip (the inventory's clip for its kind) at the thing's place, from a source of the `Character` group with its spatialising off, so plain stereo (`forceStereo`), at a volume of 1, if the listener is within 10 m (`TryPlayAtPoint`'s rolloff); then occlusion is worked for it (not built). A stack only topped up from it stays on the ground and is silent. In the app: on the take of `F` (`inventory_view::toggle`, `WORLD SOUNDS`), in the world's group at the overall volume.

## The groups' presets (`AudioGroupPreset`; read 2026-10-23)

Each of the engine's source groups is made from a preset in the build's Resources (`Audio/AudioGroupPresets/<name>`, 17 of them, in `resources.assets`, read by `trp_data::audio_preset` to their last byte): its overall volume, spatial blend, binaural limits, occlusion and reverb settings, and its rolloff curve (`SoundRolloff`), which every source of the group is set to (custom rolloff). The curve's time is a share of the source's farthest distance (each curve ends near 1 at 0): a bank at a point sets the farthest to the bank's rolloff; a character's steps to their movement's rolloff (`footsteps.md`). `Character`: 1 at 0, 0.205 at 0.151, 0 at 1.001; `Collisions` (and `InteractiveObjects`): 1 at 0, 0.540 at 0.053, 0 at 1. The backend's `settings.json` lays its overall volumes and binaural limits over them (equal to the presets' here). **Used** by the weapons' landings and a dummy's steps since 0.106.0; the other sounds of the world (the hits, the things, the spent cases, the player's own steps) still fall as one over the distance (`PROVISIONAL`), to be moved onto their groups' curves with a listen.

## The hands' sounds (`BaseSoundPlayer`; read and built 2026-10-16)

What a weapon, an item used or the empty hands sound as their animation goes.

- **Every rig the hands hold has a sound player of its own**, on the prefab's own object (beside its `WeaponPrefab` or `UsableHandsPrefab`, above the animated rig): `BaseSoundPlayer`, or for a weapon and the empty hands `WeaponSoundPlayer`, which adds the shots and the aiming. It has two lists of elements, `MainSounds` and `AdditionalSounds`: each an event's name, a rolloff (metres), a volume and its clips.
- **A `Sound` mark of the rig's animator** (one of its states' marks, with a string: `IEventsConsumer.OnSound`, `SoundEventHandler`) plays the element of `AdditionalSounds` named by the string, or by the string with `Snd` before it: one of its clips at random (`Random.Range`), at its volume (`PlayRandomClip`). `SoundAtPoint` the same. `MainSounds` (ShellEject, MagOut and the rest) are played only by `EventReceiver`, which nothing in the build calls; `OnUseProp` plays the player's own `PropIn` and `PropOut` (not built).
- **How it plays** (`PlayClip`): for the character's own eyes in stereo, whole (`forceStereo`), whatever the distance; seen from outside, from the weapon's place, and not at all past the element's rolloff; on the source kind `Weaponry`, which goes to the mixer's `Guns/Instrumental` group (`BetterAudio.method_5`: `GunsInstrumentalMixer`; read 2026-10-17, night).
- **The AI-2's** (`item_medkit_container`): Draw, Open, Take (the syringe), CapRemove, Inject, Discard (the syringe thrown away), Holster, each one clip, and the backpack's drop; its animator's marks name the first seven in that order. **The AKM's**: 35, among them Draw, Holster, MagOut, MagIn, MagPullOut (seven clips of a pouch), BoltOut, BoltIn, Selector, HandOn, HandOff, ChamberCheck_in and _out, DryFire, and a malfunction's. **The empty hands'**: 21.

**Built** (`trp_data::sound::hands_sounds`, read with each rig; `hands_sound`): the marks the firearm, the empty hands and an item's hands pass are played so, through the app's one source; for the character's own eyes whole, a mono clip at equal power in the middle (as the things' sounds); from outside from the character's place 1.4 m up, falling as one over the distance past 1 m and panned as the steps (`PROVISIONAL`: the weapon's own place, the source's curve and the pan law are not read). ## The shots (`WeaponSoundPlayer`; read and built 2026-10-16)

The plan's next step after the hands' sounds. What a weapon's shots sound like, from the banks of its own sound player.

- **The banks.** A weapon's sound player (`WeaponSoundPlayer`) has six banks (`SoundBank`): `Body`, `Tail`, `Doublet`, and the three silenced. A bank has a volume, a rolloff, a length (`ClipLength`: its `CustomLength` if not 0, else the length kept with it), blend distances (`DistanceBlendOptions`) and, for each environment (outdoor, indoor), three lists of clips by distance (close, distant, far). `Non_auto` says whether each shot plays whole.
- **The clip by distance** (`PickClipsByDistance`): the distance from the camera to the weapon is counted against the blend values: under the first, one clip of the close list; between the first and the second, one of the close and one of the distant, the close's share falling from 1 to 0 across the span; between the second and the third, a distant one; between the third and the fourth, a distant and a far blended; past the fourth, a far one. A clip of a list is dealt from the bank's deck (`GetRandomClipIndex`): the deck holds the list's places in a shuffled order, dealt from its end, and is shuffled afresh when it is out. The shuffle swaps each place with one drawn by an integer `Random.Range(0, n - 1)`, which never draws the last, so a list of two always deals them in turn.
- **Which bank** (`FireBullet`): `Body`, or `Doublet` when two chambers fire together (the game calls the sound once for each chamber, `multiShot` set when more than one fires). With a silencer on (`IsSilenced`: any part of the build with a `SilencerComponent`, a `Silencer` or a barrel whose template `IsSilencer`), the silenced banks; a missing silenced body or tail falls back to the plain one, a missing silenced doublet does not, and a missing bank is silent.
- **The queue.** The shots play through a queue of two sources (`SuperBetterAudioQueue`), each sample scheduled on the audio clock (`dspTime`). A sample begins at its start, on the other source from the one before, and that one is given its end at the new sample's start (`SetScheduledEndTime`): **each sample ends the one before it**. A sample with no length loops until the next begins.
- **A weapon that is not `Non_auto`** (an automatic weapon, the AKM): the first shot starts the body's clip as a loop, after the sound's travel (the distance over 340.29 m/s). The loop is the weapon's fire recorded at its rate, sixteen beats long (the AKM's 1.6 s: sixteen shots at 600 a minute). Further shots while it plays only nudge its pitch. The loop ends when the controller lets go of the trigger (`IsTriggerPressed` set false, which raises `BreakLoop`): the tail is queued on the **next beat** after that moment, a beat being a sixteenth of the body's length, counted from the first shot (`StopFiringLoop`: the beats passed, rounded down, plus one), and the loop ends where the tail begins. A shot made with the trigger already let go (a single shot's operation lets go at its shot) has its tail queued one beat on at once. So an AKM's single shot is one beat of the loop and then the tail; a pull of three rounds is three beats and the tail; the run that empties the magazine (thirty and the chambered one) ends on the 31st beat, when the cycle's end lets go of the trigger. A new pull before the last tail has begun ends that tail at its own start.
- **A `Non_auto` weapon** (the SV-98, the M3, the Five-seveN, the Rhino, the SKS, the MP-43): each shot's body clip plays whole, ended by the next shot.
- **The pitch** (`CalculateBaseAimingVolume`): each shot asks for 1 ± 0.03 at random; a weapon in automatic fire whose overheating has slowed its rate asks the ratio of the rates instead (the overheating is not built: its multiplier stays 0). The first shot of a loop takes its pitch whole; each further shot sets the queue's pitch, held within 0.965 to 1.045, which the loop and the tail queued follow. Between letting go and the tail's start, each frame draws the pitch toward 1 by the share of the time passed since the first shot over the time left to the tail (`SmoothResetPitch`).
- **Heard** (`EnableStereo`): for the character's own eyes in stereo, whole, whatever the distance; seen from outside, from the weapon's place. Through the mixer's `Gunshots` group (`BetterAudio.GunshotMixerGroup`).

**Built** (`trp_data::sound::hands_sounds` reads the banks with the rig; `shot_sound` is the player and its queue; the app's mixer schedules a sound on its own clock, loops it and ends it on a frame set later, `ui_sound::Voice::at_frame`). The simulation tells the host the moment of each shot's sound and whether the trigger is still held then (`Event::FireSound`, one per chamber, with `doublet`), and every time the controller lets go of the trigger (`Event::TriggerReleased`): the sound plays on the shot's own frame, which for a single shot is the frame of its bullet, one before the recoil's kick (`firing.md`). Only the outdoor clips are read: the range has no indoors. The volume is the bank's, at the overall volume and the `Gunshots` group's path, 0 dB as read (below). From outside, from the character's place 1.4 m up, falling as one over the distance past 1 m and panned as the hands' sounds, its share of two clips fixed when it is queued (`PROVISIONAL`, as theirs: the source's curve, its moving and the pan law are not read). The options' **`SHOT SOUNDS`** switch (restart required): off, the banks are not read and the shots are silent.

**Heard in runs** (`--log-sounds`, `--sound-out`; `local\shots_runs_s86.ps1`), each as above: the AKM held on automatic (the loop from the first shot, the tail on the 31st beat, the hammer's click after), five single shots (one beat and the tail each, each ended by the next), pulls of two, three and one rounds (tails after 2, 3 and 1 beats), the SV-98's shot whole, the MP-43's barrels one at a time, the AKM from outside at 2.9 m (8.5 ms later, placed). Tested: the deck of two dealing in turn, the clip and share by distance, a loop cut on the frame its tail begins, the banks of the AKM, the SV-98 and the MP-43 read. **Compared with the game** (`courses8`'s check, 2026-11-01, 0.181.2: the AKM's three single shots at rest, every sound the game started with the time it was scheduled for): each loop starts on its shot's frame; each tail is queued for one beat after its loop's start over that shot's pitch (0.0993, 0.1009 and 0.1009 s: a beat of 0.1 s over 1.0070, 0.9915 and 0.9916, inside the 1 ± 0.03 drawn), as built. The game queues a single shot's tail a frame or two after the shot rather than on it, for the same time, so nothing heard differs (the pitches themselves are random and not recorded). Not compared: automatic fire, the levels, the clip dealt, the indoor clips the game played there. **Not built**: the crack of a supersonic bullet heard from outside (`FireSonicSound`), occlusion, the indoor clips, the burst of two's own rules (`PlayFireBulletSound`, `IsBirstOf2Start`: its first shot's tail two beats on, its second shot's sound skipped while a round is chambered), a malfunction's break of the loop (no malfunction is built), the mounted weapon's and the aiming's sounds, the `Guns` group's three sends to the mixer's returns (its reverb and delay).

## The aiming's sounds (read and built 2026-10-16)

What the sights sound like brought up and down (`FirearmController.SetAim`), the call the aim key makes (and an obstacle's push, a long fall, a vault: whatever the controller asks of `SetAim`). When the call changes whether the sights are up:

- **The kit rattles**, up or down: the player's `PlayGearSound(0.2)`, not fast, as the steps' kit sounds (`footsteps.md`, "The kit": times the movement's multiplier and the equipment's noise; not while dead).
- **Coming up, the weapon's aiming sound** (`PlayAimingSound`): a clip of the bank for the weapon's kind of sound, played as the hands' marks are (`PlayClip`: for the character's own eyes in stereo, whole; from outside, not past the bank's rolloff). The banks are in the weapons' sounds (`WeaponSounds.AimingSounds`, a `WeaponAimingSoundsSO` keeping kinds and banks as two lists): rifles, shotguns and grenade launchers share one (18 clips, 0.4, 40 m), pistols and every other weapon another (8, 0.5, 30 m), machine guns (12, 0.45, 45 m) and SMGs (11, 0.3, 35 m) their own. **The weapon's kind** (`AudioWeaponUtils.GetWeaponType`) by its template's classes: a grenade launcher `Launcher`; an assault rifle or carbine, a marksman or a sniper rifle `Riffle`; `Shotgun`; `MachineGun`; a pistol or revolver `Pistol` (the M32A1, a revolver by its classes, among them); `Smg`; anything else `SpecialWeapon` (the RShG-2). **The clip** (`GetRandomClipWithExclusion`): one of those not played since all last were, drawn by an integer `Random.Range(0, n - 1)` among them, so the last of them is never drawn while another is left. **The volume** (`CalculateAimingSoundVolume`): the square of how far the weapon's ergonomics fall short of 100, as a share, held within 0.1 and 0.2 (0.2 for nearly every rifle: below 55; 0.1 for most pistols: over 68), times one less the draw skill's buff, the equipment's noise (1 and the overweight, less the Covert Movement buff) and one less the bots' sound coefficient; times the bank's volume.
- **Other ways the sights go down are silent**: a sprint, a reload, the body's state, a bolt action's shot set the aiming without `SetAim`.

**Built**: the simulation says when the aim's call moves the sights (`Event::Aimed`); the host asks the steps' rules for the kit (`StepSounds::kit`) and plays the aiming's clip (`hands_sound`; the banks read away from the frame at the start, `trp_data::sound::aiming_sounds`). The weapon's ergonomics are its parts' sum as the aiming's speed takes them (the game's `TotalErgonomics` adds what buffs give, none here); the skills' buffs untrained (0). Under the options' `HANDS SOUNDS` (the clip, as the marks' sounds come from the same source) and `MOVEMENT SOUNDS` (the kit). **Heard in runs** (`--tap 300:rmb:2` and twice more): the AKM, the kit at each change and a rifle clip each time up, at 0.08; the Five-seveN, a pistol clip at 0.05 (its ergonomics at the floor). Tested: the volume by the ergonomics, a clip not again until all have played, the event only when the call moves the sights. **Not compared with the game.** **Not built**: the mount's sound (`PlayMountSound`, a mounted weapon not built).

## The game's mixer (read 2026-10-16)

The game's audio mixer is a bundle of its own (`assets/content/audio/mastermixer.bundle`), kept as Unity builds a mixer: one `AudioMixerController` whose `m_MixerConstant` holds 78 groups (each with its parent, and the place of its volume and pitch among a snapshot's values), 156 effects (each with its kind, its group and the places of its parameters) and three snapshots (Indoor, Outdoor, Bunker: each a list of 456 values), beside one object a group naming it by its GUID. The mixer starts on its **Outdoor** snapshot (`m_StartSnapshot`); a script may set the values it exposes (104 of them). Read so (`trp_data::sound::master_mixer`, `probe mixer`):

- **The volumes on the way to the master, Outdoor**: `Gunshots` under `Guns`, `World`, `InGame` and `Master`, all 0 dB; the player's own movement, `ClientPlayerMovement`, -3.5 dB under groups at 0 dB (with two equalisers); `Inventory` 0 dB. The app plays the shots and the steps at those (the steps since 0.40.0; before, the movement's group added nothing).
- **The master's compressor.** The master group has the group's attenuation and an effect of kind 16, with four parameters: -1, 10, 20 and 0. By my reading the kinds are FMOD's numbers for its effects (16 its compressor, 17 its reverb on the reverb returns, 3 and 5 its low and high pass, 8 its distortion and 10 its equaliser where those would be): a compressor's threshold (dB), attack and release (ms) and make-up gain (dB), the attack and release at the least FMOD allows. **It takes nothing off a loud mix** (`courses2`'s `7-sound-shots`, 0.207.2: a shot heard at one gain through mixes of +1.0 to −4.6 dBFS, the mix running to +1.5 dBFS), whatever the effect is; the app's mix applied it as a limiter at −1 dB from 0.40.0 to 0.207.1 and no longer does (the figures are still read and logged).
- **The spent cases' sounds** (`firing.md`, "Spent cases"): their banks' source kind is `Collisions`, which BetterAudio sends to `Main/Environment/CommonSounds`, 0 dB with every group above it: nothing added.
- **Its exposed parameters** (`Mixer::exposed`, `probe mixer`): 104, each kept by the CRC32 of the name scripts set it by beside the value it moves, a group's volume, **a send's level** or another effect's parameter (`GunsVolume`, `MainVolume`, `WorldVolume`, `WorldLowpassFreq` and the rest are found; `ReverbVolume` is not among them). **A send keeps its level apart from its parameters** (it has none: its `wetMixLevelIndex`), which the reader took only since 0.228.0; before, every send's name was "not found", and this spec and `tinnitus` said `Tinnitus1` and `Tinnitus2` named nothing: they are the `Guns → SimpleOccluded` and `Main → SimpleOccluded` sends (at −80 dB in every snapshot), which a contusion's tinnitus moves by `TinnitusLowpas` (not built). The app moves its groups' levels as a helmet's cut and a contusion's tinnitus do ("Hearing under headgear", below; health spec, "A contusion's tinnitus").
- **The `Guns` group's sends** (by their targets): to `SimpleOccluded` (−80 dB, `Tinnitus1`), to `Reverb` (0 dB indoors and in the bunker, −80 outdoors) and to `GunCompressor` (−15 dB, the headphones' bus: `GunsCompressorSendLevel`). The −3.1 and −30 dB said here until then were the `TarkovReverb` and `TarkovDelay` groups' own volumes outdoors, which they are sent to from `AmbientEffects`, `TarkovDelay` and `RadioOut`, not from `Guns`.
- **Not built**: the snapshots but the start's, the values scripts set (but the tinnitus' dip and a headphones template's levels), every other effect (the equalisers, the `InGame` group's plug-in, the reverb and delay returns, the occlusion's, the headphones' bus's filters and compressors).
- **The clips are loud**: the AKM's loop is mastered with a tenth of its samples at full scale and its level 7 dB under it; played at 0 dB its transients take the mix over full scale, as the game's.

**Not built:** the sounds of searching (Vorbis, read since 0.39.0; the searching is not built), of the context menu and its buttons, of the options' own buttons, of a thing thrown, of the quick-use keys, of the death screen; the sounds of the world but the player's own moving (`footsteps.md`, since 0.36.0, played into the same one source, whose sounds are since then kept as read, 16 bits, and read at their own rate and pitch) and shooting (above), and the spent cases' bounces (`firing.md`), and a grenade's blast (`grenades-and-knife.md`, "The blast's sound").

## Hearing under headgear (`Player.UpdatePhonesReally`, `BetterAudio.ApplyHeadphonesTemplate`, `ActiveHeadphonesController`, `ActiveHeadphonesDefaultConfigurator`, `AudioMixerFader`; read and built 2026-11-09)

**Two muffles that do not touch.** The voice's (`UpdateMuffledState`, health spec, "The voice": a face shield down over armour on the jaw sends the player's own speech to `ClientPlayerSelfSpeechReverb` in place of `ClientPlayerSpeech`) is the speech alone. What headgear does to everything heard goes another way: for the player's own character (`IsYourPlayer`), `UpdatePhones` → `UpdatePhonesReally` chooses a headphones template and has BetterAudio apply it. The two stack: a muffled voice under a High helmet goes through its reverb group and then the helmet's cut of the `ClientPlayer` group.

**The choice** (in this order; the first that is not the default template wins). (1) A headset: the thing in the earpiece slot if it is `Headphones`, else any `Headphones` among the headwear's things (the FAST's RAC): its own template, over any cut. (2) Headwear that is armoured equipment: the strongest of its own `DeafStrength` and the cut of every armour component in it and its parts (`GetItemComponentsInChildren<CompositeArmorComponent>`). (3) The face cover: the strongest cut among its own and its parts' armour components. (4) Else the default. A thing has an armour component only where its template's greatest durability and armour class are both above nought (`ArmoredEquipment`'s making), so a part's or a face cover's cut counts only where it is armour, while the headwear's own always counts (in this install no helmet is armour itself: its armour is in its inserts). Body armour's cut is never read, nor any switch's state: a face shield up or down changes nothing (as recorded). `Low` gives the template `LowMute`, `High` `StrongMute`.
