# The particle system

Unity's particle system (its "Shuriken"), as the game's effects use it and as this work runs it. Written 2026-10-18 for the muzzle's fumes (`firing.md`, "The muzzle's fumes"), the first of the game's effects to need it; the heat haze is on it too, drawn into the camera's distortion (`firing.md`, "The muzzle's heat haze"); a grenade's explosion is on it (`grenades-and-knife.md`, "The blast's effect"); the muzzle's particle prefabs are still to come (below, "Not built"). (The muzzles' sparks are no Unity particles but the game's own renderer: `firing.md`, "The muzzle's sparks".)

**Where the knowledge comes from.** The stored form is read from the install (every module's figures are in the bundles with their type trees: `probe dump <bundle> <its id>`). What each module does is **Unity's documentation** (the manual's pages for the particle system's modules, and the scripting reference for `ParticleSystem.Emit`, `EmitParams`, `MinMaxCurve`, `MinMaxGradient` and `Gradient`): the engine's own code is not published, so where the documentation leaves the rule open it is said here and marked `PROVISIONAL`. The game's own code is read where it drives a system (`MuzzleManager`, `MuzzleFume`).

## What a system is made of

A `ParticleSystem` object, beside a `ParticleSystemRenderer` on the same game object, the renderer naming a material (and a second for trails).

- **The system's own figures**: its duration (`lengthInSec`), whether it loops, its simulation space (`moveWithTransform`: 0 local, 1 world, 2 a custom transform's), its scaling mode, and its modules, each with an `enabled` flag.
- **The main module** (`InitialModule`, always on): the start values a particle is born with (lifetime, speed, colour, size, rotation, each a curve or a range: below), the gravity modifier and the most particles alive (`maxNumParticles`).
- **The modules over a particle's life**, each read at the particle's age as a share of its lifetime (0 at birth, 1 at its end): size (`SizeModule`), rotation (`RotationModule`, an angular speed), colour (`ColorModule`), the speed's limit (`ClampVelocityModule`), a force (`ForceModule`, in the world's or the system's axes), the turn by speed (`RotationBySpeedModule`: an angular speed by where the particle's speed is in its range), the picture's sheet (`UVModule`), and the rest (below, "Not built").
- **Its own emission** (`EmissionModule`): a rate a second and bursts, each at a time of the system's cycle with a count, a number of cycles (0 for ever), an interval between them and a chance; **its shape** (`ShapeModule`): where on it a particle is born and which way it goes (a cone's base, a sphere, half of one), with the shape's own place, turn and size, a share of the way at random (`randomDirectionAmount`), a share out from the middle (`sphericalDirectionAmount`) and a place scattered (`randomPositionAmount`).
- **The renderer**: how a particle is drawn (`m_RenderMode`: 0 a billboard facing the camera, others stretched, flat or a mesh), the billboard's alignment (`m_RenderAlignment`: 0 to the view), the order particles are drawn in (`m_SortMode`: 0 none), the least and most size on the screen (`m_MinParticleSize`, `m_MaxParticleSize`, as shares of the view's width where the particle is: "Drawn"), and its material.

**A value that can vary** (`MinMaxCurve`): by its mode (`minMaxState`) a constant (0, `scalar`), a curve over the share of life times `scalar` (1), between two curves (2), or between two constants (3, `minScalar` and `scalar`); where it is between two, each particle has its own draw for where between. Rotations are kept in radians (5 degrees a second is stored as 0.0873).

**A colour that can vary** (`MinMaxGradient`): a colour (0), a gradient (1), between two colours (2), between two gradients (3), or a random colour of a gradient (4). **A gradient** has up to eight colour keys and eight alpha keys apart (`key0` to `key7`, their colours and their alphas; `ctime`, `atime` their times in 65535ths; `m_NumColorKeys`, `m_NumAlphaKeys` how many are used), blended (`m_Mode` 0) or stepped (1); before the first key the first's value, after the last the last's.

## What happens to a particle

- **Born** (`ParticleSystem.Emit(EmitParams, 1)`, as the game's code emits): each value the call gives (place, velocity, lifetime, colour, size, rotation, its own random seed) is the particle's; any other is the main module's start value. A world-space system takes the place as it is.
- **A system played** (`Play`): after its start delay (drawn), each burst at its time of the cycle (again each interval, so many cycles, each by its chance) and the rate's share over each step (its fractions carried) give so many particles, each born on the shape with the main module's start values at the cycle's share (its speed along the shape's way); the duration ends the cycle, which starts again for a looping system; a system that loops not is done emitting. A world-space system's particles are born where its object puts them and stay there; a local one's are kept in its own axes and go where it goes. `PROVISIONAL`: the object's size moves where on the shape they are born and not their speed, as the scaling mode `Shape` does (`scalingMode` is not read); a cone's way leans out from its axis by its angle in proportion to how far out on the base the particle is born; a sphere's places are even over its volume, a cone's over its base's area, within the shell `radiusThickness` leaves.
- **Born within a step, lived through the rest of it** (the engine's code, decompiled 2026-11-03: `ParticleSystem::Update1Incremental`, `StartParticles`, `StartModules`, `EmissionModule::EmitCommon`, `AccumulateBursts`; and every recorded explosion of `courses3`): a step first updates the particles already alive, then emits, and each particle emitted lives at once through the time from its birth to the step's end: it ages by it, its velocity modules act over it, it moves by it, and it meets the world over it. The rate's particles come first, the `i`th of a step's through `(i + carried) / amount` of the emitting time (`carried` the share of a particle left over after this step's, `amount` the particles the rate gives this step); then the bursts', every one of them through the share of the emitting time after the last burst that fired this step (by its first cycle's time: the engine keeps one share for all the step's bursts). Each share is held between a millionth and the whole. The emitting time is from the system's time before the step (or the end of its delay) to its time after, held at the cycle's end for a system that does not loop. A burst fires while its time is before that end. A particle set off by another's meeting with the world (a sub-emitter) is born at the step's end, aged nought (recorded: `drop_dust` first seen aged nought).
- **A burst's count** (`EmissionModule::AccumulateBurst`, `RangedRandom`, decompiled): a burst whose chance is below one fires where a draw falls below the chance, one of nought never; between two constants its count is a whole number drawn evenly from the lesser to the greater, both ends counted; a constant or a curve (read at the cycle's share after the step) is cut to its whole part, not rounded.
- **A system paused while unseen** (`cullingMode`: 0 left to the engine, a looping system paused and another simulated; 1 paused and caught up when seen again; 2 paused; 3 always simulated): played with nothing to draw, it takes no step on the frame it is played, and that frame's time is not caught up (recorded: the grenade's `explosion_center` and `HeatDistortion`, mode 1, a frame behind the effect's other systems from their first frame in four of the five F-1 blasts on concrete). The engine pauses such a system on any frame its renderer was not seen (`1-f1-ground`'s four blasts: both systems never ran, the camera away). `PROVISIONAL`: modes 2 and 0 taken as 1; every system taken as seen on every frame after its first (this work has no test of what the camera saw); the one blast whose two such systems stepped on their first frame not explained.
- **An effect's object switched off** (`m_IsActive`), with all under it, is not played with the effect (Unity's documentation: an inactive object's components do not run; recorded: the small grenade's `grenade_ash`, followed and never playing).
- **The force** is a speed added each second, in its own axes (turned into the system's where they differ); `randomizePerFrame` draws it afresh each step. **The turn by speed** is an angular speed read from its curve where the speed is between its range's ends. **Rotation's direction** (`randomizeRotationDirection`): that share of particles turn the other way.
- **The sheet** (`UVModule`, its grid mode): the frame its curve gives at the share of life (its cycles repeating it), plus the start frame, of so many cells (a row's, where it goes by rows: one row, or one at random); frame 0 the top left, left to right then down. `PROVISIONAL`: with more than one cycle, the curve's share repeated (`fract(share × cycles)`) rather than the curve read at a repeated time.
- **Each step**, by the time passed: the particle ages; its velocity is limited (below); it moves by its velocity; it turns by its angular speed. It dies at the end of its lifetime. Gravity is the modifier times the world's gravity (none where the modifier is 0).
- **What is drawn**: its size the size it was born with times the size module's at its share of life; its colour the colour it was born with times the colour module's; its turn the one it was born with plus what it has turned since.
- **A module's own draw for a particle** (`RandN::RandN` and each module's update, decompiled 2026-11-03): where a module's value is drawn between two constants or two curves, its share is a number of Unity's own generator seeded with the particle's random seed plus the module's constant (each next word the last times 1812433253 plus one, as `Random.InitState`; the number the low 23 bits times the engine's unit, as `Random.value`): the first number for one value, the first, second and third for the three axes. The constants: size `8D2C8431`, rotation over life `6AED452E`, the turn's direction (the flip) `FF2BB1A4`, colour `591BC05C`, force `12460F3B`, the speed's limit `13371337` and its drag `A6790296`, rotation by speed `DEC4AEA1`, velocity over life `E0FBD834`.
- **The speed's limit** (Limit Velocity over Lifetime): a speed above the limit (`magnitude`, a value over life) is taken down by the `dampen` share, and `drag` (a value over life; times the particle's size, its speed, or both, where `multiplyDragByParticleSize`, `multiplyDragByParticleVelocity` say) slows it. The documentation gives `dampen` as a share of the speed above the limit taken off without saying over what time; this work took it off over each 1/30 of a second (the speed above the limit falls as (1 − dampen) to the power of 30 times the time passed), and the engine does the same (decompiled 2026-10-18, `ClampVelocityModule::Update`). **The engine's drag** (the same reading): the speed less the drag times the time passed, never below nothing, the drag first times the particle's cross-section, π times half its size squared, where `multiplyDragByParticleSize`, and times the speed squared where `multiplyDragByParticleVelocity`. This work's drag is the engine's since 0.68.3 (until then a slowing of drag times the speed a second; no system drawn has a drag). `PROVISIONAL`: the size the cross-section is of taken as the particle's size as drawn.
- **External forces** act through wind zones and force fields only: none are in the range, and the module does nothing.
- **The most particles**: a particle emitted when the system already has its most alive is not made.
- **A long frame** (`Time.maximumParticleDeltaTime`, the install's "Maximum Particle Timestep", 0.03 s, the time settings' fourth figure after the fixed step, the longest step and the time's scale; measured, `courses3`, 2026-11-04): a frame longer than it is made in so many equal steps, the fewest none longer than it (0.039 s as two of 0.0195), each a whole update (the emission, the modules, the move, the meeting with the world). Whole steps of 0.03 and the rest do not match the recording; equal steps do (particles' places a frame on within the recorder's six figures again, the meetings the game made on long frames ours too). A system paused unseen on the frame it is played takes none of its steps (`ParticleSystem::step_frame`).

## Meeting the world (`CollisionModule`, of the world's kind)

Read from the engine's code (`UnityPlayer.dll` decompiled with Unity's public symbols, 2026-10-19: `CollisionModule::Update`, `WorldCollision`, `PerformPlaneCollisions`, `PhysXRaycast::ParticleIntersect`). The explosion's concrete pieces meet the world at high quality (`type` 1, the world's; `quality` 0); its sparks, ash and smoke at medium (1), the hits' glass shards at high and their sparks at low (2): the next section (`probe collisions`, 2026-11-06; read as all high before).

- **When**: each step, once the particles have moved, each particle is tried against the scene's colliders on the module's layers (`collidesWith`) over the move it has just made. At high quality the physics engine is asked: a sphere swept through its scene (a single sweep, asking for the surface's normal and, where the sphere starts inside a shape, the least way out of it).
- **The sphere**: its radius `r` is half the particle's size times the module's `radiusScale` (the module halves the scale it keeps before it uses it). The sweep is of a sphere of `r` less 0.151 of `r`, from where the particle was to where it is and 0.151 of `r` further.
- **Where it met**: the particle's place where the swept sphere's middle met the surface, drawn back along the way by that 0.151 of `r`; where the sphere began inside a shape, pushed out of it along the least way out by its depth and that 0.151, and the normal turned about, to face into the shape (a reflection is the same either way; the crossing of medium and low quality is not).
- **The response**: what was left of the move past the surface, and the velocity, are each reflected about the surface's normal, kept by one less the dampen (`m_Dampen` at the particle's share of life), and their parts along the normal kept by the bounce (`m_Bounce`): with no bounce a particle that strikes slides on along the surface. Its age is put on by the lifetime's loss (`m_EnergyLossOnCollision` times its lifetime). It dies where that ends its life, or where its speed coming in was below `minKillSpeed` or above `maxKillSpeed` (the engine compares their squares).
- **What it sets off**: each particle that met a surface on the step and was alive before it did sets off the system's sub-emitters of the collision kind (below).
- A local system's particles are met in the world's axes and put back into the system's.

- **The world met is the level's colliders as the game has them then**, in the axes the recorder writes (each place less its origin, "Compared with"): a level's are read from the scenes its location loads (`trp_data::level_colliders`: boxes, spheres, capsules and meshes, on the module's layers, `collidesWith`), a mesh's front faces only (the sweep's, as PhysX gives them: a particle rising through a floor from below passes it).
- **The scenes a location loads**: its base in the server's database names its preset (`Scene`: the bundle under `maps/` and the preset's name); the game loads the preset's scenes in their order, then each child preset's in turn, all of them offline (a scene marked offline-only is left out online). Factory by day 14 (three presets), Reserve 49, Customs 29. Its AI's, sound's and culling's scenes are not read for the world (`PROVISIONAL` for the AI's).
- **What a raid switches off**: a loot container the server did not spawn in that raid (it is missing from the raid's loot list: the client sets its own list of objects to destroy off, `GameWorld.DestroyContainers`; a group's containers are drawn by the server, so of two same-named suitcases one may stand and the other not); a window whose glass breaks, its obstructive collider at once (`WindowBreaker.Break`). Which containers stood is not recorded; the comparison leaves out those the particles show absent.

`PROVISIONAL`: the module's planes, its force on what it meets, `maxCollisionShapes` and `collidesWithDynamic` at high quality not built (none matters to the explosion's on the range); the layers not tried (every collider of the range taken as on them). `interiorCollisions` is read by none of these paths at any quality: it changes nothing.

## Meeting the world at medium and low quality

Read from the engine's code (`UnityPlayer.dll` decompiled with Unity's public symbols, 2026-11-04, checked again 2026-11-06: `CollisionModule::Update`, `WorldCollision`, `ReadCache`, `ComputeHash`, `PerformPlaneCollisions`, `ParticleSystem::AssignRayBudgets`, `BeginUpdate`, `Update1b`, `StartModules`, `PhysXRaycast::FindParticleIntersections` and `ParticleIntersect`; the game's `QualityLevelPreset`), and set beside `courses3` ("Compared with"). **Which systems** (`probe collisions`, the effects' bundle): of 188 that meet the world, 66 at high quality, 119 at medium, 3 at low. The grenades' and blasts' sparks, smoke (`Effect Smoke Distorted`), ash and long smoke are medium; the hits' shared `Drop Sparks` and its black and white are low; the concrete pieces and the glass shards high. The game never changes a system's quality. Medium and low run the same code; they differ only in how often a system takes part in the frame's rays.

- **The frame's rays**: `QualitySettings.particleRaycastBudget`, which the game sets to 256 at each of its four quality levels (`QualityLevelPreset.Apply`). Once a frame, before any system steps (the engine's own count: a call of `AssignRayBudgets`, made at the start of the frame's update and again when a system that prewarms is played), they are shared. Every system's share is nought. The systems that take part are those in the engine's list of systems it updates that meet the world at medium or low quality and whose turn it is: a medium one every frame; a low one one frame in five (it counts four frames down from when its quality was first seen, takes part when the count is nought and counts again; the count runs only while it is in the list). With `n` of them, each gets the lesser of the rays over `n` (a whole number) and its particles (as they are before this frame's births); then, in the list's order, each gets the lesser of what it still lacks and what is left. A system in the list with no particles counts in `n`. A high system takes no part.
- **Its share in the frame**: raised to a multiple of four (the engine's rays go four at a time), for all its steps of the frame (a long frame's equal steps draw on the same).
- **Which particles get a ray**: the system keeps a cursor, a particle's place in its list, from call to call and frame to frame. A call over the particles from `start` to before `end` begins at the cursor if the cursor is before `end` (at `start` if before that), and otherwise at `start`; the particles from there up to the lesser of `end` and the beginning plus the rays left get a ray, in order. The cursor is then put at the beginning plus the rays left (whatever was used), and the rays left lose those used and are raised to a multiple of four again. Each step makes a call over the particles alive, then one for each four born on it in turn (from their birth, `StartModules`). So a frame whose rays are spent makes no ray on its later steps; a call with none left still moves the cursor; a call that uses fewer than four gets them back. The cursor is a place, not a particle: as particles die and the list closes up, it points at others.
- **A ray**: the high quality's sphere (`r` less its 0.151, above) swept from where the particle was at the step's start (its place less the step times its velocity) along its way for four cubes' width (four times `voxelSize`), whatever its move; among the still shapes alone (the module's `collidesWithDynamic` is set aside at these qualities, so nothing is pushed). A step under a millionth of a second casts none (the particle counts as rayed all the same). What a ray meets is kept (below), and met only by the rule of crossing.
- **The cache**: each system's own, made the first frame it meets the world at medium or low quality and kept for as long as the system lives: nothing clears it, so a copy of an effect played again finds the planes of its last play. A plane is kept by a key made from where its ray began and its way. The place gives its cube of the world: `voxelSize` across in x and z and four times that in y, counted from the world's origin down to the whole number below (one out of a 32-bit integer's range, the least). Those three whole numbers are hashed (CityHash64, the published algorithm's version 1.1, of their twelve bytes, x, y, z), and the way's class is put in by exclusive or. The class is the axis of the way's largest part (x before y before z where two are as large) and that part's sign: six classes; a way of nought is in x's negative class. A plane holds the surface's normal and a point on it, in the system's space. The point is where the sweep's middle met the surface, drawn back along the ray by 0.151 of the writer's own `r`, so the plane lies about the writer's radius off the surface for every particle that reads it. Where the sweep began inside a shape, the point is pushed out along the least way out and the normal turned to face into the shape (above). The newest plane of a key is kept; a ray that meets nothing takes nothing away; no plane ages or is let go.
- **A particle without a ray** in a call reads the plane of its own key: where it was at the step's start and its move's way. One rayed in that call reads nothing, even where its ray met nothing.
- **The meeting**: a plane found, a ray's own or one read, is met only where the particle's middle crossed it on this step going against its normal. With the move `m` (the step times the velocity) and `p` where it was, the move's part along the normal must be below nought (and not within 2^-23 of it), and `t`, the plane's distance from `p` along the normal over that part, from 0 to 1. The meeting point is `p + t m`; what a meeting does to the particle is the high quality's ("Meeting the world"), from that point. So a particle meets nothing in a cube and way that no ray has met; and one coming out of a shape it began a ray inside is turned back into it.
- **The step times of four at once**: each particle's ray is swept over its own time since the step's start (or since its birth). Reading the cache and crossing the plane take the time of the particle in the same lane of four, counted from the start of the stretch read (before the rayed ones, or after them) for the reading and from the particle's own place in the list for the crossing; on a call over four born whose first is not at a multiple of four, that is another particle's. The engine does so; the app does the same.
- **Not reproduced**: the engine's way of unit length from an approximate inverse square root (ours exact: the ray's end within a hair); its random draws for the dampen, bounce and lifetime's loss, by slots of four in its results' order (ours by the particle's seed, as at high); its results applied last to first (the order of the sub-emitters' births); a particle's added velocity (velocity over life) in its move.

`PROVISIONAL`: the order of the engine's list (who gets the rays left over: `ParticleSystem::AddToManager`, not read) is the app's (the muzzles' blasts', the shared, the effects' copies by their names); a system in the list is one alive; what a low system's count begins at (the quality it last saw, before the first frame: the system's constructor, not read); a particle that has not moved sweeps nothing (the engine sweeps nought long); in a lane past a call's end the particle's own time; a script's `Emit` with its own values (the hits' sparks) taken to make no call of the module (the engine's `Emit` of a count makes one with no time: it casts nothing and moves the cursor; the path of the other not read).

## Set off by another (`SubModule`)

A system names others as its sub-emitters, each with when (`type`: 0 a particle's birth, 1 its collision, 2 its death, among others), what it takes from the particle or the system (`properties`, as bits: 1 its colour, 2 its size, 4 its turn, 8 its lifetime, 16 the parent's duration) and a chance. A system so named never emits by itself (the engine marks it when the parent starts: `grenades-and-knife.md`, "The blast's effect"). Set off, it is moved to the parent's particle and emits its bursts' counts there, born on its own shape with its start values at the parent's share of its cycle where it takes the duration (at nought where not), each burst by its chance. `PROVISIONAL`: each burst's count at once, its rate and its own cycle not run for it; the kinds but the collision's not built (the explosion's one is of that kind).

## Its lights (`LightsModule`)

A system with lights lights a share of its particles (`ratio`) with copies of the light it names (an effect's own light, kept off: its colour, range and intensity), at most `maxLights` at once. Each copy stands at its particle; its colour the light's, times the particle's where `useParticleColor`; its range the light's times `rangeCurve` at the particle's share of life, times the particle's size where `sizeAffectsRange`; its intensity the light's times `intensityCurve`, times the particle's alpha where `alphaAffectsIntensity`. `PROVISIONAL`: with a ratio under one, every so-manyth particle in the order kept (`randomDistribution`, at random, not built); the explosion's two give their one particle each a light.

## Trails (`TrailModule`, its particles' mode)

Unity's documentation (the Trails module's page): each particle (of the `ratio` share) leaves a trail of the places it has been, a point dropped once it has moved `minVertexDistance` from the last, each kept for `lifetime` (a share of the particle's lifetime); drawn as a strip facing the camera, as wide as `widthOverTrail` along it (times the particle's size where `sizeAffectsWidth`), coloured `colorOverLifetime` at the particle's share of life times `colorOverTrail` along it (times the particle's colour where `inheritParticleColor`), its picture stretched over its length (`textureMode` 0), by the renderer's second material, dying with its particle where `dieWithParticles`. **Built** (for the RShG-2's fragments: `firing.md`, "A launcher's blasts"): the first point at the particle's birth; `PROVISIONAL`, the share along a trail by distance, its picture's 0 at the particle; the other modes (ribbons), `sizeAffectsLifetime`, a trail outliving its particle, `worldSpace` for a local system, not built.

## Drawn

A renderer that is off (`m_Enabled` 0 on the `ParticleSystemRenderer`) draws nothing, its system running all the same (the flashbang's shock wave: `grenades-and-knife.md`). A billboard is a square of the particle's size about its place, in the view's plane (alignment 0), turned in that plane by the particle's turn; its corners' picture coordinates run from 0 to 1 across and up (the sheet's cell where it has one); its colour the particle's. **A billboard's size is held on the screen** (read from the engine's code 2026-11-04: `ParticleSystemRenderer::PrepareForRender`, `GenerateParticleGeometry`, `Camera::CalculateFarPlaneWorldSpaceLength`, `RendererCullingCallbackProperties`): for each camera that draws it, the renderer's least and most (`m_MinParticleSize`, `m_MaxParticleSize`) are each a share of the view's width where the particle is. For a perspective camera that width is the camera's far plane's width (between the left and right edges of its rectangle, at its far distance) over the far distance, times the particle's depth along the view (so `2 tan(half the horizontal field of view)` times the depth, below nought behind the camera); for an orthographic one, the view's width. Both shares are first divided by the system's scale, the figures kept finite. The particle's size (the larger of its two) is raised to the least and held to the most, both of its sizes scaled alike: where the least comes out below nought (behind the camera) the particle is drawn at nought, and where the most does, it holds nothing. Every kind of billboard takes it (view-facing, stretched, horizontal, vertical), a mesh none. The install's systems give most of them a most far past the screen: the hits' dust (`Effect Puff Big`, `Effect Puff Big dark`) and the muzzle's fumes 10 views wide, the splinters (`Drop Wood`, `Drop Concrete`) 1, the explosion's `Effect Smoking Fragments small` 0.5, the muzzle's heat haze 0.5. A stretched billboard (mode 1) lies along the particle's way (`grenades-and-knife.md`, "The blast's effect"). **A mesh** (mode 4) is the renderer's mesh (`m_Mesh`; with more, `m_Mesh1` to `m_Mesh3` by `m_MeshDistribution`) at the particle's place, scaled by its size, turned by its turn in the axes `m_RenderAlignment` gives (2, the system's own), each corner coloured the particle's colour; the explosion's shock wave is Unity's built-in sphere (`Library/unity default resources`, object 10207: 515 corners, a metre across, read from the install's own copy by a mesh's type tree borrowed from a bundle, the file having none). The material's shader draws it (each shader is the game's own, read and disassembled: the fume's in `firing.md`, the explosion's in `grenades-and-knife.md`). Within a system, the particles go by its sort mode: by distance far to near (1), the oldest in front (2), the youngest in front (3). With no sorting (mode 0) the particles are drawn in the order the engine keeps them, and the engine puts the last particle in a dead one's place (decompiled 2026-10-18, `ParticleSystem::KillParticles`: groups of four from the top, a group looked at again after any death); this work takes them away so too since 0.68.3 (`trp_sim::particles::kill`; until then it kept them as born, oldest first), and a renderer that sorts by age orders them by the life left (the engine's key). A system's renderer among the scene's other transparent things: by queue, then far to near (`grenades-and-knife.md`); `firing.md` says where the muzzle's are drawn.

## Drawn off the frame (the options' `DUST RESOLUTION`)

**The app's own drawing, not the game's**: the game draws every effect's particles on the frame. At `FULL` the app does so too. At `HALF` or `QUARTER` the effects' systems whose shader blends over the frame by its alpha and writes its colour alone (`Particles/Smoke Distorted`, `Particles/Smoke Bumped`, `Global Fog/Alpha Blended Lighted`: the hits' and grenades' dust and smoke) are drawn off the frame at a half or a quarter of its size each way, after GPU Gems 3's "High-Speed, Off-Screen Particles". The simulation is the same at every setting; only the pixels the dust is drawn at change.

- **The order kept**: the effects' draws go in the engine's order (by queue, then far to near: "Drawn"); each run of such draws one after another goes into an off-frame picture of its own, laid over the frame before the next draw of another kind, and after the last.
- **The picture**: of half floats, cleared to no colour and an alpha of one; each draw blends its colour as on the frame and multiplies the alpha by one less its own, so the picture holds the colour the run adds and how much of the frame shows through it. Laid over, the frame is multiplied by that alpha and the colour added: the same sum as the draws blended one by one on the frame. Grouped at the frame's own size, its pixels differ from the draws blended one by one only by the half floats' rounding (1 to 3 steps of 255 under the densest dust).
- **The depth**: the scene's depth (the effects' copy) made as small, each small pixel the nearest of the full ones under it, so that dust behind anything in the block is not drawn there; the draws' depth test and soft fade read it.
- **Laid over by nearest-depth upsampling** (NVIDIA's OpacityMapping and Particle Upsampling samples): for each pixel of the frame, the four small pixels around it; where each lies within a tenth of the pixel's depth (`ESTIMATE`), the picture read between them; else the one nearest the pixel's depth alone.
